% ===== Abstract ===== #
\begin{abstract}
The large-scale primordial power spectrum is well constrained, but the observational constraints on small-scale ($<1 {\rm cMpc}$) spectrum are relatively limited.  We use dark-matter-only cosmological $N$-body simulations to quantify how enhanced small-scale primordial power spectrum affects non-linear structure formation. We compare a standard power-law primordial power spectrum with two blue-tilted spectra ($P(k)\propto k^{m_s}$ with $m_s>1$ above a pivot wavenumber $k_p$), examining their halo mass functions, mass assembly histories, mass-concentration relations, and nonlinear matter power spectra. 


We find that the blue-tilted model with $k_p=1\,h\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1};\; m_s=1.5$ significantly boosts halo abundance relative to the power-law model, by factors of 5-7 at $z=8.52$ for halo mass $M \sim 10^{10}\,M_\odot$, and by 50\% at $z=0$. Halo assembly also shifts to earlier times: for $M_{z=0}\simeq 10^9\,M_\odot$, the mean half-mass formation redshift $z_{1/2}$ increases from $\sim 2$ to $\sim 4$. Dark matter halos in the blue-tilted model are more concentrated: the median halo concentration in the blue-tilted model for $M \sim 10^9\,M_\odot$ is higher by factors of $\sim 3$ at $z=0$. While the blue-tilted model significantly boosts the non-linear matter power spectrum for $k> k_p$, the enhancement diminishes significantly at low redshift. {\color{red} quantify}


We benchmark these blue-tilted simulations against analytic models, providing an efficient means to constrain the small-scale power-spectrum with current and upcoming observations, including high-redshift galaxies observed by the James Webb Space Telescope, dwarf-galaxy structure, strong lensing, and 21 cm measurements.


\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction\label{intro}}
The standard cosmological model, combining a nearly power-law primordial power spectrum with the $\Lambda$ Cold Dark Matter ($\Lambda$CDM) paradigm, provides an accurate description of the Universe on large scales ($>1 {\rm Mpc})$. Measurements of the cosmic microwave background, galaxy clustering, and the Lyman-$\alpha$ forest are consistent with the standard cosmological model \citep{Planck20CMB,Blanton17SDSSsurvey,Chabanier19LyalphaPS}. On the other hand, the constraints on small scales are weaker. For example, there are potential small-scale tensions in cosmology model such as the missing-satellites, core-cusp, and too-big-to-fail problems \citep{Bullock17smallscalechallenges,Sales22challenges}, although they are subject to the uncertainties in baryonic physics \citep{Benson2002reionization,Chan2015corecusp}. At the same time, early JWST measurements have revealed unexpectedly abundant and potentially massive galaxy candidates at $z\gtrsim 8$--$10$ \citep{Labbe23MassiveGalaxies,Donnan23UVLF}. These observations have motivated renewed interest in enhanced small-scale primordial matter power \cite{Parashari23JWSTPPS,Hira24bluetilt},but high redshift baryonic physics is also potential solutions, e.g., evolving star-formation efficiency, weak feedback, dust, UV emission efficiency, and selection effects \citep{Lovell23JWSTExtreme}.

The discreteness of primordial black holes can also induce an isocurvature term that enhances especially the small-scale spectrum \cite{Inman2019PBHBT}.'' need rephase. }








We use a blue-tilted (BT) power spectrum following the previous literatures \citep[e.g.][]{Hirano15BlueTiltedPPS,Parashari23JWSTPPS,Hira24bluetilt,Tkac24BumpyPPS,deKruijf25DarkAges,Dekker25DwarfPPS,Wu25BT}. {\color{red} need to describe what blue tilted is.} Note that the BT models are phenomenological models instead of directly deriving from, e.g., any inflationary model. 










%In this paper, the BT parameters should be read as a phenomenological description of the linear matter spectrum used to generate the initial conditions, not as a complete inflationary or curvature-spectrum model. 




The large-scale power spectrum follows the standard power-law form $P(k)\propto k^{n_s}$ with $n_s\simeq0.965$, 


but the primordial matter power spectrum is enhanced above a pivot wavenumber $k_p$. The pivot therefore separates the well-measured large scales from the more weakly constrained small scales, preserving the successful large-scale predictions of $\Lambda$CDM while directly changing the abundance and collapse history of small halos.
Several recent studies have addressed different observational aspects of this question. Enhanced or modified small-scale spectra have been invoked to interpret high-redshift galaxy abundances in light of JWST \citep{Parashari23JWSTPPS,Hira24bluetilt}. The BT models have also been explored with zoom-in simulations of Milky Way--mass environments \citep{Wu25BT}, while dwarf-galaxy densities, satellite statistics, sub-galactic strong-lensing probes, and 21 cm observations provide complementary tests of small-scale matter power \citep{Kim18nomissingsatellites,Dekker25DwarfPPS,Gilman22SubgalacticPPS,Munoz20SmallScale21cm,deKruijf25DarkAges,Zhu26SKAPPS}. These works show that the same physical idea---changing the small-scale primordial matter power---can be tested through several distinct observables, but they also highlight a key difficulty: galaxy formation, reionization, baryonic physics, and observational selection effects can be degenerate with the primordial power spectrum.


The purpose of this paper is to isolate the gravitational, nonlinear response to enhanced small-scale power in the primordial matter spectrum before adding those observation-specific complications. We therefore perform dark-matter-only cosmological $N$-body simulations in periodic boxes, comparing a standard power-law input matter spectrum with two transfer-function-modified BT benchmark models. This approach is complementary to zoom-in studies of selected host environments \citep{Wu25BT}: here we measure population-level halo and field diagnostics across a periodic volume. Our diagnostics include the halo mass function, half-mass assembly redshift, concentration-based structural diagnostics, and the nonlinear matter power spectrum. These statistics probe different stages of the same physical process: enhanced small-scale fluctuations collapse earlier, populate the low-mass halo regime more efficiently, and are redistributed by nonlinear evolution into halo internal structure and high-$k$ clustering power.

\section{Theoretical Overview}
\label{sec:theory}


This study investigates the imprints of primordial matter power spectrum on the distributions and internal structures of dark matter halos. The theoretical overview defines the input matter spectra used to generate the simulations and summarizes how their small-scale differences propagate into nonlinear structure formation.


\subsection{From Matter Power Spectrum to Cosmic Structure}








%The simulation inputs are specified by the linear matter power spectrum at the initial redshift. In a complete early-universe model, this spectrum would be obtained by combining a primordial curvature spectrum with a transfer function and growth factor.Here, however, we do not introduce an independently normalized curvature-spectrum model. Instead, we describe the relevant small-scale modification directly through the shape of the initial-condition linear matter power spectrum used by the initial-condition generator. Up to a common normalization fixed by the shared background cosmology, the input linear spectrum can be written schematically as \(P_{\mathrm{lin}}(k,a)\propto D^2(a)T^2(k)k^{n_s}\), where \(D(a)\) is the growth factor and \(T(k)\) is the transfer function used by the initial-condition calculation. In the PL case, \(T(k)\) is the Eisenstein--Hu transfer function. In the BT cases, the code modifies the transfer function above a pivot scale \(k_p\), producing the target small-scale enhancement in the resulting input matter power spectrum. The subsequent gravitational collapse and nonlinear evolution of overdensities lead to the formation of a hierarchical network of dark matter halos, whose internal structure and abundance serve as fundamental probes of the underlying cosmology.
