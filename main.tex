\begin{abstract}
The large-scale primordial power spectrum is tightly constrained by cosmic microwave background and large-scale-structure measurements, but its small-scale shape remains much less certain. Departures from the standard nearly power-law form can arise in dark-matter or inflationary scenarios. We quantify how enhanced small-scale primordial power affects nonlinear structure formation with matched-phase, dark-matter-only cosmological \(N\)-body simulations. We compare a standard power-law (PL) model with two broken-power-law blue-tilted (BT) models in which the spectrum steepens above a pivot wavenumber \(k_p\). We measure Friends-of-Friends (FOF) halo mass functions, assembly histories, radial density profiles, concentration--mass relations, and the nonlinear matter power spectrum.

Among the BT models considered here, the \(k_p=1\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\), \(m_s=1.5\) case, hereafter \BTKPone{}, produces the largest changes in the measured statistics. Its FOF halo abundance exceeds that of PL by factors of \(4.8\)--\(6.6\) at \(z=8.52\) over \(M_{\rm FOF}\simeq1.2\times10^9\)--\(1.2\times10^{10}\,M_\odot\), and by \(1.4\)--\(1.5\) at \(z=0\) over the same mass range. Halo assembly also shifts earlier: near \(M_{z=0}\simeq1.25\times10^9\,M_\odot\), the half-mass redshift increases from \(z_{1/2}=2.28\) in PL to \(4.24\) in \BTKPone{}. At \(z=0\), the median concentration in the \(10^9\)--\(10^{9.5}\,M_\odot\) bin is higher by a factor of \(3.20\). The nonlinear matter power spectrum changes more modestly, with \(P_{\rm BT}/P_{\rm PL}=1.17\) at \(k=20\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\) and \(1.34\) at \(k=40\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\).

\revisionblue{We also compare the simulated mass functions, mass-assembly histories, and concentration--mass relations with analytic prescriptions evaluated using the PL and BT linear matter power spectra.} These dark-matter-only reference results are intended to guide future studies that add baryons, galaxy formation, reionization, and survey selection when comparing enhanced small-scale primordial power with high-redshift galaxies, dwarf-galaxy dynamics, strong lensing, and 21-cm probes.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction\label{intro}}
\revisionblue{On large scales, the standard \(\Lambda\)CDM model combines constant dark energy and cold dark matter with a nearly scale-invariant, close-to-power-law primordial power spectrum, \(P_{\rm prim}(k)\propto k^{n_s}\) with \(n_s\simeq0.965\). This framework is consistent with measurements of the cosmic microwave background, galaxy clustering, and the Lyman-\(\alpha\) forest \citep{Planck20CMB,Blanton17SDSSsurvey,Chabanier19LyalphaPS}. However, the small-scale ($\ll {\rm Mpc}$) primordial power spectrum is less directly constrained. Small-scale probes include satellite statistics and density profiles \citep{Wu25BT,Dekker25DwarfPPS,Esteban2024lumpyDM,Nadler2025enhanced}, strong-lensing flux-ratio and substructure measurements \citep{Gilman22SubgalacticPPS}, high-redshift galaxy counts, and future 21-cm observations \citep{Munoz20SmallScale21cm}.}

\revisionblue{Previous studies have simulated enhanced small-scale power in high-redshift cosmological volumes and in zoom-in models of Milky Way--mass environments \citep{Hirano15BlueTiltedPPS,Wu25BT,Nadler2025enhanced}. However, systematic comparisons of halo abundance, assembly histories, internal structure, and nonlinear matter power across matched cosmological volumes remain limited.}


%These probes do not by themselves select a blue-tilted model, but they motivate controlled tests of how small-scale primordial power affects nonlinear structure.

Small-scale enhancements of the primordial power spectrum arise in several classes of early-universe models, including warm inflation \citep{Arya20WarmInflation}, axion models with curved field-space effects \citep{Sai26BlueTilt}, and multi-field or hybrid inflation models \citep{Linde94Hybridinflation,Wang24BTinflationtheory,Wang25HybridInflation}. A population of primordial black holes (PBHs) can also add small-scale matter power through an isocurvature component seeded by Poisson fluctuations \citep{Inman2019PBHBT}. 

We do not model these mechanisms explicitly. Instead, we use broken-power-law blue-tilted spectra as examples for testing how enhanced small-scale primordial power changes nonlinear structure formation. \revisionblue{ ``Blue-tilted'' (BT) refers specifically to a broken-power-law enhancement whose small-scale slope is steeper than the usual PL slope, following the usage in previous small-scale-power studies \citep[e.g.][]{Hirano15BlueTiltedPPS,Parashari23JWSTPPS,Hira24bluetilt,Tkac24BumpyPPS,deKruijf25DarkAges,Dekker25DwarfPPS,Wu25BT}. }

%We use BT here as a phenomenological label for enhanced small-scale primordial power, not as a complete inflationary model.

Recent work has examined enhanced small-scale primordial power with several complementary probes. James-Webb-Space-Telescope-motivated studies test whether enhanced small-scale primordial power can increase the abundance of early galaxies \citep{Parashari23JWSTPPS,Hira24bluetilt,2026arXiv260522161K}. BT models have also been studied with zoom-in simulations of Milky Way--mass environments \citep{Wu25BT}. Dwarf-galaxy densities, satellite statistics, sub-galactic strong lensing, and 21-cm observations provide additional tests \citep{Kim18nomissingsatellites,Dekker25DwarfPPS,Gilman22SubgalacticPPS,Munoz20SmallScale21cm,deKruijf25DarkAges,Zhu26SKAPPS}. Interpreting these observables requires astrophysical and observational modeling, but the first step is to quantify how the dark-matter distribution changes when small-scale primordial power is enhanced.



Here we provide such simulations before adding baryonic physics, galaxy formation, or survey selection. We run dark-matter-only cosmological \(N\)-body simulations in periodic boxes, with matched initial phases for PL and BT runs. We compare a standard power-law primordial power-spectrum model with two BT models. We measure the halo mass function, half-mass assembly redshift, full mass-assembly histories, radial density profiles, the concentration--mass relation, and the nonlinear matter power spectrum. Together, these statistics provide dark-matter-only results that future hydrodynamic, semi-analytic, reionization, and observational analyses can build on.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. Sec.~\ref{sec:theory} defines the PL and BT primordial power-spectrum models and their corresponding input linear matter power spectra at \(z_i=200\). Sec.~\ref{sec:numerical} describes the initial conditions, simulations, halo catalogs, and analysis choices. Sec.~\ref{sec:results} presents the halo mass function, halo assembly, concentration, and nonlinear matter-power results. Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses the physical interpretation, numerical limitations, and links to observations. Sec.~\ref{sec:conclusion} summarizes the main quantitative findings.


\section{Theoretical Overview}
\label{sec:theory}

We study how enhancements in the primordial power spectrum affect the abundance, spatial distribution, and internal structure of dark matter halos. We first define the PL and BT primordial power-spectrum models and the corresponding input linear matter power spectra used to generate the initial conditions. We then summarize how small-scale differences in these spectra enter nonlinear halo formation. Throughout the paper, we use two terminology levels to distinguish primordial models from their matter-power-spectrum realizations.

\subsection{Power-Spectrum Models and Linear Matter Power}

In the power-law (PL) model, the primordial power spectrum scales as \(P_{\rm prim}(k)\propto k^{n_s}\), with \(n_s\simeq0.96\) \citep{Planck20CMB}.

We model small-scale enhancements with blue-tilted (BT) primordial power-spectrum models \citep[e.g.][]{Hira24bluetilt,Dekker25DwarfPPS}. In these models, the spectrum follows the PL form up to \(k_p\) and becomes steeper above \(k_p\):
\begin{equation}
P_{\rm prim}^{\rm BT}(k)
\propto
\begin{cases}
k^{n_s}, & k \le k_p,\\[4pt]
k^{n_s}\left(k/k_p\right)^{m_s-n_s}, & k > k_p,
\end{cases}
\label{eq:bt_input_power}
\end{equation}
where \(k_p\) is the pivot wavenumber and \(m_s\) controls the high-\(k\) slope of the BT primordial power-spectrum model. This form reduces to the PL shape when \(m_s=n_s\), while \(m_s>n_s\) gives a blue-tilted enhancement above \(k_p\). The initial redshift \(z_i=200\) enters later, when this model is converted into the input linear matter power spectrum used by the initial-condition generator.

In the initial-condition calculation, we implement the broken-power-law enhancement in Eq.~\eqref{eq:bt_input_power} by modifying the transfer function. The input linear matter spectrum at \(z_i=200\), \(P_{\rm lin}\propto T^2(k)k^{n_s}\), is therefore enhanced above \(k_p\).

The corresponding linear matter power spectrum can be written schematically as
\begin{equation}
P_{\rm lin}(k,z) \propto P_{\rm prim}(k)\,T^2(k)\,D^2(z),
\label{eq:P_MD}
\end{equation}
where \(T(k)\) is the linear transfer function and \(D(z)\) is the linear growth factor.



%Equivalently, the transfer-function ratio needed to realize Eq.~\eqref{eq:bt_input_power} is \(T_{\rm BT}(k)/T_{\rm PL}(k)=1\) for \(k\le k_p\) and \(T_{\rm BT}(k)/T_{\rm PL}(k)=(k/k_p)^{(m_s-n_s)/2}\) for \(k>k_p\). In this work the large-scale normalization is kept fixed; only the effective small-scale shape above $k_p$ is varied, so the model description can be specified by $k_p$ and $m_s$ for the present comparison. The BT spectra are therefore controlled transfer-function modifications of the initial-condition matter spectrum. The parameters $k_p$ and $m_s$ should not be interpreted as a complete inflationary parameterization or as independently constrained curvature-spectrum parameters in this paper.

We compare a standard power-law primordial spectrum, denoted PL, with two broken-power-law BT models that we label \BTKPone{} and \BTKPten{}. Both BT models use \(m_s=1.5\), while \BTKPone{} has \(k_p=1\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\) and \BTKPten{} has \(k_p=10\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\). These names are labels used in this paper; they should not be read as identical to naming choices in any particular previous study. \BTKPone{} affects a broader range of mildly nonlinear scales, whereas \BTKPten{} confines the enhancement to smaller scales. This pair of models allows us to test how nonlinear structure formation changes with the range of modes whose primordial power is enhanced.

\section{Numerical Methods}
\label{sec:numerical}

This section describes the numerical setup, including the generation of matched PL and BT initial conditions, halo identification, halo-property measurements, and the numerical ranges used for quantitative comparisons.

\subsection{Cosmological Parameters}
\label{subsec:cosmo_params}

All simulations use the following Planck 2018 background cosmology \citep{Planck20CMB}:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Density parameters: \(\Omega_{\rm m}=0.3153\), \(\Omega_{\rm b}=0.0493\), and \(\Omega_\Lambda=0.6847\).
    \item Hubble constant: \(H_0=67.36\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}\), corresponding to \(h\equiv H_0/(100\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}})=0.6736\).
    \item Scalar spectral index \(n_s = 0.9649\).
    \item The PL and BT initial conditions use the same target normalization, \(\sigma_8 = 0.8111\).
\end{enumerate}
The BT spectra are not assigned separately fitted \(\sigma_8\) values. They use the same background cosmology and target \(\sigma_8\) normalization as PL. MonofonIC then applies the required global normalization to each generated spectrum. The BT prescription changes the spectral shape for \(k>k_p\); below \(k_p\), the transfer-function shape remains the PL/Eisenstein shape apart from the small global renormalization needed to enforce the same target \(\sigma_8\). Table~\ref{tab:simulation_parameters} lists these cosmological parameters together with the numerical simulation parameters.


\subsection{Initial Conditions}
\revisionblue{We generate the initial conditions with \textsc{Music2-MonofonIC} \citep{Hahn2020MUSIC2monofonIC,Michaux2021MUSIC2monofonIC}, using the Eisenstein \& Hu transfer function \citep{Eisenstein1998} for PL. For the BT runs, we modify the MonofonIC transfer-function plugin only for \(k>k_p\). MonofonIC normalizes PL and BT to the same target value, \(\sigma_8=0.8111\). Thus the BT runs change the small-scale shape of the input spectrum, not the large-scale amplitude. For each matched set of runs, we keep the background cosmology, numerical settings, and MonofonIC random seed fixed. The main PL, \BTKPone{}, and \BTKPten{} \(25\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\), \(1024^3\) simulations therefore share the same initial phases. The matched phases ensure that PL--BT differences primarily trace the enhanced small-scale primordial power-spectrum model rather than realization-to-realization variance.}

As an initial-condition consistency test, we compare the BT pivot scales with the particle-grid scale. For the main \(25\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\), \(1024^3\) runs, \(k_{\rm Ny}\simeq128.7\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\). The two pivots, \(k_p=1\) and \(10\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\), are therefore well below the grid Nyquist scale. The largest BT input value over this range is also small, \(\Delta^2(k,z_i)\simeq5.8\times10^{-5}\) for \BTKPone{}, so the enhanced modes are still linear at \(z_i=200\). This comparison addresses the location and initial linearity of the imposed break, but it does not constitute a nonlinear convergence test.

\subsection{Simulation Code}
We evolve the initial conditions with the cosmological $N$-body code \small{SWIFT} \citep{Schaller2024SWIFT}\footnote{Publicly available at \url{https://swiftsim.com/}.}. In the dark-matter-only, collisionless mode used here, {\small SWIFT}'s gravity solver uses a fast-multipole method \citep{Dehn00FMM}, coupled to a Particle-Mesh solver at large scales \citep{Hock88PM}.

\subsection{Halo Finder and Halo Property Post-processor}

The halo statistics require two catalog layers: one for tracking object histories and one for measuring spherical-overdensity properties.

Halos and merger trees are identified with \textsc{HBT-HERONS}, a particle-tracking halo finder developed by \cite{Victor25HBTHERONS}\footnote{\url{https://github.com/SWIFTSIM/HBT-HERONS}}. We require a minimum of 20 particles for an object to be retained as a halo candidate, and define the halo center as the position of the most bound particle. For the main $25\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}$, $1024^3$ runs, the snapshot mass table gives \(m_{\rm DM}=1.89\times10^6\,M_\odot=1.27\times10^6\,h^{-1}M_\odot\). The nominal 20-particle halo threshold is therefore \(M\simeq3.8\times10^7\,M_\odot\), while a 100-particle structural cut corresponds to \(M\simeq1.9\times10^8\,M_\odot\). Based on the \textsc{HBT-HERONS} catalogs, we then use \textsc{SOAP} (Spherical Overdensity and Aperture Processor) \cite{McGibbon2025SOAP}\footnote{\url{https://github.com/SWIFTSIM/SOAP}} to compute spherical-overdensity properties such as $M_{200c}$, $R_{200c}$, and the structural quantities used below. \revisionblue{We define \(R_{200c}\) as the radius within which the mean enclosed density is \(200\rho_{\rm crit}(z)\). Here \(\rho_{\rm crit}(z)=3H^2(z)/(8\pi G)\), and \(M_{200c}=(4\pi/3)200\rho_{\rm crit}(z)R_{200c}^3\). We read these quantities from the \textsc{SOAP} \texttt{SO/200\_crit} fields.} This separation retains the tracking information used for the half-mass and full mass-assembly histories while providing consistent spherical-overdensity quantities for the internal-structure analyses.

Different statistics use different halo mass definitions. For the halo mass function, we use the Friends-of-Friends (FOF) algorithm \citep{Davis1985FOF} to define halo masses and compare them to analytic predictions evaluated with the same FOF mass definition. \revisionblue{For the half-mass and full mass-assembly histories, we follow each halo across snapshots using its persistent \textsc{HBT-HERONS} \texttt{TrackId} and record its Warren-corrected FOF mass. The resulting $z=0$ mass bins are therefore $M_{\rm FOF}$ bins.} For the internal-structure measurements, we use spherical-overdensity quantities measured by \textsc{SOAP}. \revisionblue{The radial profiles are measured directly from simulation particles in bins of \texttt{SO/200\_mean} mass and are plotted against $r/R_{200m}$.} The dashed NFW curves are reference profiles, not fits. The concentration--mass relation uses $M_{200c}$, $R_{200c}$, and $c_{200c}$ from \texttt{SO/200\_crit}. The nonlinear matter power spectrum is measured from the particle density field and does not require a halo mass definition. We therefore avoid directly mixing FOF, $M_{200m}$, and $M_{200c}$ masses when interpreting a single statistic, and the relevant captions specify the mass definition used.

\subsection{Halo Mass Function\label{subsub:HMF}}

We quantify halo abundance with the FOF halo mass function. Fig.~\ref{fig:mass_function} shows the FOF halo mass functions at representative snapshots from $z=8.52$ to $z=0$. The plotted simulation points are computed from the FOF catalogs in logarithmic mass bins, with Poisson counting uncertainties and the Warren et al. correction, $N_{\rm corr}=N(1-N^{-0.6})$, applied at the low-particle end \citep{Warren_2006}. Before binning, each FOF mass is corrected for the low-particle sampling bias using \(M_{\rm corr}=M_{\rm FOF}[1-(M_{\rm FOF}/m_{\rm DM})^{-0.6}]\), following the Warren correction as used in the high-redshift HMF analysis of \citet{Lukic2007HMF}. For each non-empty logarithmic bin, the plotted mass coordinate is the mean corrected halo mass in that bin, \(\bar M=\sum_i M_{{\rm corr},i}/N_{\rm h}\), rather than the logarithmic bin midpoint, following the bin-position prescription of \citet{Lukic2007HMF}. The FOF mass-function plot uses the conservative lower cut summarized in Table~\ref{tab:trusted_ranges}, \(M_{\rm FOF}\ge10^8\,M_\odot\), and the quantitative mass bins discussed below are above this limit. Appendix~\ref{app:fof_hmf_resolution} repeats the PL FOF mass-function measurement at lower particle resolution and in a larger PL box to show how this adopted mass range behaves under numerical resolution and volume changes.

The analytic curves are reference predictions rather than fits to the simulation measurements. For the FOF comparison, we use the Reed et al. (2007) fitting function \citep{Reed_2007} with the corresponding PL, \BTKPone{}, or \BTKPten{} linear matter spectrum. The simulation points are the measured nonlinear abundances, and the BT/PL ratios quantify changes relative to PL. Because mass-function fits depend on halo definition, we keep the FOF and spherical-overdensity comparisons separate: Fig.~\ref{fig:mass_function} uses FOF masses with Reed et al. (2007), while Fig.~\ref{fig:m200c_bocquet16_hmf} uses SOAP \(M_{200c}\) masses with Bocquet et al. (2016) \citep{Bocquet2016}. \revisionblue{For each PL or BT model, the analytic curve is computed from that model's linear matter power spectrum; none of the fitting-function parameters is refitted to the simulation data.}

The middle ratio panels show the BT/PL abundance ratio, \(f_{\rm BT}(M)/f_{\rm PL}(M)\), computed in the same FOF mass bins and plotted where both the BT and PL bins are populated. Here \(f(M)=dn/d\log_{10}M\) is the halo mass function in the plotted logarithmic-bin convention. The ratio error bars propagate the Poisson counting uncertainties from both simulation measurements. The bottom panels show \(f_{\rm sim}/f_{\rm Reed07}\), using the same bins, to show the level of agreement with the Reed et al. (2007) reference curves.

Within the resolved mass range, both blue-tilted models increase the low-mass FOF abundance relative to PL, with the largest enhancement in \BTKPone{}. The enhancement is largest at high redshift. For \BTKPone{}, the abundance ratio relative to PL is \(4.81\)--\(6.55\) over \(M_{\rm FOF}\simeq1.2\times10^9\)--\(1.2\times10^{10}\,M_\odot\) at \(z=8.52\). By \(z=0\), the ratio over the same mass range falls to \(1.42\)--\(1.47\). The BT/PL ratio decreases toward higher masses and lower redshifts. This interpretation is restricted to the bins above the plotted catalog cut, \(M_{\rm FOF}\ge10^8\,M_\odot\).

We use the Reed et al. (2007) FOF fitting function as published, without refitting its multiplicity function. \revisionblue{For each model, \(\sigma(M,z)\) is computed from its own PL, \BTKPone{}, or \BTKPten{} linear matter power spectrum.}

\subsection{Half-Mass Redshift\label{subsub:HMR}}
The half-mass assembly redshift, \(z_{1/2}\), is defined as the redshift at which a \(z=0\) halo first reaches half of its final FOF mass. \revisionblue{For each \(z=0\) FOF halo, we use its persistent \textsc{HBT-HERONS} \texttt{TrackId} to follow the same object over snapshots 0021--0056 and record its Warren-corrected FOF mass. We assign \(z_{1/2}\) only when two consecutive snapshots lie on opposite sides of \(M_0/2\). We use the recorded masses directly: missing snapshots are not filled, and the history is not forced to grow monotonically with a cumulative-maximum filter.} The same rule is used for PL, \BTKPone{}, and \BTKPten{}. \revisionblue{Temporary FOF bridge or split events can therefore remove a halo from the measurable sample or produce a temporary mass decrease.}

\revisionblue{The BK09 and COCO/Hellwing16 curves in Fig.~\ref{fig:half_mass_redshift} are external \(\Lambda\)CDM relations based on a standard power-law primordial spectrum. They provide a check that the PL result obtained with the procedure above lies within the range of previous measurements.} The main comparison in this paper is the matched BT--PL difference measured with the same method. The assembly shift is largest in \BTKPone{}. Around \(M_{z=0}\simeq1.25\times10^9\,M_\odot\), the mean \(z_{1/2}\) is \(2.28\) for PL, \(4.24\) for \BTKPone{}, and \(2.61\) for \BTKPten{}. Thus \BTKPone{} forms earlier than PL by \(\Delta z_{1/2}=1.96\), while \BTKPten{} forms earlier by \(0.33\), in this mass bin. Around \(M_{z=0}\simeq7.9\times10^9\,M_\odot\), the corresponding means are \(1.95\), \(3.12\), and \(2.05\), giving BT--PL shifts of \(1.17\) and \(0.10\). At \(M_{z=0}\simeq2.0\times10^{11}\,M_\odot\), the difference is smaller: \(1.43\) for PL, \(1.78\) for \BTKPone{}, and \(1.39\) for \BTKPten{}. Thus, the model with the largest high-redshift HMF enhancement also produces the largest assembly-time shift, especially below \(\sim10^{10}\,M_\odot\).

\subsection{Median Mass-Assembly Histories\label{subsub:MAH}}

\revisionblue{The half-mass redshift reduces each halo history to one number. Figure~\ref{fig:mass_assembly_history_correa} instead shows the median mass-assembly histories. We select halos at \(z=0\) in seven Warren-corrected FOF mass windows. The windows have \(|\log_{10}(M_{\rm FOF}/M_0)|\le0.1\) and are centered on \(M_0=3\times10^8\), \(10^9\), \(3\times10^9\), \(10^{10}\), \(3\times10^{10}\), \(10^{11}\), and \(3\times10^{11}\,M_\odot\). We then follow each halo through its persistent \texttt{TrackId}. At each snapshot, we take the median \(M_{\rm FOF}(z)\) among halos with a finite positive recorded mass. Missing detections are not filled, and no cumulative-maximum filter is applied. The contributing sample can therefore decrease with redshift. We restrict quantitative interpretation to \(M_0\ge10^9\,M_\odot\) and \(z\le6\). More than 90 per cent of each selected sample is detected throughout this range.}

\revisionblue{We compare these histories with the extended Press--Schechter model of \citet{Correa2015MAHI},}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:correa2015_mah}
\revisionblue{M(z)=M_0(1+z)^{a f(M_0)}\exp[-f(M_0)z],}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:correa2015_fm0}
\revisionblue{f(M_0)=\left[S(M_0/q)-S(M_0)\right]^{-1/2},}
\end{equation}
\revisionblue{where \(S(M)=\sigma^2(M)\), \(a\) depends on the linear growth factor, and \(q\) sets the formation scale. We compute \(S(M)\) and \(a\) using the project cosmology and the corresponding PL or \BTKPone{} linear matter power spectrum. The faint curves use the published mass-dependent relations for \(q\) and \(\widetilde z_{\rm f}\). For the prominent dashed and dotted curves, we instead solve the EPS formation equation together with the half-mass closure \(M(\widetilde z_{\rm f})=M_0/2\). This step recomputes \(q\) and \(\widetilde z_{\rm f}\) for each spectrum. It does not use the measured histories, so the curves are not fits to the plotted points.}

\revisionblue{At fixed final FOF mass, the \BTKPone{} median lies above the PL median at earlier times. The difference grows with redshift. This directly shows that \BTKPone{} halos assembled a larger fraction of their final mass earlier. The result agrees with the half-mass-redshift shift in Fig.~\ref{fig:half_mass_redshift}. The modified Correa curves reproduce the broad change in the history shape, but the agreement varies with mass and redshift. We therefore use them as a diagnostic connection to the linear power spectrum, not as a calibrated BT fitting formula. Figure~\ref{fig:mass_assembly_history_correa} does not include \BTKPten{}; the three-model assembly comparison remains the half-mass result above.}

\subsection{Halo Internal Structure}

We next test whether earlier halo growth also changes internal structure. We use direct radial density profiles and the concentration--mass relation.

\subsubsection{Radial Halo Density Profiles\label{subsub:DensityProfile}}
\revisionblue{The radial-profile analysis tests whether earlier assembly changes halo internal structure by measuring density profiles directly from simulation particles. Figure~\ref{fig:halo_density_analysis} compares the median profiles in \textsc{SOAP} \(M_{200m}\) bins with Diemer et al. NFW profiles computed for the corresponding PL and BT models \citep{Diemer2019}. The NFW curves are not fitted to the simulated profiles. The error bars show bootstrap 16th--84th percentile intervals for the median profile in each bin. We define the convergence radius as the radius at which the relaxation parameter of \citet{Power2003} reaches \(\kappa=0.6\). At smaller radii, two-body relaxation associated with finite particle number may affect the measured density profile.}

The structural changes are concentrated toward the halo centers. In the resolved inner regions, \BTKPone{} has the largest central-density enhancement relative to PL, \BTKPten{} shows a weaker enhancement, and the three models approach one another toward larger \(r/R_{200m}\). The lower ratio panels show that the \BTKPone{} enhancement is strongest in the low- and intermediate-mass bins, while \BTKPten{} is closer to PL. The direct \(V_{\max}\)--\(R_{\max}\) summary at \(z=0\) shows the same ordering. In the \(10^9\)--\(10^{9.5}\,M_\odot\) bin, \BTKPone{} has a median \(V_{\max}\) that is \(1.27\) times larger than PL and a median \(R_{\max}\) that is \(0.59\) times the PL value. For \BTKPten{}, the corresponding ratios are \(1.06\) and \(0.82\).

At larger radii (\(r\gtrsim0.3R_{200m}\)), the differences are weaker and more mass dependent. In the highest-mass bins shown, the measured profiles agree within the plotted scatter. We therefore interpret the radial profiles as a direct dark-matter structural signal associated with earlier collapse, not as evidence for a different subhalo population. A subhalo interpretation would require host-controlled subhalo mass functions, radial number-density profiles, and explicit subhalo resolution thresholds, which are outside the scope of this work.

\subsubsection{Concentration-Mass Relation\label{subsub:concentration}}
The concentration--mass relation summarizes the structural changes through \(c_{200c}\), a scalar measure of halo compactness. We use the \textsc{SOAP} \texttt{SO/200\_crit} catalogs associated with the \textsc{HBT-HERONS} halo catalogs, taking \(M_{200c}\), \(c_{200c}\), and the number of dark-matter particles for each halo. Catalog masses are converted to solar masses using the stored unit factor \(10^{10}\,M_{\odot}\). We require finite positive masses and concentrations, impose \(c_{200c}<500\), and retain only halos with at least 100 dark-matter particles, corresponding to \(M_{200c}\simeq1.9\times10^8\,M_\odot\) for the main-run particle mass.

The simulation points in Fig.~\ref{fig:concentration_comparison_i21_fit} show the median \(\log_{10}c_{200c}\) in 22 logarithmic mass bins over \(8.0 \le \log_{10}(M_{200c}/M_{\odot}) \le 13.5\), with error bars given by the 16th--84th percentile scatter. For comparison, the same figure shows the Ishiyama et al. (2021) NFW-fit concentration prescription evaluated with \textsc{colossus} \citep{Diemer2018,Ishiyama2021} using the \(200c\) mass definition and the corresponding PL or BT linear matter power spectra generated from the primordial power-spectrum models. Appendix~\ref{app:concentration_alternative_models} shows the corresponding Diemer \& Joyce (2019) and Ludlow et al. (2016) comparisons.

Here \(c_{200c}=R_{200c}/r_s\), where \(R_{200c}\) is the spherical-overdensity radius with respect to the critical density and \(r_s\) is the scale radius of the NFW profile \citep{Navarro1996NFW,Navarro1997NFW}. Fig.~\ref{fig:concentration_comparison_i21_fit} compares the concentration--mass relation at four representative redshifts for PL, \BTKPone{}, and \BTKPten{}.

The measured medians show mass- and redshift-dependent concentration changes. At \(z=0\), the median concentration in the \(10^9\)--\(10^{9.5}\,M_\odot\) bin is higher than PL by factors of \(3.20\) for \BTKPone{} and \(1.35\) for \BTKPten{}. The 16th--84th percentile ranges in Fig.~\ref{fig:concentration_comparison_i21_fit} show substantial halo-to-halo scatter, especially toward the low-particle end, so we use the median trends and quoted bin ratios rather than fitting individual-halo concentrations. The larger \BTKPone{} effect is consistent with the HMF and assembly-history results, reflecting the broader range of resolved modes enhanced by its primordial power-spectrum model after transfer-function conversion. More generally, halo concentrations are known to encode formation history and to respond to changes in the linear power spectrum in non-standard small-scale-power simulations \citep{Ludlow2016WDMConcentration,Brown2020HaloPPS}. We therefore interpret the concentration enhancement as the structural counterpart of earlier assembly, not as an independent fitted input-power parameter.
The semi-analytic curves should be interpreted as published concentration--mass relations rather than independent fits to our binned simulation points. In these comparisons,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:colossus_concentration}
c_{200c}^{\rm th}(M,z)=\mathcal{C}_{\rm model}\!\left[M_{200c},z;P_{\rm lin}(k),\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\rm cosmo}\right],
\end{equation}
where \(\mathcal{C}_{\rm model}\) denotes the concentration prescription implemented in \textsc{colossus}, \(P_{\rm lin}(k)\) is the PL or BT linear matter power spectrum, and \(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\rm cosmo}\) denotes the shared background cosmological parameters. The simulation and analytic calculations use the \(M_{200c}\) definition given in Sec.~\ref{sec:numerical} and \(c_{200c}=R_{200c}/r_s\).
The underlying assumption is that halo concentration is primarily tied to the collapse history encoded in the linear density field. The blue-tilted models therefore change the predicted concentration through the linear matter power spectrum generated from the primordial power-spectrum model, not through a refit of the analytic curves to the simulation measurements.
The lower rows in Fig.~\ref{fig:concentration_comparison_i21_fit} show the median simulation-to-theory ratio, \(c_{\rm sim}/c_{\rm I21,fit}\), and the halo-to-halo concentration scatter in each mass bin, \(\sigma_{68}(\log_{10}c)=0.5(P_{84}-P_{16})\). The scatter row describes the distribution of simulated halo concentrations and is not a model-uncertainty band.

\subsection{Power Spectrum\label{subsub:PS}}

We finally measure the nonlinear matter power spectrum from the particle density field. This statistic tests whether the halo-level changes are also present in the full dark-matter density field. For each snapshot, dark-matter particle masses are assigned to a regular \(1024^3\) Cartesian mesh, and the isotropically averaged spectrum is computed with the \textsc{swiftsimio} power-spectrum utilities. We use no folding correction or cross-deposition correction, and we normalize the shot-noise term by the number of dark-matter particles. The resulting wavenumbers and powers are converted from \({\rm Mpc}^{-1}\) and \({\rm Mpc}^3\) to \(h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\) and \((h^{-1}{\rm Mpc})^3\), respectively. For plotting, the measured spectra and ratios are subsequently log-binned by taking the geometric-mean wavenumber and median power in each plotting bin.

The nonlinear theory curves shown for orientation are computed separately from the simulations with the HMcode2020 dark-matter-only nonlinear prescription as implemented in \texttt{pyhmcode}. We first build a PL linear matter spectrum from the Eisenstein--Hu transfer function and shared cosmological parameters. For each BT case, the same PL linear spectrum is multiplied by the broken-power-law enhancement above the corresponding \(k_p\), and the resulting PL or BT linear spectrum is supplied to HMcode2020. Thus the lower-panel ``BT theory/PL theory'' curves are ratios of these HMcode2020 predictions, not fits to the simulated BT/PL ratios and not an independent calibration for the blue-tilted cosmology.

Fig.~\ref{fig:power_spectra_ratio_z0} shows matched BT/PL ratios for the \(k_p=1\) and \(k_p=10\), \(m_s=1.5\) variants in the \(25\) and \(256\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\) boxes. We restrict the quantitative ratio analysis to matched PL and BT measurements with the same box size. No additional folding correction or external window-function deconvolution is applied outside the \textsc{swiftsimio} routine, so the highest-\(k\) points are interpreted as evidence for the direction of the small-scale trend rather than as a calibrated nonlinear-power fitting formula. The added theory-ratio panels divide each measured PL or BT spectrum by the corresponding HMcode2020 prediction. \revisionblue{The amplitudes in these panels depend on the power-spectrum estimator and the treatment of the highest-\(k\) modes; we therefore use them for comparison with HMcode2020 rather than as a new fitting formula.} The box-size and high-\(k\) reliability limits are stated directly in the figure caption.

At \(z=0\), the fiducial \(25\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\) \(\BTKPone/\mathrm{PL}\) ratio is close to unity on large scales and rises gradually toward high \(k\): \(1.06\), \(1.10\), \(1.17\), and \(1.34\) at \(k=5\), \(10\), \(20\), and \(40\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\), respectively. In the larger \(256\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\) comparison, the enhancement is weaker over its conservative mesh range, reaching \(1.06\) at \(k=10\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\) and \(1.08\) near its mesh Nyquist scale, \(k_{\rm Ny}\simeq12.6\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\). We therefore interpret the nonlinear power-spectrum result as a high-\(k\) enhancement consistent in sign with the halo-level trends, while its amplitude remains sensitive to box size, estimator choices, and the adopted high-\(k\) reliability range.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Physical Interpretation of the Main Trends}

Enhancing the small-scale primordial power spectrum raises the amplitude of the corresponding small-scale density fluctuations in the initial conditions. In the BT runs, these stronger initial fluctuations tend to collapse earlier than in PL. \revisionblue{Following \citet{Wu25BT}, we associate each pivot with a comoving Lagrangian radius \(r_l=\pi/k_p\) and a characteristic mass \(M_p=(4\pi/3)r_l^3\rho_{{\rm crit},0}\Omega_{{\rm m},0}\). For our cosmology, \(k_p=1\) and \(10\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\) correspond to \(M_p=1.68\times10^{13}\) and \(1.68\times10^{10}\,M_\odot\), respectively. These characteristic masses are not sharp boundaries. At high redshift, the halo-abundance enhancement is strongest below the corresponding \(M_p\) but is not confined to that range. At later times, the half-mass redshifts, full mass-assembly histories, and central densities retain the earlier-collapse signal.}
\revisionblue{\BTKPone{} produces the larger response because its lower pivot enhances a broader range of resolved modes. The higher pivot of \BTKPten{} confines the additional power to smaller scales, so its effect weakens toward halo masses above its characteristic \(M_p\). Nonlinear growth and mergers reduce the halo-abundance contrast toward lower redshift, while assembly histories and internal structure retain the effect of earlier collapse.}

\revisionblue{The nonlinear matter power spectrum averages over the full dark-matter density field, whereas halo abundance and internal structure select collapsed objects. Its fractional response therefore need not have the same amplitude as the changes in halo counts or concentrations.}

\subsection{Relation to Previous Work}

\revisionblue{We do not model observations directly, but instead study how enhanced small-scale primordial power changes dark matter halo profiles and statistics.} JWST-oriented studies have examined whether enhanced small-scale primordial power can increase the abundance of early galaxy hosts \citep{Parashari23JWSTPPS,Hira24bluetilt}. We isolate the corresponding dark-matter effect directly: the BT models form more low-mass halos at high redshift and shift their assembly earlier. Translating that halo-side change into luminosity functions or number counts requires galaxy-formation and selection modeling, so we do not use the simulations as direct galaxy constraints.

\revisionblue{Zoom-in simulations can follow satellites and subhalos within individual Milky Way--mass hosts \citep{Wu25BT} and are better suited to host-dependent applications involving dwarf galaxies and strong-lensing substructure. Our periodic boxes instead measure population-level field-halo statistics and the nonlinear matter power spectrum. The isolated-halo profiles reported here provide dark-matter-only population trends that can inform dwarf-galaxy and strong-lensing models \citep{Dekker25DwarfPPS,Gilman22SubgalacticPPS}, but they are not direct predictions for satellite or lensing-subhalo populations. We therefore do not infer subhalo counts or radial distributions from the projected density fields.}


\revisionblue{Enhanced small-scale primordial power increases the abundance of early low-mass dark matter halos, providing more potential formation sites for the first galaxies and Population III stars. The resulting changes in radiation backgrounds, gas heating, and reionization can produce identifiable signatures in the 21-cm signal \citep{Munoz20SmallScale21cm,deKruijf25DarkAges,Zhu26SKAPPS}. Because the simulations do not model baryons or radiation, predicting those signatures still requires source-population, heating, and reionization models.}

\subsection{Numerical and Modeling Limitations}

The main limitations are the dark-matter-only setup, the single realization, and the numerical range of each statistic. First, the simulations are collisionless dark-matter-only runs. This design cleanly isolates the gravitational effect of the modified primordial power-spectrum model, but it omits gas cooling, star formation, stellar and AGN feedback, and reionization. These processes can change halo density profiles, subhalo survival, and the connection between halos and galaxies. The results should therefore be interpreted as predictions for the underlying dark matter distribution, not as direct predictions for observed galaxies or lensing samples without further modeling.

Second, the main PL, \BTKPone{}, and \BTKPten{} comparisons use one matched-seed realization per model. Using the same initial phases reduces realization noise in the model-to-model ratios and helps isolate the effect of the modified primordial power spectrum after MonofonIC conversion. However, the plotted HMF error bars include Poisson counting uncertainties only, while the concentration error bars show halo-to-halo scatter; neither includes cosmic variance or realization-to-realization scatter. This affects the covariance and precision of the quoted ratios, especially for rare high-mass halos, large-scale modes in the matter power spectrum, and statistics measured from smaller auxiliary boxes. For observational interpretation, however, the dominant additional ingredients are baryonic physics, reionization, galaxy selection, and survey selection effects.

Third, the usable range of each statistic is set by different numerical effects. For the low-mass halo mass function, the main limits are particle discreteness and halo-finder thresholds. For the assembly histories, temporary FOF bridge or split events can interrupt a \texttt{TrackId} history. The fraction of the \(z=0\) sample detected at a given snapshot also decreases toward high redshift, especially in the lowest mass window. For the structural measurements, the limits are particle number, force softening, centering, and catalog concentration measurements. For the nonlinear matter power spectrum, the limits are box size, mesh assignment, force resolution, and high-$k$ estimator choices. In addition, the matter power-spectrum ratio is quantified only for matched PL and BT measurements with the same box size.

\revisionblue{Finally, the halo mass-function and concentration prescriptions were calibrated over finite ranges of cosmology, mass, and redshift. We evaluate each prescription with the linear matter power spectrum of the corresponding PL or BT model without refitting its parameters. The Correa MAH comparison is different: we recompute its EPS quantities for each spectrum and replace the published mass-dependent closure with a half-mass closure. It is a diagnostic modification rather than a calibrated fit. Differences from the simulations may therefore reflect both nonlinear evolution and extrapolation beyond the original calibration range.}

\subsection{Connection to Observations}

\revisionblue{The connection to observations is indirect, but the analytic prescriptions tested here provide a practical link to observational modeling. Halo mass-function prescriptions evaluated with the PL and BT linear matter power spectra can be used as inputs to forward models of high-redshift galaxy populations. Likewise, the Ishiyama et al. (2021) concentration--mass relation evaluated for the BT models can inform models of low-mass halo structure relevant to dwarf-galaxy dynamics and strong lensing \citep{Ishiyama2021,Dekker25DwarfPPS,Gilman22SubgalacticPPS}. The simulated changes in early halo abundance and assembly can also be propagated through source-population and reionization models when predicting 21-cm signals \citep{Munoz20SmallScale21cm,deKruijf25DarkAges,Zhu26SKAPPS}. These applications require additional baryonic and observational modeling. The halo mass-function and concentration prescriptions are used as published, while the modified Correa MAH curves provide only a diagnostic comparison. None of these results is a direct observational constraint.}


Within this scope, the simulations provide reference dark-matter-only results before baryonic physics and survey selection are added. The largest changes occur in high-redshift low-mass halo abundance, early assembly, low-mass halo concentrations and inner densities, and high-\(k\) matter clustering. These quantities can guide hydrodynamic simulations, semi-analytic galaxy modeling, reionization calculations, and multi-probe likelihood analyses.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We used dark-matter-only cosmological \(N\)-body simulations to quantify how enhanced small-scale primordial power affects nonlinear structure formation. By comparing matched-phase simulations of a standard power-law model with two broken-power-law blue-tilted models, we measured how dark matter halos change before introducing baryonic physics, galaxy formation, or observational selection.

The main conclusions are as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The halo abundance increases most at low mass and high redshift. In the FOF halo mass function (Sec.~\ref{subsub:HMF}; Fig.~\ref{fig:mass_function}), \BTKPone{} exceeds PL by factors of \(4.81\) and \(6.55\) at \(z=8.52\) in the two mass bins, \(M_{\rm FOF}\simeq1.21\times10^9\) and \(1.21\times10^{10}\,M_\odot\). At \(z=0\), the corresponding ratios are \(1.42\) and \(1.47\). In the same two mass bins, \BTKPten{} gives ratios of \(1.39\) and \(1.05\) at \(z=8.52\), and \(1.31\) and \(1.08\) at \(z=0\). The \(M_{200c}\) comparison with spherical-overdensity masses gives the same qualitative conclusion (Fig.~\ref{fig:m200c_bocquet16_hmf}).
    \item Enhanced small-scale primordial power shifts halo assembly earlier. \revisionblue{In the half-mass measurement, each halo is followed across snapshots through its persistent \textsc{HBT-HERONS} \texttt{TrackId}} (Sec.~\ref{subsub:HMR}; Fig.~\ref{fig:half_mass_redshift}). The mean \(z_{1/2}\) near \(M_{z=0}\simeq1.25\times10^9\,M_\odot\) changes from \(2.28\) in PL to \(4.24\) in \BTKPone{} and \(2.61\) in \BTKPten{}. \revisionblue{The full histories give the same result: at fixed final FOF mass, the \BTKPone{} median progenitor is more massive than the PL median at earlier times (Sec.~\ref{subsub:MAH}; Fig.~\ref{fig:mass_assembly_history_correa}). The modified Correa comparison connects this change to the model-dependent linear power spectrum, but it is not a calibrated BT fit.} The earlier assembly is also consistent with the enhanced high-redshift halo abundance.
    \item The structural measurements show the same model ordering. The direct radial density profiles (Sec.~\ref{subsub:DensityProfile}; Fig.~\ref{fig:halo_density_analysis}) and the concentration--mass relation (Sec.~\ref{subsub:concentration}; Fig.~\ref{fig:concentration_comparison_i21_fit}) both show stronger low-mass central density and concentration in the blue-tilted runs. At \(z=0\), the median \(c_{200c}\) in the \(10^9\)--\(10^{9.5}\,M_\odot\) bin is higher than PL by factors of \(3.20\) for \BTKPone{} and \(1.35\) for \BTKPten{}.
    \item The nonlinear matter power spectrum changes coherently but more modestly. In the matched \(25\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\) fiducial comparison (Sec.~\ref{subsub:PS}; Fig.~\ref{fig:power_spectra_ratio_z0}), \BTKPone{} reaches \(P_{\rm BT}/P_{\rm PL}=1.17\) at \(k=20\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\) and \(1.34\) at \(k=40\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\). \BTKPten{} is consistent with PL in the same comparison, with ratios of \(1.00\) at both wavenumbers. The direction of the change is consistent with the halo-level trends, but its amplitude is sensitive to box size and high-\(k\) estimator choices; we therefore do not provide a calibrated nonlinear-power fitting formula.
\end{enumerate}

\Tk{This paragraph is important, but it sounds odd. Especially, how good the fitting compared to simulations? }
\revisionblue{We evaluate the Reed et al. (2007) FOF halo mass-function prescription \citep{Reed_2007}, the Bocquet et al. (2016) \(M_{200c}\) prescription \citep{Bocquet2016}, and the Diemer \& Joyce (2019), Ishiyama et al. (2021), and Ludlow et al. (2016) concentration--mass relations \citep{Diemer2019,Ishiyama2021,Ludlow2016WDMConcentration} separately with the PL and BT linear matter power spectra. Their parameters are not refitted to the simulations. We also compare the PL and \BTKPone{} assembly histories with the Correa et al. (2015) EPS form after recomputing its spectrum-dependent quantities under a half-mass closure \citep{Correa2015MAHI}. These comparisons test whether the analytic models reproduce the direction and scale dependence of the simulated changes. The projected density fields illustrate spatial differences, while the nonlinear-power measurements remain sensitive to the estimator at high \(k\) and are not supplied as a fitting formula.}

These simulations therefore provide dark-matter-only reference results for models with enhanced small-scale primordial power. The signal is largest at low halo mass, high redshift, and high wavenumber, and weakens toward massive halos and late times. A single matched-seed realization limits covariance-level precision, especially for rare halos and large-scale modes. Connecting these results to observations requires baryonic physics, reionization, galaxy selection, and survey selection effects. Within these limitations, the measurements provide comparison points for hydrodynamic simulations, semi-analytic and reionization models, and multi-probe tests of small-scale primordial power.

\section{FOF Mass-Function Resolution and Volume Check}
\label{app:fof_hmf_resolution}

This appendix tests whether the FOF halo-mass-function mass range used in the main result is stable to resolution and volume changes. Figure~\ref{fig:fof_hmf_resolution} shows the numerical comparison used to assess that range. We recompute the PL FOF mass function at \(z=0\) and \(z=8.52\) for four PL runs. The fiducial run is the \(25\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\), \(1024^3\) simulation. The same-volume lower-resolution tests use \(25\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\) boxes with \(512^3\) and \(256^3\) particles. The volume test uses a \(50\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\), \(512^3\) run. The \(25\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\), \(256^3\) and \(50\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\), \(512^3\) runs have the same particle mass, so their comparison separates a mass-resolution test from a box-volume test at fixed particle mass. The same logarithmic bins, Poisson uncertainties, and Warren et al. FOF low-particle correction used in the main mass-function figure are applied here. Vertical dotted lines mark the 50-particle mass of each plotted resolution, and the lower panels show ratios to the fiducial PL-25-\(1024^3\) measurement. The reduced data are provided in \texttt{public\_data/figure\_data/fof\_hmf\_resolution/}, and the plotting script is included as \texttt{public\_data/scripts/plot\_fof\_hmf\_resolution.py}.

At \(z=0\), the same-volume tests agree well above each comparison run's 50-particle mass. Over the common bins with at least 20 objects in both the comparison and fiducial catalogs, PL-25-\(512^3\) has a median ratio of \(0.986\) to PL-25-\(1024^3\) over \(10^{8.92}\)--\(10^{12.25}\,M_\odot\). PL-25-\(256^3\) gives \(0.987\) over \(10^{9.92}\)--\(10^{12.25}\,M_\odot\). The corresponding 16th--84th percentile ranges are \(0.963\)--\(1.029\) and \(0.952\)--\(1.070\). The \(50\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\), \(512^3\) volume test gives a median ratio of \(0.945\) over the same \(10^{9.92}\)--\(10^{12.25}\,M_\odot\) mass interval, with a wider range \(0.917\)--\(1.051\). This is consistent with finite-volume and phase differences at the level relevant for the PL reference curve.

At \(z=8.52\), the same-volume \(512^3\) run remains close to the fiducial measurement above its 50-particle mass. Its median ratio is \(0.935\) over \(10^{8.92}\)--\(10^{10.58}\,M_\odot\), with a 16th--84th percentile range \(0.900\)--\(0.979\). The \(256^3\) same-volume run and the \(50\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\), \(512^3\) volume test cover only five common high-mass bins above their \(6.0\times10^9\,M_\odot\) 50-particle scale. Their median ratios are \(0.813\) and \(0.871\), respectively. \revisionblue{We use these runs only to assess how mass resolution and box size affect the PL mass function, not as independent cosmological measurements.} The fiducial \(1024^3\) HMF has substantial resolution margin above \(10^8\,M_\odot\). Lower-resolution comparison boxes show the expected loss of high-redshift low-mass abundance when their own 50-particle limits move into the plotted mass range.
