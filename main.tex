\begin{abstract}
\currentrevisionblue{The primordial power spectrum is well constrained on large scales but remains uncertain on small scales. We use dark-matter-only cosmological \(N\)-body simulations initialized with the same random phases to determine how enhanced small-scale power changes nonlinear structure formation. We compare a standard power-law (PL) model with two broken-power-law blue-tilted (BT) models whose spectra steepen above \(k_p=1\) and \(10\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\). We measure halo mass functions, assembly histories, radial density profiles, concentration--mass relations, and nonlinear matter power spectra. Enhanced small-scale power increases the abundance of low-mass halos at high redshift, shifts halo assembly earlier, and produces denser and more concentrated low-mass halos. These effects are stronger in the \(k_p=1\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\) model than in the \(k_p=10\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\) model and weaken toward higher halo masses and lower redshifts. The nonlinear matter power spectrum changes more modestly. Comparisons with published mass-function, concentration, and mass-assembly prescriptions validate their broad trends but show that their quantitative accuracy depends on redshift and halo mass. These results isolate the gravitational effects of enhanced small-scale primordial power before baryonic physics and observational selection are included.}

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction\label{intro}}
\currentrevisionblue{The standard \(\Lambda\)CDM model assumes cold dark matter, a cosmological constant, and a nearly scale-invariant primordial power spectrum, \(P_{\rm prim}(k)\propto k^{n_s}\) with \(n_s\simeq0.965\). This model agrees with measurements of the cosmic microwave background (CMB) \citep{Planck20CMB}, galaxy clustering \citep{Blanton17SDSSsurvey}, and the Lyman-\(\alpha\) forest \citep{Chabanier19LyalphaPS,Abolfathi18DR14}. However, the primordial power spectrum is less tightly constrained on scales much smaller than a megaparsec. Probes of these scales include CMB spectral distortions \citep{Chluba2012CMBSmallscalepowerspectrum}, satellite statistics and density profiles \citep{Wu25BT,Dekker25DwarfPPS,Esteban2024lumpyDM,Nadler2025enhanced}, strong-lensing flux-ratio and substructure measurements \citep{Gilman22SubgalacticPPS}, high-redshift galaxy counts \citep{Parashari23JWSTPPS,Hira24bluetilt}, ultra-compact minihalos \citep{Bringmann2026ultracompactminihaloonprimordialpowerspectrum}, and future 21-cm observations \citep{Munoz20SmallScale21cm}.}

\currentrevisionblue{Previous studies have examined enhanced small-scale power using high-redshift cosmological simulations \citep{Hirano15BlueTiltedPPS,Inman23EnhancedSmallScaledarkage} and zoom-in simulations of Milky Way--mass environments \citep{Wu25BT,Nadler2025enhanced}. A systematic comparison of how different primordial spectra affect nonlinear evolution within the same cosmological volume remains limited.}


%These probes do not by themselves select a blue-tilted model, but they motivate controlled tests of how small-scale primordial power affects nonlinear structure.

Small-scale enhancements of the primordial power spectrum arise in several classes of early-universe models, including warm inflation \citep{Arya20WarmInflation}, axion models with curved field-space effects \citep{Sai26BlueTilt}, and multi-field or hybrid inflation models \citep{Linde94Hybridinflation,Wang24BTinflationtheory,Wang25HybridInflation}. A population of primordial black holes (PBHs) can also add small-scale matter power through an isocurvature component seeded by Poisson fluctuations \citep{Inman2019PBHBT}. 

We do not model these mechanisms explicitly. Instead, we use broken-power-law blue-tilted spectra as examples for testing how enhanced small-scale primordial power changes nonlinear structure formation. \currentrevisionblue{Here, ``blue-tilted'' (BT) refers specifically to a broken-power-law enhancement whose small-scale slope is steeper than the PL slope, following previous small-scale-power studies \citep[e.g.][]{Hirano15BlueTiltedPPS,Parashari23JWSTPPS,Hira24bluetilt,Tkac24BumpyPPS,deKruijf25DarkAges,Dekker25DwarfPPS,Wu25BT}.}

\section{Theoretical Overview}
\label{sec:theory}

\currentrevisionblue{We define the PL and BT primordial power-spectrum templates and the input linear matter spectra used to generate the initial conditions. We also relate the BT pivot wavenumbers to characteristic halo masses. Throughout the paper, ``primordial power spectrum'' refers to a PL or BT template such as Eq.~\eqref{eq:bt_input_power}. ``Input linear matter power spectrum'' refers to the spectrum at \(z_i=200\) after applying the transfer function and the common normalization.}

\subsection{Power-Spectrum Models and Linear Matter Power}
\label{subsec:power_models}

In the power-law (PL) model, the primordial power spectrum scales as \(P_{\rm prim}(k)\propto k^{n_s}\), with \(n_s\simeq0.96\) \citep{Planck20CMB}.

We model small-scale enhancements with blue-tilted (BT) primordial power-spectrum models \citep[e.g.][]{Hira24bluetilt,Dekker25DwarfPPS}. In these models, the spectrum follows the PL form up to \(k_p\) and becomes steeper above \(k_p\):
\begin{equation}
P_{\rm prim}^{\rm BT}(k)
\propto
\begin{cases}
k^{n_s}, & k \le k_p,\\[4pt]
k^{n_s}\left(k/k_p\right)^{m_s-n_s}, & k > k_p,
\end{cases}
\label{eq:bt_input_power}
\end{equation}
\currentrevisionblue{where \(k_p\) is the pivot wavenumber and \(m_s\) controls the high-\(k\) slope of the BT primordial power-spectrum model. This form reduces to the PL shape when \(m_s=n_s\), while \(m_s>n_s\) gives a blue-tilted enhancement above \(k_p\). The transfer function and common normalization are applied when constructing the input linear matter spectrum at \(z_i=200\).}

The corresponding linear matter power spectrum can be written schematically as
\begin{equation}
P_{\rm lin}(k,z) \propto P_{\rm prim}(k)\,T^2(k)\,D^2(z),
\label{eq:P_MD}
\end{equation}
where \(T(k)\) is the linear transfer function and \(D(z)\) is the linear growth factor.



%Equivalently, the transfer-function ratio needed to realize Eq.~\eqref{eq:bt_input_power} is \(T_{\rm BT}(k)/T_{\rm PL}(k)=1\) for \(k\le k_p\) and \(T_{\rm BT}(k)/T_{\rm PL}(k)=(k/k_p)^{(m_s-n_s)/2}\) for \(k>k_p\). In this work the large-scale normalization is kept fixed; only the effective small-scale shape above $k_p$ is varied, so the model description can be specified by $k_p$ and $m_s$ for the present comparison. The BT spectra are therefore controlled transfer-function modifications of the initial-condition matter spectrum. The parameters $k_p$ and $m_s$ should not be interpreted as a complete inflationary parameterization or as independently constrained curvature-spectrum parameters in this paper.

\currentrevisionblue{We compare a standard power-law primordial spectrum, denoted PL, with two broken-power-law models, \BTKPone{} and \BTKPten{}. Both BT models use \(m_s=1.5\). \BTKPone{} has \(k_p=1\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\), while \BTKPten{} has \(k_p=10\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\). The lower pivot in \BTKPone{} enhances a broader range of resolved wavenumbers than the higher pivot in \BTKPten{}.}

\revisionblue{To estimate the halo mass scale associated with each pivot, we follow \citet{Wu25BT} and define a comoving Lagrangian radius \(r_l=\pi/k_p\). The corresponding characteristic mass is}
\begin{equation}
\revisionblue{M_p=\frac{4\pi}{3}r_l^3\rho_{{\rm crit},0}\Omega_{{\rm m},0}.}
\label{eq:pivot_mass}
\end{equation}
\revisionblue{For our cosmology, \(k_p=1\) and \(10\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\) give \(M_p=1.68\times10^{13}\) and \(1.68\times10^{10}\,M_\odot\), respectively. These values indicate the approximate mass scales affected by each pivot, but they are not sharp boundaries.}

\subsection{Cosmological Parameters}
\label{subsec:cosmo_params}

All simulations use the following Planck 2018 background cosmology \citep{Planck20CMB}:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Density parameters: \(\Omega_{\rm m}=0.3153\), \(\Omega_{\rm b}=0.0493\), and \(\Omega_\Lambda=0.6847\).
    \item Hubble constant: \(H_0=67.36\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}\), corresponding to \(h\equiv H_0/(100\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}})=0.6736\).
    \item Scalar spectral index \(n_s = 0.9649\).
    \item The PL and BT initial conditions use the same target normalization, \(\sigma_8 = 0.8111\).
\end{enumerate}
\currentrevisionblue{Table~\ref{tab:simulation_parameters} lists the cosmological and numerical parameters.}


\subsection{Initial Conditions}
\currentrevisionblue{We generate the initial conditions with \textsc{Music2-MonofonIC} \citep{Hahn2020MUSIC2monofonIC,Michaux2021MUSIC2monofonIC}. PL uses the Eisenstein \& Hu transfer function \citep{Eisenstein1998}. For each BT model, we modify the transfer-function plugin above \(k_p\). MonofonIC then applies a small model-dependent global normalization to reach the shared target \(\sigma_8=0.8111\). Below \(k_p\), the BT models retain the PL/Eisenstein transfer-function shape, with only this small change in overall amplitude.}

\currentrevisionblue{For each set of corresponding PL and BT runs, we keep the background cosmology, numerical settings, and MonofonIC random seed fixed. The main PL, \BTKPone{}, and \BTKPten{} \(25\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\), \(1024^3\) simulations therefore share the same initial random phases. This choice reduces the uncertainty associated with the particular random realization in the PL--BT comparisons.}

As an initial-condition consistency test, we compare the BT pivot scales with the particle-grid Nyquist wavenumber, \(k_{\rm Ny}=\pi N_{\rm side}/L_{\rm box}\), where \(N_{\rm side}\) is the number of particles along one dimension and \(L_{\rm box}\) is the comoving box length listed in Table~\ref{tab:simulation_parameters}. For the main \(25\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\), \(1024^3\) runs, \(k_{\rm Ny}\simeq128.7\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\). The two pivots, \(k_p=1\) and \(10\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\), are therefore well below the grid Nyquist scale. The largest BT input value over this range is also small, \(\Delta^2(k,z_i)\simeq5.8\times10^{-5}\) for \BTKPone{}, so the enhanced modes are still linear at \(z_i=200\). This comparison addresses the location and initial linearity of the imposed break, but it does not constitute a nonlinear convergence test.

\revisionblue{We use Friends-of-Friends (FOF) and spherical-overdensity (SO) masses for different measurements. \textsc{SWIFT} identifies FOF groups with a linking length equal to $0.2$ times the mean interparticle separation \citep{Davis1985FOF}. We denote the total dark matter mass of each group by $M_{\rm FOF}$. \textsc{HBT-HERONS} identifies the bound objects associated with these groups and retains candidates with at least 20 particles \citep{Victor25HBTHERONS}.}

\revisionblue{We use \textsc{SOAP} to measure SO properties around the position of the most-bound \textsc{HBT-HERONS} particle \citep{McGibbon2025SOAP}. For a reference density $\rho_{\rm ref}(z)$, $R_{200}$ encloses a mean density $200\rho_{\rm ref}(z)$, and}
\begin{equation}
\revisionblue{M_{200}=\frac{4\pi}{3}\,200\rho_{\rm ref}(z)R_{200}^3.}
\end{equation}
\currentrevisionblue{We use \(\rho_{\rm ref}=\rho_{\rm crit}\) for \(M_{200c}\) and \(R_{200c}\), and the cosmic mean matter density for \(M_{200m}\) and \(R_{200m}\). These quantities are read from the \texttt{SO/200\_crit} and \texttt{SO/200\_mean} catalog fields.}

\currentrevisionblue{We describe halo structure with the NFW density profile \citep{Navarro1996NFW,Navarro1997NFW},}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:nfw_profile}
\currentrevisionblue{\rho_{\rm NFW}(r)=
\frac{\rho_s}{(r/r_s)(1+r/r_s)^2},}
\end{equation}
\currentrevisionblue{where \(r_s\) is the scale radius and \(\rho_s\) is the characteristic density. The \(200c\) concentration is \(c_{200c}=R_{200c}/r_s\).}

Particle radii smaller than the force-softening length are set to that length in this calculation.}

\subsection{Halo Mass Function\label{subsub:HMF}}

\currentrevisionblue{Using the FOF groups defined in Sec.~\ref{subsec:halo_catalogs}, we measure the FOF halo mass function at representative snapshots from \(z=8.52\) to \(z=0\) (Fig.~\ref{fig:mass_function}). Before binning, we correct each FOF mass for low-particle sampling bias using
\(M_{\rm corr}=M_{\rm FOF}[1-(M_{\rm FOF}/m_{\rm DM})^{-0.6}]\) \citep{Warren_2006,Lukic2007HMF}. For each non-empty logarithmic bin, the plotted mass coordinate is the mean corrected halo mass,
\(\bar M=\sum_i M_{{\rm corr},i}/N_{\rm h}\), rather than the bin midpoint \citep{Lukic2007HMF}. The HMF error bars show Poisson counting uncertainties. We compute the BT/PL ratios only in bins populated in both simulations and propagate the Poisson uncertainties from both measurements.}

\currentrevisionblue{We adopt the conservative lower limit \(M_{\rm FOF}\ge10^8\,M_\odot\) listed in Table~\ref{tab:trusted_ranges}. Appendix~\ref{app:fof_hmf_resolution} tests this limit with lower-resolution PL runs and a larger PL box.}

\subsection{Half-Mass Redshift\label{subsub:HMR}}
The half-mass assembly redshift, \(z_{1/2}\), is the redshift at which a \(z=0\) halo first reaches half of its final FOF mass. \currentrevisionblue{We use the main progenitor branch defined in Sec.~\ref{subsec:halo_catalogs}. For each \(z=0\) FOF halo, we follow its persistent \textsc{HBT-HERONS} \texttt{TrackId} over snapshots 0021--0056 and record its Warren-corrected FOF mass. We assign \(z_{1/2}\) when two consecutive snapshots lie on opposite sides of \(M_0/2\). The same procedure is applied to PL, \BTKPone{}, and \BTKPten{}.}

\currentrevisionblue{We do not fill missing snapshots or force the recorded mass histories to increase monotonically. Temporary FOF bridge or split events can therefore remove a halo from the measurable sample or produce a temporary mass decrease.}

\currentrevisionblue{Figure~\ref{fig:half_mass_redshift} compares our measurements with two external \(\Lambda\)CDM relations. BK09 measured the mean half-mass formation redshift in the Millennium-II simulation using virial masses \citep{BoylanKolchin2009MSII}. COCO/Hellwing16 used the same fitting form with FOF masses \citep{Hellwing16cocoproject}. We evaluate both published relations at the mean final mass in each of our FOF mass bins. The external simulations differ from all of our runs, including PL, in their background cosmologies and halo-mass definitions. We therefore use the published relations only as qualitative checks. Because they were calibrated for standard power-law spectra, we compare them with the PL assembly histories and do not treat them as predictions for the BT models.}

\currentrevisionblue{For the 15 resolved PL bins spanning \(1.25\times10^9\le M_{\rm FOF}/M_\odot\le8.22\times10^{11}\), the mean absolute offset \(\langle|z_{1/2}^{\rm PL}-z_{1/2}^{\rm ref}|\rangle\) is \(0.053\) from BK09 and \(0.097\) from COCO/Hellwing16. The maximum absolute offsets are \(0.128\) and \(0.207\), respectively. Thus the PL measurements follow both external relations to within \(\Delta z_{1/2}\simeq0.1\) on average.}

\currentrevisionblue{At fixed final mass, halos in both blue-tilted models form earlier than PL halos, with the largest shift in \BTKPone{}. Around \(M_{z=0}\simeq1.25\times10^9\,M_\odot\), the mean \(z_{1/2}\) is \(2.28\) for PL, \(4.24\) for \BTKPone{}, and \(2.61\) for \BTKPten{}. The corresponding shifts relative to PL are \(\Delta z_{1/2}=1.96\) for \BTKPone{} and \(0.33\) for \BTKPten{}.}

\currentrevisionblue{Around \(M_{z=0}\simeq7.9\times10^9\,M_\odot\), the mean \(z_{1/2}\) values are \(1.95\), \(3.12\), and \(2.05\) for PL, \BTKPone{}, and \BTKPten{}, giving BT--PL shifts of \(1.17\) and \(0.10\). At \(M_{z=0}\simeq2.0\times10^{11}\,M_\odot\), the values are \(1.43\), \(1.78\), and \(1.39\), respectively. The model with the largest high-redshift HMF enhancement therefore also has the largest assembly-time shift, especially below \(\sim10^{10}\,M_\odot\).}

\subsection{Power Spectrum\label{subsub:PS}}

\currentrevisionblue{We measure the nonlinear matter power spectrum from the full dark matter density field. For each snapshot, we assign each particle mass to the nearest cell of a \(1024^3\) Cartesian mesh and compute the isotropically averaged spectrum with the \textsc{swiftsimio} power-spectrum utilities. The routine subtracts the Poisson term \(P_{\rm shot}=L_{\rm box}^3/N_{\rm DM}\), using \(N_{\rm DM}\) as \texttt{shot\_noise\_norm}. We do not deconvolve the nearest-grid-point window or apply an aliasing correction. For plotting, we group the measurements in logarithmic bins and use the geometric-mean wavenumber and median power in each bin.}

%\Tk{Need to cite HMcode2020 and reference \texttt{pyhmcode} url}

\currentrevisionblue{The nonlinear theory curves are computed with the HMcode2020 dark-matter-only prescription \citep{Mead2021HMcode2020}, using the \texttt{pyhmcode} implementation.\footnote{\url{https://github.com/tilmantroester/pyhmcode}}} We first construct the PL linear matter spectrum from the Eisenstein--Hu transfer function and the adopted cosmological parameters. For each BT model, we apply the broken-power-law enhancement above its \(k_p\) and supply the resulting linear spectrum to HMcode2020. The theory ratios are HMcode2020 predictions; no parameters are fitted to the simulated BT/PL ratios.

Fig.~\ref{fig:power_spectra_ratio_z0} shows BT/PL ratios for the \(k_p=1\) and \(k_p=10\), \(m_s=1.5\) models in the \(25\) and \(256\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\) boxes. The sim/HMcode2020 panels show \(P_{\rm sim}/P_{\rm HMcode2020}\) for each model and box. \currentrevisionblue{The lowest nonzero mode in a periodic box is \(k_{\min}=k_{\rm f}=2\pi/L_{\rm box}\). It is \(0.251\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\) for the \(25\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\) box and \(0.0245\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\) for the \(256\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\) box. Modes with \(k<k_{\min}\) are not sampled. We show measurements below \(4k_{\rm f}\) as faded open symbols. At high \(k\), we omit simulation measurements above \(0.25k_{\rm Ny}\), where \(k_{\rm Ny}=\pi N_{\rm mesh}/L_{\rm box}\) and \(N_{\rm mesh}=1024\). The adopted display intervals are therefore \(1.0\le k/(h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1})\le32.2\) for the 25 box and \(0.098\le k/(h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1})\le3.14\) for the 256 box.}

\currentrevisionblue{We test the mesh dependence by remeasuring the \(z=0\) PL spectra on a \(512^3\) mesh using the same simulation particles. Near \(0.25k_{\rm Ny}\) of the \(1024^3\) mesh, \(P_{512}/P_{1024}=0.871\) for the 25 box and \(0.864\) for the 256 box. If the leading grid error scales as the square of the cell size, these differences imply \(1024^3\)-mesh errors of \(4.3\%\) and \(4.5\%\), respectively. This test supports the adopted cutoff at the \(5\%\) level. It tests the power-spectrum estimator, not the particle-mass or force resolution of the simulations.}

\currentrevisionblue{We estimate the Gaussian finite-mode uncertainty using \citeauthor{Takahashi2008FiniteModes}'s mode-count approximation \citep{Takahashi2008FiniteModes},
\begin{equation}
N_{\rm mode}(k)=\frac{L_{\rm box}^{3}k^2\Delta k}{2\pi^2},
\qquad
\frac{\sigma_P}{P}=\left(\frac{2}{N_{\rm mode}N_{\rm real}}\right)^{1/2}.
\label{eq:finite_mode_power_error}
\end{equation}
We use the width of each plotted bin and \(N_{\rm real}=1\). Equation~\eqref{eq:finite_mode_power_error} is a continuum approximation because the reduced data do not retain the discrete Fourier wavevectors. It is least accurate close to \(k_{\min}\). We use this estimate to assess low-\(k\) sampling, but do not apply it as a correction. We do not assign it to BT/PL because the PL and BT runs use the same initial random phases and their errors are correlated.}

\currentrevisionblue{At \(z=0\) and \(1.07\), the absolute spectra from the \(25\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\) box lie below both HMcode2020 and the \(256\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\) measurements over their common displayed interval. At \(z=0\), for example, \(P_{25}/P_{256}=0.27\) near \(k=1\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\) and \(0.38\) near \(k=2\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\). These differences exceed the Gaussian mode-count estimates of \(13\%\) and \(5\%\), respectively. They therefore cannot be explained by mode counting alone. Missing long-wavelength modes, nonlinear finite-volume coupling, and differences between realizations may all contribute. These effects partly cancel in BT/PL because the PL and BT runs within each box use the same initial random phases. We therefore interpret the absolute normalization of the small-box spectrum cautiously and place more weight on BT/PL.}

\currentrevisionblue{At \(z=0\), both boxes give \(\BTKPone/\mathrm{PL}\simeq1\) over their common displayed interval, \(1\lesssim k/(h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1})\lesssim3\). In the \(25\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\) box, the ratio then rises to \(1.06\), \(1.10\), and \(1.17\) at \(k=5\), \(10\), and \(20\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\), respectively. The displayed \(256\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\) measurements end at \(k=3.14\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\) and therefore do not test this higher-\(k\) rise.}

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Physical Interpretation of the Main Trends}

\currentrevisionblue{Enhancing the primordial power spectrum above \(k_p\) increases the amplitude of the initial density fluctuations on those scales. Regions with larger overdensities collapse earlier. The characteristic mass \(M_p\) in Eq.~\eqref{eq:pivot_mass} estimates the halo scale associated with each pivot but does not define a sharp boundary. At high redshift, the HMF enhancement is strongest below \(M_p\). At later times, the HMF difference decreases, while the assembly histories and central densities remain different from PL.}

\currentrevisionblue{\BTKPone{} produces the larger change because its lower pivot enhances a broader range of resolved modes. The higher pivot of \BTKPten{} restricts the added power to smaller scales, so the differences from PL decrease more rapidly with halo mass. Nonlinear growth and mergers further reduce the abundance contrast toward lower redshift.}

\currentrevisionblue{The matter power spectrum measures fluctuations in the full matter distribution, whereas the HMF and concentration describe the abundance and internal structure of bound halos. Therefore, a modest change in the matter power spectrum can coexist with larger changes in halo abundance and concentration.}

\currentrevisionblue{Some studies used analytic or semi-analytic models rather than new \(N\)-body simulations. \citet{Parashari23JWSTPPS} used the Sheth--Tormen halo mass function to estimate high-redshift stellar mass densities. \citet{Esteban2024lumpyDM} modeled how enhanced small-scale power changes dwarf-halo concentrations and satellite kinematics. \citet{Dekker25DwarfPPS} combined a concentration model with existing \(\Lambda\)CDM merger trees and a galaxy-formation model. \subsection{Numerical and Modeling Limitations}

\revisionblue{The main limitations are the dark-matter-only setup, a single realization for each model using the same initial random phases, finite mass resolution, finite force resolution, and finite box size.}
%\Tk{resolution and box size?}

\currentrevisionblue{First, the simulations are collisionless dark-matter-only runs. This setup isolates the gravitational effect of the modified primordial power-spectrum model, but it omits gas cooling, star formation, stellar and AGN feedback, and reionization. These processes can change halo density profiles, subhalo survival, and the connection between halos and galaxies.}

%\Tk{I don't think they will make these  interpretations. }The results should therefore be interpreted as predictions for the underlying dark matter distribution, not as direct predictions for observed galaxies or lensing samples without further modeling.

\currentrevisionblue{Second, we have one initial realization for each model. Using the same initial random phases reduces the uncertainty associated with the chosen realization in the PL--BT ratios, but it does not quantify the variation among independent realizations. The HMF error bars include only Poisson counting uncertainties, while the concentration error bars show halo-to-halo scatter. Neither includes cosmic variance. This omission matters most for rare high-mass halos, large-scale power-spectrum modes, and statistics measured in the smaller auxiliary boxes.}

\currentrevisionblue{The mode-count estimate in Eq.~\eqref{eq:finite_mode_power_error} describes only the finite number of sampled Fourier modes. At low redshift, nonlinear coupling between modes can add further realization-dependent deviations. In second-order perturbation theory, this contribution relative to the linear density field grows approximately with the linear growth factor \(D(z)\), so it is expected to be larger at \(z=0\) than at high redshift \citep{Takahashi2008FiniteModes}. Missing long-wavelength modes can also shift the mean power spectrum in a finite box. We do not subtract either effect from the measurements.}

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusion}



We used dark-matter-only cosmological \(N\)-body simulations to quantify how enhanced small-scale primordial power affects nonlinear structure formation. \currentrevisionblue{We compared a standard power-law model with two broken-power-law blue-tilted models defined by Eq.~\eqref{eq:bt_input_power}. Both BT models have \(m_s=1.5\): \BTKPone{} has \(k_p=1\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\), and \BTKPten{} has \(k_p=10\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\).} %\Tk{Need to re-emphasize which two BT models you consider here, because some readers just read conclusions.}

%no need to repeat so many times: we measured how dark matter halos change before introducing baryonic physics, galaxy formation, or observational selection.



The main conclusions are as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \currentrevisionblue{Enhanced small-scale primordial power increases halo abundance most strongly at low mass and high redshift (Sec.~\ref{subsub:HMF}). At \(M_{\rm FOF}\simeq1.21\times10^9\,M_\odot\), the \BTKPone{}/PL ratio decreases from \(4.81\) at \(z=8.52\) to \(1.42\) at \(z=0\), while \BTKPten{} produces a smaller change. The \(M_{200c}\) mass function shows the same trend (Fig.~\ref{fig:m200c_bocquet16_hmf}).}

    \revisionblue{The median differences from the Reed et al. (2007) \citep{Reed_2007} and Bocquet et al. (2016) \citep{Bocquet2016} models are below \(8\%\) at \(z=0\) and \(3.44\), but reach \(32\%\) at \(z=8.52\).}

    \item \currentrevisionblue{Enhanced small-scale primordial power shifts halo assembly earlier (Sec.~\ref{subsub:HMR}). For halos with \(M_{z=0}\simeq1.25\times10^9\,M_\odot\), \(z_{1/2}\) increases from \(2.28\) in PL to \(4.24\) in \BTKPone{} and \(2.61\) in \BTKPten{}. The full mass-assembly histories show the same ordering (Sec.~\ref{subsub:MAH}).}

    \currentrevisionblue{Across \(10^9\le M_0/M_\odot\le10^{11}\), the Correa et al. (2015) curves \citep{Correa2015MAHI} differ from the median histories by at most \(25\%\) for PL and \(7\%\) for \BTKPone{} over the redshifts used for the comparison.}
    
    \item \currentrevisionblue{The blue-tilted runs have denser inner profiles and higher concentrations at low halo mass (Secs.~\ref{subsub:DensityProfile} and \ref{subsub:concentration}). At \(z=0\), the median \(c_{200c}\) in the \(10^9\)--\(10^{9.5}\,M_\odot\) bin is higher than PL by factors of \(3.20\) for \BTKPone{} and \(1.35\) for \BTKPten{}.}


    \currentrevisionblue{In Sec.~\ref{subsub:concentration}, for the resolved mass bins, the Diemer \& Joyce (2019) relation agrees with the simulation medians within \(20\%\) for PL, \BTKPone{}, and \BTKPten{} at all four redshifts \citep{Diemer2019}.}

    
    \item \currentrevisionblue{\BTKPone{} increases the nonlinear matter power at high \(k\), but less strongly than it changes the halo statistics. At \(z=0\), \(P_{\rm BT}/P_{\rm PL}=1.17\) at \(k=20\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\) in the fiducial box, while \BTKPten{} remains close to unity (Sec.~\ref{subsub:PS}; Fig.~\ref{fig:power_spectra_ratio_z0}). Across the adopted intervals, the HMcode2020 BT/PL ratios differ from the simulation ratios by less than \(17\%\). The largest difference occurs for \BTKPone{} at \(z=8.52\) in the \(25\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\) box. Predictions for the absolute spectra are less accurate.}

\section{FOF Mass-Function Resolution and Volume Check}
\label{app:fof_hmf_resolution}

\currentrevisionblue{This appendix tests the FOF halo-mass-function range against changes in mass resolution and box volume. Figure~\ref{fig:fof_hmf_resolution} compares four PL runs at \(z=0\) and \(z=8.52\). The fiducial run has \(L_{\rm box}=25\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\) and \(1024^3\) particles. The same-volume resolution tests use \(512^3\) and \(256^3\) particles, while the volume test uses a \(50\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\), \(512^3\) run. The \(25\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\), \(256^3\) and \(50\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\), \(512^3\) runs have the same particle mass, allowing a volume comparison at fixed mass resolution.}

\currentrevisionblue{We use the same logarithmic bins, Poisson uncertainties, and Warren correction as in the main FOF mass-function analysis. Vertical dotted lines mark the 50-particle mass for each resolution, and the lower panels show ratios to PL-25-\(1024^3\). The reduced data and plotting script are provided in \texttt{public\_data/figure\_data/fof\_hmf\_resolution/} and \texttt{public\_data/scripts/plot\_fof\_hmf\_resolution.py}, respectively.}

\currentrevisionblue{At \(z=0\), the same-volume tests agree well above each comparison run's 50-particle mass. Over the common bins with at least 20 objects in both catalogs, PL-25-\(512^3\) has a median ratio of \(0.986\) to PL-25-\(1024^3\) over \(10^{8.92}\)--\(10^{12.25}\,M_\odot\). PL-25-\(256^3\) gives \(0.987\) over \(10^{9.92}\)--\(10^{12.25}\,M_\odot\). The corresponding 16th--84th percentile ranges are \(0.963\)--\(1.029\) and \(0.952\)--\(1.070\).}

\currentrevisionblue{The \(50\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\), \(512^3\) volume test gives a median ratio of \(0.945\) over \(10^{9.92}\)--\(10^{12.25}\,M_\odot\), with a 16th--84th percentile range of \(0.917\)--\(1.051\). The wider interval reflects finite-volume effects and differences in the random phases.}

\currentrevisionblue{At \(z=8.52\), the same-volume \(512^3\) run remains close to the fiducial measurement above its 50-particle mass. Its median ratio is \(0.935\) over \(10^{8.92}\)--\(10^{10.58}\,M_\odot\), with a 16th--84th percentile range of \(0.900\)--\(0.979\).}

The \(256^3\) same-volume run and the \(50\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\), \(512^3\) volume test cover only five common bins above their \(6.0\times10^9\,M_\odot\) 50-particle scale. Their median ratios are \(0.813\) and \(0.871\), respectively. \currentrevisionblue{These runs test mass resolution and box size for the PL mass function.} The fiducial \(1024^3\) HMF has substantial resolution margin above \(10^8\,M_\odot\). Lower-resolution boxes lose high-redshift low-mass halos when their 50-particle limits enter the plotted mass range.

Upper panels show the Warren-corrected FOF mass functions at \(z=0\) and \(z=8.52\) for the fiducial PL-25-\(1024^3\) run, same-volume lower-resolution PL-25-\(512^3\) and PL-25-\(256^3\) runs, and the larger PL-50-\(512^3\) run. \section{Alternative Concentration Prescriptions}
\label{app:concentration_alternative_models}

\currentrevisionblue{This appendix shows the concentration--mass comparison for two additional published prescriptions. The main text uses the Diemer \& Joyce (2019) prescription as the primary concentration reference. Figures~\ref{fig:concentration_comparison_i21_fit} and \ref{fig:concentration_comparison_l16} repeat the same simulation measurements with the Ishiyama et al. (2021) and Ludlow et al. (2016) prescriptions. The simulated concentration medians and halo-to-halo scatter are unchanged.}

\currentrevisionblue{At \(z=0\) and \(10^9\le M_{200c}/M_\odot<10^{9.5}\), the median \(c_{\rm sim}/c_{\rm I21,fit}\) values are \(0.96\), \(1.38\), and \(1.12\) for PL, \BTKPone{}, and \BTKPten{}, respectively.}

%\Tk{mention Ludlow is less accurate at high mass end especially high redshift, since it does not have turn-over.}

\currentrevisionblue{The L16 comparison is less accurate at the high-mass end, especially at \(z=8.52\). The simulated concentration--mass relation turns upward in the highest-mass bins, whereas the L16 curve continues to decline and misses this turnover. However, the Ishiyama et al. (2021) and Diemer \& Joyce (2019) models can capture this upturn.}
