\begin{abstract}
\revisionreplace{The small-scale shape of the primordial power spectrum is still weakly constrained. If primordial power is enhanced on these scales, small-scale density fluctuations can collapse earlier and change the abundance and internal structure of dark matter halos. We test this effect with dark-matter-only cosmological \(N\)-body simulations. Using matched initial phases, we compare a standard power-law model with two blue-tilted benchmark models, in which the small-scale power is enhanced above a pivot wavenumber \(k_p\). We measure halo mass functions, assembly histories, concentration--mass relations, and the nonlinear matter power spectrum.}{The large-scale primordial power spectrum is well constrained, but observational constraints on its small-scale shape (\(<1\,{\rm cMpc}\)) remain limited. We use dark-matter-only cosmological \(N\)-body simulations to quantify how enhancements in the small-scale primordial power spectrum affect nonlinear structure formation. We compare a standard power-law primordial spectrum with two blue-tilted spectra (\(P(k)\propto k^{m_s}\) with \(m_s>1\) above a pivot wavenumber \(k_p\)), examining their halo mass functions, mass assembly histories, mass-concentration relations, and nonlinear matter power spectra.}

\revisionreplace{The model with \(k_p=1\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\) and \(m_s=1.5\), which we call BT\_soft, gives the largest response. Its FOF halo abundance is higher than in the power-law model by factors of \(4.8\)--\(6.6\) at \(z=8.52\) over \(M_{\rm FOF}\simeq1.2\times10^9\)--\(1.2\times10^{10}\,M_\odot\), and by \(1.4\)--\(1.5\) at \(z=0\) over the same mass range. Halo assembly also shifts earlier: near \(M_{z=0}\simeq1.25\times10^9\,M_\odot\), the half-mass redshift \(z_{1/2}\) increases from \(2.28\) in the power-law model to \(4.24\) in BT\_soft. At \(z=0\), the median concentration in the \(10^9\)--\(10^{9.5}\,M_\odot\) bin is higher by a factor of \(3.20\). The nonlinear matter power spectrum changes less strongly, with \(P_{\rm BT}/P_{\rm PL}=1.17\) at \(k=20\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\) and \(1.34\) at \(k=40\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\).}{We find that the blue-tilted model with $k_p=1\,h\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1};\; m_s=1.5$ significantly boosts halo abundance relative to the power-law model, by factors of 5-7 at $z=8.52$ for halo mass $M \sim 10^{10}\,M_\odot$, and by 50\% at $z=0$. Halo assembly also shifts to earlier times: for $M_{z=0}\simeq 10^9\,M_\odot$, the mean half-mass formation redshift $z_{1/2}$ increases from $\sim 2$ to $\sim 4$. Dark matter halos in the blue-tilted model are more concentrated: the median halo concentration in the blue-tilted model for $M \sim 10^9\,M_\odot$ is higher by factors of $\sim 3$ at $z=0$. For the nonlinear matter-power summary statistic, the response is more modest but measurable: in the fiducial \(25\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\) box at \(z=0\), BT\_soft reaches \(P_{\rm BT}/P_{\rm PL}=1.17\) at \(k=20\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\) and \(1.34\) at \(k=40\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\), whereas BT\_deep remains close to unity at both wavenumbers.}

\revisionreplace{These results show that enhanced small-scale primordial power has its clearest effect on low-mass halos and on the nonlinear matter power spectrum at small scales, with a response that depends on pivot scale and redshift. The simulations provide benchmarks for how dark matter halos respond to enhanced small-scale primordial power, which can be compared in later work with high-redshift galaxies, dwarf galaxies, strong lensing, and 21 cm measurements.}{We benchmark these blue-tilted simulations against analytic models, providing an efficient means to constrain the small-scale power-spectrum with current and upcoming observations, including high-redshift galaxies observed by the James Webb Space Telescope, dwarf-galaxy structure, strong lensing, and 21 cm measurements.}

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction\label{intro}}
\revisionblue{On large scales, the standard \(\Lambda\) Cold Dark Matter (\(\Lambda\)CDM) model with a nearly power-law primordial power spectrum agrees well with measurements of the cosmic microwave background, galaxy clustering, and the Lyman-\(\alpha\) forest \citep{Planck20CMB,Blanton17SDSSsurvey,Chabanier19LyalphaPS}. On smaller scales, the shape of the primordial power spectrum is less tightly measured. Earlier small-scale problems, such as the missing-satellites, core-cusp, and too-big-to-fail problems, motivated tests of departures from the simplest cold-dark-matter picture \citep{Bullock17smallscalechallenges,Sales22challenges}. These problems are difficult to interpret on their own because baryonic physics can change the same observables \citep{Benson2002reionization,Chan2015corecusp}.}

\revisionblue{The recent JWST discovery of abundant and potentially massive galaxy candidates at \(z\gtrsim 8\)--\(10\) has renewed interest in the small-scale primordial power spectrum \citep{Labbe23MassiveGalaxies,Donnan23UVLF}. One possible explanation is enhanced small-scale primordial power, which can make low-mass halos form earlier \citep{Parashari23JWSTPPS,Hira24bluetilt}. This is not the only explanation: high-redshift galaxy formation, dust, feedback, star-formation efficiency, UV emission efficiency, and selection effects can also affect the observed galaxy counts \citep{Lovell23JWSTExtreme}.}



%One economical way to test this possibility is to modify the input matter power only on scales that are not already tightly constrained by CMB and large-scale structure data. 


\revisionblue{Several theoretical models can produce enhanced small-scale power. Warm inflation can amplify curvature perturbations \citep{Arya20WarmInflation}. Axion models with curved field-space effects can produce blue spectra \citep{Sai26BlueTilt}, and multi-field or hybrid inflation models can produce broken-power-law enhancements \citep{Linde94Hybridinflation,Wang24BTinflationtheory,Wang25HybridInflation}. A population of primordial black holes (PBHs) can also add small-scale matter power through Poisson fluctuations, which act as an isocurvature component \citep{Inman2019PBHBT}. We do not model these mechanisms separately. Instead, we use the blue-tilted spectra as simple benchmarks for testing how dark matter structure responds to enhanced small-scale primordial power.}




\revisionblue{Following previous literature \citep[e.g.][]{Hirano15BlueTiltedPPS,Parashari23JWSTPPS,Hira24bluetilt,Tkac24BumpyPPS,deKruijf25DarkAges,Dekker25DwarfPPS,Wu25BT}, we call these models ``blue-tilted'' (BT) because their small-scale slope is steeper than the usual power-law slope. Below a pivot wavenumber \(k_p\), the primordial power-spectrum template follows the standard PL shape. Above \(k_p\), the slope changes to \(m_s\), with \(m_s>n_s\), so the model has more power at larger \(k\), or smaller physical scales. In this paper the BT spectra are phenomenological benchmarks, not direct fits to a specific inflation model.}





%In this paper, the BT parameters should be read as a phenomenological description of the linear matter spectrum used to generate the initial conditions, not as a complete inflationary or curvature-spectrum model. 


Several recent studies have addressed this question with different probes. \revisionreplace{JWST-oriented studies ask whether enhanced small-scale primordial power can increase early galaxy abundances}{Enhanced or modified small-scale spectra have been invoked to interpret high-redshift galaxy abundances in light of JWST} \citep{Parashari23JWSTPPS,Hira24bluetilt}. The BT models have also been studied with zoom-in simulations of Milky Way--mass environments \citep{Wu25BT}. \revisionreplace{Other studies motivate complementary probes from dwarf-galaxy densities, satellite statistics, sub-galactic strong lensing, and 21 cm observations}{Other tests come from dwarf-galaxy densities, satellite statistics, sub-galactic strong lensing, and 21 cm observations} \citep{Kim18nomissingsatellites,Dekker25DwarfPPS,Gilman22SubgalacticPPS,Munoz20SmallScale21cm,deKruijf25DarkAges,Zhu26SKAPPS}. \revisionreplace{These probes are important, but their interpretation requires astrophysical and observational modeling; they do not by themselves isolate the dark-matter halo response to}{These probes are important, but they include astrophysical and observational uncertainties. They therefore do not by themselves show how the dark matter halo population responds to} \revisionreplace{a change in the small-scale primordial power spectrum}{changing the small-scale primordial matter power}.

The purpose of this paper is to isolate the dark-matter halo response to \revisionreplace{enhancements in the small-scale primordial power spectrum}{enhanced small-scale power in the primordial matter spectrum} before adding galaxy formation or survey selection. We therefore run dark-matter-only cosmological $N$-body simulations in periodic boxes, \revisionreplace{comparing a standard power-law primordial power-spectrum template with two BT templates and generating matched initial conditions from the corresponding input linear matter spectra}{comparing a standard power-law input matter spectrum with two transfer-function-modified BT benchmark models}. This approach is complementary to zoom-in studies of selected host environments \citep{Wu25BT}: here we measure halo statistics across a periodic volume. Our diagnostics include the halo mass function, half-mass assembly redshift, concentration-based structural diagnostics, and the nonlinear matter power spectrum. Together, these statistics show how enhanced small-scale primordial power changes dark matter halo abundance, assembly time, internal structure, and nonlinear matter clustering.

Section~\ref{sec:theory} defines the PL and BT primordial power-spectrum templates and their corresponding input linear matter power spectra at \(z_i=200\). Section~\ref{sec:numerical} describes the initial conditions, simulations, halo catalogs, and analysis choices. Section~\ref{sec:results} presents the halo mass function, assembly-redshift, concentration, and nonlinear matter-power results. Section~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses the physical interpretation, numerical limitations, and links to observations. Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} summarizes the main quantitative findings.}


\section{Theoretical Overview}
\label{sec:theory}

This study investigates the imprints of \revisionreplace{the shape of the primordial power spectrum}{primordial matter power spectrum} on the distributions and internal structures of dark matter halos. The theoretical overview defines \revisionreplace{the PL and BT primordial power-spectrum templates and the corresponding input linear matter power spectra used for the initial conditions}{the input matter spectra used to generate the simulations} and summarizes how their small-scale differences propagate into nonlinear structure formation.
\revisionblue{We use two terminology levels throughout the paper. ``Primordial power spectrum'' refers to the phenomenological early-Universe template in Eq.~\ref{eq:bt_input_power}; when emphasizing the seeded matter fluctuations, we occasionally use ``primordial matter power spectrum'' in the same descriptive sense. ``Input linear matter power spectrum at \(z_i=200\)'' refers to the spectrum actually passed to \textsc{Music2-MonofonIC} after applying the transfer-function implementation and the shared normalization.}

\subsection{From Matter Power Spectrum to Cosmic Structure}




%The simulation inputs are specified by the linear matter power spectrum at the initial redshift. In a complete early-universe model, this spectrum would be obtained by combining a primordial curvature spectrum with a transfer function and growth factor.Here, however, we do not introduce an independently normalized curvature-spectrum model. Instead, we describe the relevant small-scale modification directly through the shape of the initial-condition linear matter power spectrum used by the initial-condition generator. Up to a common normalization fixed by the shared background cosmology, the input linear spectrum can be written schematically as \(P_{\mathrm{lin}}(k,a)\propto D^2(a)T^2(k)k^{n_s}\), where \(D(a)\) is the growth factor and \(T(k)\) is the transfer function used by the initial-condition calculation. In the PL case, \(T(k)\) is the Eisenstein--Hu transfer function. In the BT cases, the code modifies the transfer function above a pivot scale \(k_p\), producing the target small-scale enhancement in the resulting input matter power spectrum. The subsequent gravitational collapse and nonlinear evolution of overdensities lead to the formation of a hierarchical network of dark matter halos, whose internal structure and abundance serve as fundamental probes of the underlying cosmology.

%\subsection{The Blue-Tilted Input-Power Model}

The primordial power spectrum in the usual power-law (PL) model is described by $P_{\rm prim}(k)\propto k^{n_s}$, where the spectral index $n_s\sim 0.96$ \citep{Planck20CMB}.

\revisionreplace{We model small-scale enhancements with blue-tilted (BT) primordial power-spectrum templates \citep[e.g.][]{Hira24bluetilt,Dekker25DwarfPPS}. In these templates, the spectrum follows the PL form up to \(k_p\) and becomes steeper above \(k_p\):}{We consider phenomenological blue-tilted models captured as a broken-power-law shape.}
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:bt_input_power}
    P_{\rm prim}(k)
    \propto
    \begin{cases}
        k^{n_s}, & k \le k_p,\\[4pt]
        k^{n_s}\left(k/k_p\right)^{m_s-n_s}, & k > k_p
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}
where \revisionreplace{\(k_p\) is the pivot wavenumber and \(m_s\) controls the target high-\(k\) slope of the primordial power-spectrum template}{\(z_i=200\), \(k_p\) is the pivot wavenumber, and $m_s$ controls the target high-\(k\) slope of the primordial matter spectrum}. This form reduces to the PL shape when \(m_s=n_s\), while \(m_s>n_s\) gives a blue-tilted enhancement above \(k_p\). \revisionblue{The initial redshift \(z_i=200\) enters later, when this template is converted into the input linear matter power spectrum used by the initial-condition generator.}

\revisionreplace{In the initial-condition calculation, we implement this target by modifying the transfer function used in the power-spectrum calculation. The generated input linear matter power spectrum at \(z_i=200\), \(P_{\rm lin}\propto T^2(k)k^{n_s}\), then carries the corresponding small-scale enhancement above \(k_p\).}{Operationally, this target is implemented by modifying the transfer function used in the power-spectrum calculation, so that \(P_{\rm lin}\propto T^2(k)k^{n_s}\) follows the broken-power-law form above \(k_p\).} 

\revisionreplace{The corresponding linear matter power spectrum can be written schematically as}{The matter power spectrum post recombination can be described with:}
\begin{align}
    P(k,t) = P_i(k) T^2(k) D^2(t),
    \label{eq:P_MD}
\end{align}
where $D(t)$ is the growth factor and  $T(k)$ is the linear transfer function (the Eisenstein--Hu transfer function here \citep{Eisenstein1998}).



%Equivalently, the transfer-function ratio needed to realize Eq.~\ref{eq:bt_input_power} is \(T_{\rm BT}(k)/T_{\rm PL}(k)=1\) for \(k\le k_p\) and \(T_{\rm BT}(k)/T_{\rm PL}(k)=(k/k_p)^{(m_s-n_s)/2}\) for \(k>k_p\). In this work the large-scale normalization is kept fixed; only the effective small-scale shape above $k_p$ is varied, so the model description can be specified by $k_p$ and $m_s$ for the present comparison. The BT spectra are therefore controlled transfer-function modifications of the initial-condition matter spectrum. The parameters $k_p$ and $m_s$ should not be interpreted as a complete inflationary parameterization or as independently constrained curvature-spectrum parameters in this paper.

In particular, we compare a \revisionreplace{standard power-law primordial power spectrum}{standard power-law primordial matter spectrum}, denoted PL, with two blue-tilted benchmark spectra, denoted BT\_soft and BT\_deep. The two BT runs use the same target small-scale spectral index but different pivot scales, as summarized in \revisionreplace{the combined model and run table, Table~\ref{tab:simulation_parameters}}{Table~\ref{tab:Params_all_models}}. BT\_soft affects a broader range of mildly nonlinear scales, whereas BT\_deep confines the enhancement to smaller scales. This pair of models allows us to test how the nonlinear response depends on the range of modes whose primordial power is enhanced.

The resulting input linear matter power spectrum at \(z_i=200\) follows the broken-power-law enhancement described in Section~\ref{sec:theory}: the shape below \(k_p\) is the PL/Eisenstein shape up to the small global normalization factor, while the power at \(k>k_p\) is enhanced.}{the implementation modifies the transfer function above \(k_p\), rather than introducing a separate explicitly normalized curvature-spectrum amplitude, so that the resulting input linear matter power spectrum at \(z_i=200\) follows the broken-power-law enhancement described in Section~\ref{sec:theory}. The large-scale modes remain unchanged while the power at $k>k_p$ is enhanced.} \revisionreplace{For each fixed box size and particle number, we keep the background cosmology, numerical settings, and MonofonIC random seed identical across the matched PL and BT runs. The main PL, BT\_soft, and BT\_deep \(25\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\), \(1024^3\) simulations therefore share the same initial phases. Given these matched phases, we can directly test the effect of enhancing the small-scale primordial power-spectrum template.}{For each fixed box size and particle number, the background cosmology and numerical settings are kept identical across the compared models. \subsection{Halo Finder and Halo Property Post-processor}

Halos and merger trees are identified with \textsc{HBT-HERONS}, a particle-tracking halo finder developed by \cite{Victor25HBTHERONS}\footnote{\url{https://github.com/SWIFTSIM/HBT-HERONS}}. We require a minimum of 20 particles for an object to be retained as a halo candidate, and define the halo center as the position of the most bound particle. For the main $25\,h^{-1}\mathrm{Mpc}$, $1024^3$ runs, the snapshot mass table gives \(m_{\rm DM}=1.89\times10^6\,M_\odot=1.27\times10^6\,h^{-1}M_\odot\). The nominal 20-particle halo threshold is therefore \(M\simeq3.8\times10^7\,M_\odot\), while a 100-particle structural cut corresponds to \(M\simeq1.9\times10^8\,M_\odot\). Based on the \textsc{HBT-HERONS} catalogs, we then use \textsc{SOAP} (Spherical Overdensity and Aperture Processor) \cite{McGibbon2025SOAP}\footnote{\url{https://github.com/SWIFTSIM/SOAP}} to compute spherical-overdensity properties such as $M_{200c}$, $R_{200c}$, and the structural quantities used below. This separation between halo identification and property measurement retains the tracking and merger-tree information used for the half-mass assembly analysis while adopting consistent spherical-overdensity quantities for the \revisionreplace{NFW profile and concentration analyses}{compactness and concentration analyses}.

Different statistics use different halo mass definitions. For the halo mass function, we use Friends-of-Friends (FOF) algorithm \citep{Davis1985FOF} to define halo masses and compare them to analytic predictions evaluated with the same FOF mass definition. The half-mass assembly diagnostic uses FOF-based same-TrackId mass histories from the \textsc{HBT-HERONS} catalogs, so its $z=0$ mass bins should be interpreted as $M_{\rm FOF}$ bins. For the internal-structure diagnostics, we use spherical-overdensity quantities measured by \textsc{SOAP}: the \revisionreplace{concentration-derived NFW density-profile reconstruction}{NFW-equivalent compactness diagnostic} uses \texttt{SO/200\_mean} masses, radii, and concentrations, while the concentration--mass relation uses $M_{200c}$, $R_{200c}$, and $c_{200c}$ from \texttt{SO/200\_crit}. The nonlinear matter power spectrum is \revisionreplace{measured from the particle density field}{a particle-field statistic} and does not require a halo mass definition. We therefore avoid directly mixing FOF, $M_{200m}$, and $M_{200c}$ masses when interpreting a single statistic, and the relevant captions specify the mass definition used.

\subsection{Halo Mass Function\label{subsub:HMF}}

Figure~\ref{fig:mass_function} shows the FOF halo mass functions at representative snapshots from $z=8.52$ to $z=0$. The plotted simulation points are computed from the FOF catalogs in logarithmic mass bins, with Poisson counting uncertainties and the Warren et al. correction, $N_{\rm corr}=N(1-N^{-0.6})$, applied at the low-particle end \citep{Warren_2006}. \revisionreplace{The FOF mass-function plot uses the conservative lower cut summarized in Table~\ref{tab:trusted_ranges}, \(M_{\rm FOF}\ge10^8\,M_\odot\), and the quantitative mass bins discussed below are above this limit.}{For the fiducial \(25\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\), \(1024^3\) runs, the particle mass is \(1.89\times10^6\,M_\odot\), so the nominal 20-particle halo threshold is \(3.8\times10^7\,M_\odot\). The FOF mass-function plot applies a more conservative lower cut \(M_{\rm FOF}\ge10^8\,M_\odot\), corresponding to roughly 50 particles before the Warren correction, and the quantitative mass bins discussed below are above this limit.} \revisionblue{Appendix~\ref{app:fof_hmf_resolution} repeats the PL FOF mass-function measurement at lower particle resolution and in a larger PL box to show how this adopted mass range behaves under numerical resolution and volume changes.}

The analytic curves use the \revisionreplace{Reed et al. (2007) FOF fitting function}{Reed07 FOF fitting function} \citep{Reed_2007} \revisionreplace{for the FOF comparison, with separate PL, BT\_soft, and BT\_deep predictions computed from the corresponding linear matter power spectra}{evaluated with the same FOF mass definition and with the same model-matched linear matter power spectra generated from the PL or BT primordial power-spectrum templates as the simulations}. \revisionreplace{These curves are used only as reference HMF predictions; the simulation points and BT/PL ratios are the measured nonlinear response.}{The comparison is therefore model-matched at the level of the primordial power-spectrum template after transfer-function conversion, while the simulation points capture the nonlinear abundance response.} Because halo mass-function fits are calibrated for specific halo definitions, we keep the FOF and spherical-overdensity comparisons separate: Fig.~\ref{fig:mass_function} uses FOF masses with \revisionreplace{the Reed et al. (2007) fitting function}{Reed07}, while the $M_{200c}$ cross-check in Fig.~\ref{fig:m200c_bocquet16_hmf} uses SOAP spherical-overdensity masses with the \revisionreplace{Bocquet et al. (2016) fitting function}{Bocquet et al. fitting function} \citep{Bocquet2016}.

The lower ratio panels show the \revisionreplace{BT/PL abundance ratio}{direct model response}, \(f_{\rm BT}(M)/f_{\rm PL}(M)\), computed in the same FOF mass bins and plotted where both the BT and PL bins are populated. Here \(f(M)=dn/d\log_{10}M\) is the halo mass function in the plotted logarithmic-bin convention. The ratio error bars propagate the Poisson counting uncertainties from both simulation measurements. We do not show the \revisionreplace{simulation-to-Reed et al. (2007) residuals}{simulation-to-Reed07 residuals} in the main figure, so that the ratio panels focus on the BT response relative to PL.

Within the plotted resolved range, both blue-tilted models increase the low-mass FOF abundance relative to PL, with the largest response in BT\_soft. The effect is most visible at high redshift and weakens by \(z=0\): for BT\_soft, the abundance ratio relative to PL is \(4.81\)--\(6.55\) over \(M_{\rm FOF}\simeq1.2\times10^9\)--\(1.2\times10^{10}\,M_\odot\) at \(z=8.52\), but \(1.42\)--\(1.47\) over the same mass range at \(z=0\). The high-mass end approaches PL because the transfer-function modification affects \revisionreplace{the small-scale primordial power that seeds lower-mass objects}{smaller-scale input matter power} most strongly, and because late nonlinear growth and mergers partially erase the relative abundance contrast. This interpretation is restricted to the bins above the plotted catalog cut, \(M_{\rm FOF}\ge10^8\,M_\odot\).

Following the multiplicity-function convention used for the \revisionreplace{Reed et al. (2007) fitting formula}{Reed07 fitting formula} \citep{Reed_2007}, the analytic halo mass function can be written as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:hmf_multiplicity}
\frac{dn}{d\ln M}
=
\frac{\bar{\rho}_{\rm m}}{M}
f(\sigma)
\left|\frac{d\ln \sigma^{-1}}{d\ln M}\right| ,
\end{equation}
where \(\bar{\rho}_{\rm m}\) is the mean matter density, \(\sigma(M,z)\) is the linear-theory rms fluctuation of the density field smoothed on the mass scale \(M\), and \(f(\sigma)\) is the \revisionreplace{Reed et al. (2007) FOF multiplicity function}{Reed07 FOF multiplicity function}. In our comparison, \(\sigma(M,z)\) is computed from the \revisionreplace{model-matched linear matter power spectrum}{model-matched linear power spectrum} for PL, BT\_soft, or BT\_deep.

\subsection{Halo Internal Structure}

\subsubsection{Radial Halo Density Profiles\label{subsub:DensityProfile}}
We next use \revisionreplace{direct particle radial density profiles}{a concentration-derived NFW-equivalent compactness diagnostic} to ask whether earlier assembly is reflected in halo internal structure. Figure~\ref{fig:halo_density_analysis} compares median particle-count radial profiles in \textsc{SOAP} \(M_{200m}\) bins, rather than profiles reconstructed only from catalog concentrations. The dashed curves show Diemer et al. NFW reference profiles evaluated for the corresponding PL and BT models \citep{Diemer2019}. The comparison is therefore a direct check of the dark-matter density profile over the resolved radial range, while the gray shaded region marks radii inside the plotted convergence criterion.

The structural response is concentrated toward the halo centers. In the resolved inner regions, BT\_soft has the largest central-density enhancement relative to PL, BT\_deep is weaker, and the three models approach one another toward larger \(r/R_{200m}\). The lower ratio panels show that the BT\_soft enhancement is strongest in the low- and intermediate-mass bins, while BT\_deep is closer to PL. This ordering is consistent with the direct \(V_{\max}\)--\(R_{\max}\) summary at \(z=0\): in the \(10^9\)--\(10^{9.5}\,M_\odot\) bin, BT\_soft has a median \(V_{\max}\) that is \(1.27\) times larger than PL and a median \(R_{\max}\) that is \(0.59\) times the PL value, while BT\_deep gives ratios of \(1.06\) and \(0.82\).

At larger radii (\(r\gtrsim0.3R_{200m}\)), the differences are weaker and more mass dependent. In the highest-mass bins shown, the measured profiles converge within the visual precision of the plotted curves. We therefore interpret the radial profiles as a direct dark-matter structural signal associated with earlier collapse, not as evidence for a different subhalo population. A subhalo interpretation would require host-controlled subhalo mass functions, radial number-density profiles, and explicit subhalo resolution cuts, which are outside the scope of this work.

\subsubsection{Concentration-Mass Relation\label{subsub:concentration}}
The concentration--mass relation provides a compact summary of the halo structural response. We use the \textsc{SOAP} \texttt{SO/200\_crit} catalogs associated with the \textsc{HBT-HERONS} halo catalogs, taking \(M_{200c}\), \(c_{200c}\), and the number of dark-matter particles for each halo. Catalog masses are converted to solar masses using the stored unit factor \(10^{10}\,M_{\odot}\). We require finite positive masses and concentrations, impose \(c_{200c}<500\), and retain only halos with at least 100 dark-matter particles, corresponding to \(M_{200c}\simeq1.9\times10^8\,M_\odot\) for the main-run particle mass.

The simulation points in Fig.~\ref{fig:concentration_comparison} show the median \(\log_{10}c_{200c}\) in 35 logarithmic mass bins over \(8.0 \le \log_{10}(M_{200c}/M_{\odot}) \le 13.5\), with error bars given by the 16th--84th percentile scatter. For comparison, the same figure shows the semi-analytic concentration models evaluated with \textsc{colossus} \citep{Diemer2018} using the \(200c\) mass definition and the corresponding PL or BT \revisionreplace{linear matter power spectra generated from the primordial power-spectrum templates}{input power spectra}. The plotted model curves use the same background cosmological parameters as the simulations and include the Diemer \& Joyce (2019) and Ishiyama et al. (2021) prescriptions \citep{Diemer2019,Ishiyama2021}.

Here \(c_{200c}=R_{200c}/r_s\), where \(R_{200c}\) is the spherical-overdensity radius with respect to the critical density and \(r_s\) is the scale radius of the NFW profile \citep{Navarro1996NFW,Navarro1997NFW}. Figure~\ref{fig:concentration_comparison} compares the concentration--mass relation at four representative redshifts for PL, BT\_soft, and BT\_deep. This notation replaces the older ``strong'' and ``weak'' BT language: BT\_soft is the \(k_p=1\,h\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}\) model, while BT\_deep is the \(k_p=10\,h\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}\) model.

The measured medians show a clear mass- and redshift-dependent concentration response. At \(z=0\), the median concentration in the \(10^9\)--\(10^{9.5}\,M_\odot\) bin is higher than PL by factors of \(3.20\) for BT\_soft and \(1.35\) for BT\_deep. \revisionblue{The 16th--84th percentile ranges in Fig.~\ref{fig:concentration_comparison} show substantial halo-to-halo scatter, especially toward the low-particle end, so we use the median trends and quoted bin ratios rather than fitting individual-halo concentrations.} The larger BT\_soft response is consistent with the HMF and assembly-history results because its \revisionreplace{primordial power-spectrum template enhances a broader range of resolved modes after transfer-function conversion}{transfer-function-modified input spectrum enhances a broader range of resolved modes}. More generally, halo concentrations are known to encode formation history and to respond to changes in the linear power spectrum in non-standard small-scale-power simulations \citep{Ludlow2016WDMConcentration,Brown2020HaloPPS}. We therefore interpret the concentration enhancement as the structural counterpart of earlier assembly, not as an independent fitted input-power parameter.
The semi-analytic curves should be interpreted as published concentration--mass mappings rather than independent fits to our binned simulation points. In these comparisons,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:colossus_concentration}
c_{200c}^{\rm th}(M,z)=\mathcal{C}_{\rm model}\!\left[M_{200c},z;P_{\rm lin}(k),\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\rm cosmo}\right],
\end{equation}
where \(\mathcal{C}_{\rm model}\) denotes the \revisionreplace{Diemer \& Joyce (2019) or Ishiyama et al. (2021) prescription}{Diemer--Joyce or Ishiyama prescription} implemented in \textsc{colossus}, \(P_{\rm lin}(k)\) is the PL or BT linear matter power spectrum, and \(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\rm cosmo}\) denotes the shared background cosmological parameters. The mass and concentration are defined with the same spherical-overdensity convention used for the simulation points,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:m200c_c200c_definitions}
\begin{gathered}
M_{200c}=\frac{4\pi}{3}\,200\rho_{\rm crit}(z)R_{200c}^{3},
\qquad
c_{200c}=\frac{R_{200c}}{r_s}.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The underlying assumption is that halo concentration is primarily tied to the collapse history encoded in the linear density field. The blue-tilted models therefore change the predicted concentration through the \revisionreplace{linear matter power spectrum generated from the primordial power-spectrum template}{input power spectrum}, not through a refit of the analytic curves to the simulation measurements.

\subsection{Power Spectrum\label{subsub:PS}}

Finally, the nonlinear matter power spectrum gives \revisionreplace{a matter power-spectrum summary statistic measured from the particle density field}{a field-level view of the same response}. We measure the spectra from the dark-matter particle distribution using the \textsc{swiftsimio} power-spectrum utilities. For each snapshot, particle masses are deposited onto a regular \(1024^3\) Cartesian mesh with \texttt{render\_to\_deposit} using \texttt{project="masses"}, and the isotropically averaged spectrum is then computed with \texttt{deposition\_to\_power\_spectrum}. In the production script we set \texttt{folding=0}, \texttt{cross\_deposition=None}, \texttt{wavenumber\_bins=None}, and pass the number of dark-matter particles as \texttt{shot\_noise\_norm}. The resulting wavenumbers and powers are converted from \({\rm Mpc}^{-1}\) and \({\rm Mpc}^3\) to \(h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\) and \((h^{-1}{\rm Mpc})^3\), respectively. For visualization only, the measured spectra and ratios are subsequently log-binned by taking the geometric-mean wavenumber and median power in each plotting bin.

\revisionreplace{The nonlinear theory curves shown for orientation are generated separately from the simulations with the HMcode2020 dark-matter-only nonlinear mapping as implemented in \texttt{pyhmcode}. We first build a PL linear matter spectrum from the Eisenstein--Hu transfer function and shared cosmological parameters. For each BT case, the same PL linear spectrum is multiplied by the broken-power-law enhancement above the corresponding \(k_p\), and the resulting PL or BT linear spectrum is supplied to HMcode2020. Thus the lower-panel ``BT theory/PL theory'' curves are ratios of these HMcode2020 predictions, not fits to the simulated BT/PL ratios and not an independent calibration for the blue-tilted cosmology.}{The nonlinear theory curves shown for orientation are generated separately from the simulations. The PL curve starts from an Eisenstein--Hu linear matter spectrum with the shared cosmological parameters. For each BT curve, we multiply that PL linear spectrum by the broken-power-law enhancement above the corresponding \(k_p\), then pass the PL or BT linear spectrum to the dark-matter-only HMcode2020 calculation through \texttt{pyhmcode}. These curves are reference predictions for the same input spectra, not fits to the simulation ratios.}

Figure~\ref{fig:power_spectra_ratio_z0} emphasizes the physical response by showing matched BT/PL ratios for the \(k_p=1\) and \(k_p=10\), \(m_s=1.5\) variants in the \(25\) and \(256\,h^{-1}\mathrm{Mpc}\) boxes. We restrict the quantitative ratio analysis to matched PL and BT products with the same box size. No additional folding correction or external window-function deconvolution is applied outside the \textsc{swiftsimio} routine, so the highest-\(k\) points are interpreted as a consistency check on the small-scale trend rather than as a calibrated nonlinear-power fitting formula. \revisionblue{Appendix~\ref{app:power_spectrum_reliability} shows the separate simulation/theory validation plot used to visualize the box-size-dependent reliability ranges.}

At \(z=0\), the fiducial \(25\,h^{-1}\mathrm{Mpc}\) BT\_soft/PL ratio is close to unity on large scales and rises gradually toward high \(k\): \(1.06\), \(1.10\), \(1.17\), and \(1.34\) at \(k=5\), \(10\), \(20\), and \(40\,h\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}\), respectively. In the larger \(256\,h^{-1}\mathrm{Mpc}\) comparison, the response is weaker over its conservative mesh range, reaching \(1.06\) at \(k=10\,h\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}\) and \(1.08\) near its mesh Nyquist scale, \(k_{\rm Ny}\simeq12.6\,h\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}\). We therefore interpret the nonlinear power-spectrum result as a coherent high-\(k\) signal that is qualitatively consistent with the halo diagnostics, but whose exact amplitude remains sensitive to box size, estimator choices, and the high-\(k\) reliability range.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Physical Interpretation of the Main Trends}

The \revisionreplace{results in Section~\ref{sec:results} are consistent with the following physical picture}{results in Section~\ref{sec:results} point to a single physical picture}. \revisionreplace{Enhancing the small-scale primordial power spectrum raises the amplitude of the corresponding small-scale density fluctuations in the initial conditions. In the BT runs, these stronger initial fluctuations tend to collapse earlier than in PL. The earliest visible effect is a high-redshift excess of low-mass halos; at later times, the same trend appears in earlier assembly times, shifted mass-accretion histories, and higher central densities.}{Increasing the small-scale input matter power at \(z_i=200\) raises the initial amplitude of low-mass density fluctuations, so these perturbations cross the collapse threshold earlier than in the PL model. This earlier collapse first appears as a high-redshift abundance excess and then leaves a lower-redshift structural imprint.} For BT\_soft, the FOF abundance at $z=8.52$ is larger than PL by factors of $4.81$--$6.55$ over $M\simeq1.2\times10^9$--$1.2\times10^{10}\,M_\odot$, while the raw same-\texttt{TrackId} half-mass diagnostic near \(M_{z=0}\simeq1.25\times10^9\,M_\odot\) shifts from \(z_{1/2}=2.28\) in PL to \(4.24\) in BT\_soft. Across the \(10^9\)--\(10^{10}\,M_\odot\) range, the same diagnostic shows BT\_soft reaching half of its final FOF mass earlier than PL by roughly \(1.1\)--\(2.0\) in redshift. The mass-accretion-rate diagnostic further shows that BT\_soft growth is shifted to earlier epochs rather than being a simple late-time boost. At $z=0$, the same earlier formation history is reflected in the internal structure of halos: in the $10^9$--$10^{9.5}\,M_\odot$ bin, the median concentration is higher than PL by a factor of $3.20$ for BT\_soft, and the direct radial profile shows a corresponding inner-density enhancement.

The \revisionreplace{BT response varies with pivot scale, redshift, halo mass, and the statistic being measured, such as abundance, assembly time, concentration, or \(P(k)\)}{signal is not simply a uniform rescaling of all nonlinear statistics. It depends on the pivot scale, redshift, halo mass, and diagnostic}. BT\_soft produces the larger response because its enhancement begins at $k_p=1\,h\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}$ and therefore affects more of the resolved mode range in the fiducial box. BT\_deep, with $k_p=10\,h\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}$, gives a weaker response in the halo mass function and concentration--mass relation, and becomes close to PL at sufficiently high halo mass. The abundance ratios also decrease at low redshift: for example, the BT\_soft/PL ratio at $M\simeq1.21\times10^9\,M_\odot$ falls from $4.81$ at $z=8.52$ to $1.42$ at $z=0$. This evolution is expected because nonlinear growth and hierarchical merging partially erase the relative abundance contrast by the present day, even though halo assembly histories and concentrations retain a memory of the earlier collapse.

The nonlinear matter power spectrum provides a complementary \revisionreplace{matter power-spectrum summary statistic}{field-level view}. In the matched PL and BT\_soft fiducial-box comparison, the ratio reaches $P_{\rm BT}/P_{\rm PL}=1.17$ at $k=20\,h\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}$ and $1.34$ at $k=40\,h\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}$. This response is more modest than the low-mass halo and concentration signals, but it has the same sign and appears on the expected small scales. \revisionreplace{Taken together, these diagnostics are consistent with a common response to the modified primordial power spectrum after transfer-function conversion into the initial conditions, rather than a feature of one statistic alone.}{The agreement among halo abundance, assembly history, concentration-based structural diagnostics, and high-$k$ clustering supports the interpretation that the measured trends are driven by the transfer-function-induced change in the input matter power spectrum rather than by one particular analysis statistic.}

\subsection{Relation to Previous Work}

This study is complementary to work that connects \revisionreplace{modified small-scale primordial power spectra}{modified small-scale primordial matter power} to individual observational probes. JWST-motivated studies ask whether enhanced small-scale power can help produce more early luminous galaxies \citep{Parashari23JWSTPPS,Hira24bluetilt}. \revisionreplace{Our focus is the dark-matter halo response before observational modeling: the BT models form more low-mass halos at high redshift and shift halo assembly earlier. These are the dark-matter effects that any galaxy-formation interpretation would have to build on.}{Because the simulations analyzed here are collisionless dark-matter-only runs, they do not include a galaxy-formation or survey-selection model. We therefore do not predict galaxy luminosities or observed galaxy number counts directly. Instead, we test the underlying dark-matter response required by such scenarios: the BT models form more low-mass halos at high redshift and shift halo assembly earlier, both of which would be prerequisites for increasing the abundance of early galaxies.}

The present periodic-box approach also differs from zoom-in studies of individual environments. Zoom-in simulations can follow the satellite and subhalo population of a Milky Way--mass host in detail \citep{Wu25BT}, whereas our boxes are designed to measure population-level halo statistics and \revisionreplace{matter power-spectrum statistics}{matter-field statistics} such as the nonlinear matter power spectrum. We therefore avoid drawing subhalo-specific conclusions from the projected maps or stacked host profiles. Instead, our concentration-based structural measurements provide the corresponding dark-matter-only response for isolated halo structure, which is closely related to the quantities entering dwarf-galaxy density constraints and strong-lensing analyses of low-mass structure \citep{Dekker25DwarfPPS,Gilman22SubgalacticPPS}.

The connection to 21 cm studies is more indirect but still useful. Cosmic-dawn and dark-ages measurements are sensitive to the abundance and timing of early structure formation, and have been proposed as probes of small-scale matter fluctuations beyond the scales directly tested by the CMB and galaxy surveys \citep{Munoz20SmallScale21cm,deKruijf25DarkAges,Zhu26SKAPPS}. The simulations here identify the mass, redshift, and wavenumber ranges where \revisionreplace{enhanced primordial power}{enhanced primordial matter power} produces the largest dark-matter response. \revisionreplace{They are therefore a dark-matter-only benchmark for later observation-specific forward modeling.}{They are therefore best viewed as an intermediate simulation-level calibration, not as a replacement for observation-specific forward modeling.}

\subsection{Numerical and Modeling Limitations}

Several limitations should be kept in mind. First, the simulations are collisionless dark-matter-only runs. This design cleanly isolates the gravitational response to the \revisionreplace{modified primordial power-spectrum template}{modified primordial matter power spectrum}, but it omits gas cooling, star formation, stellar and AGN feedback, and reionization. These processes can change halo density profiles, subhalo survival, and the mapping between halos and galaxies. The results should therefore be interpreted as predictions for the underlying dark matter distribution, not as direct predictions for observed galaxies or lensing samples without further modeling.

Second, the main PL, BT\_soft, and BT\_deep comparisons use one matched-seed realization per model. Using the same initial phases reduces realization noise in the model-to-model ratios and helps isolate the response to the \revisionreplace{modified primordial power spectrum after MonofonIC conversion}{modified input power spectrum}. However, the plotted HMF error bars include Poisson counting uncertainties only, while the concentration error bars show halo-to-halo scatter; neither includes cosmic variance or realization-to-realization scatter. \revisionreplace{This affects the covariance and precision of the quoted ratios, especially for rare high-mass halos, large-scale modes in the matter power spectrum, and statistics measured from smaller auxiliary boxes. It is not the main obstacle to observational interpretation; the larger missing ingredients are baryonic physics, reionization, galaxy selection, and survey response.}{This limitation is most important for rare high-mass halos, large-scale modes in the matter power spectrum, and any statistic measured from the smaller auxiliary boxes. The broad direction of the BT response is consistent across diagnostics, but the quoted ratios should be interpreted as single-realization simulation benchmarks rather than covariance-level constraints.}

Third, the usable range of each statistic is set by different numerical effects. The low-mass halo mass function is limited by particle discreteness and halo-finder thresholds; the concentration-based structural diagnostics are limited by particle number, force softening, centering, and the catalog concentration measurement; and the nonlinear matter power spectrum depends on box size, mesh assignment, force resolution, and high-$k$ estimator choices. In addition, the \revisionreplace{matter power-spectrum ratio}{field-level power-spectrum ratio} is quantified only for matched PL and BT products with the same box size.

Finally, the analytic curves used for the halo mass function and concentration--mass relation are controlled reference models, not exact predictions in the deeply nonlinear regime. They are useful because they are evaluated with the same \revisionreplace{linear matter power spectra generated from the transfer-function-modified primordial power-spectrum templates}{transfer-function-modified input power spectra}, but residual differences between the simulations and fitting formulae may reflect both physical nonlinear evolution and the limited calibration range of the analytic prescriptions.

\subsection{Connection to Observations}

The most direct observational connections are probes of low-mass structure and early collapse. High-redshift galaxy counts are sensitive to the abundance and assembly time of halos near the cooling and star-formation thresholds. Dwarf-galaxy dynamics and strong-lensing perturbations probe the density and abundance of low-mass structures at low redshift \citep{Dekker25DwarfPPS,Gilman22SubgalacticPPS}. Future 21 cm measurements can test related information in the timing of early structure formation and the small-scale matter field \citep{Munoz20SmallScale21cm,deKruijf25DarkAges,Zhu26SKAPPS}. \revisionreplace{The diagnostics measured here map onto these observables as dark-matter inputs, not as direct constraints; each application still requires the relevant baryonic, selection, and survey effects.}{The diagnostics measured here map naturally onto these observables, but only after the relevant baryonic, selection, and survey effects are added.}

The main value of the present work is therefore to identify where \revisionreplace{a dark-matter signal from enhanced primordial power}{an primordial-power signal} is likely to be largest before those additional complications are introduced. The simulations show that the strongest responses occur in high-redshift low-mass halo abundance, early assembly histories, low-mass halo concentrations, and high-$k$ matter clustering. These quantities can serve as a compact target set for future hydrodynamic simulations, semi-analytic galaxy modeling, reionization calculations, and likelihood analyses that combine several small-scale probes.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We have used dark-matter-only cosmological $N$-body simulations to quantify the effect of \revisionreplace{enhanced small-scale primordial power}{enhanced small-scale primordial matter spectrum} on nonlinear structure formation. By comparing simulations with a standard power-law and two blue-tilted (enhanced) models, we isolated the gravitational response of dark matter halos to the modified initial conditions before introducing baryonic physics, galaxy formation, or observational selection.

\revisionreplace{The main conclusions are as follows:}{A separate response-summary table collected the same trends. The main conclusions, with the corresponding result sections and figures, are as follows:}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The halo-abundance response is strongest at low mass and high redshift. In the FOF halo mass function (Section~\ref{subsub:HMF}; Fig.~\ref{fig:mass_function}), BT\_soft exceeds PL by factors of \(4.81\) and \(6.55\) at \(z=8.52\) for \(M_{\rm FOF}\simeq1.21\times10^9\) and \(1.21\times10^{10}\,M_\odot\), respectively, while the corresponding \(z=0\) ratios are \(1.42\) and \(1.47\). \revisionblue{In the same two mass bins, BT\_deep gives ratios of \(1.39\) and \(1.05\) at \(z=8.52\), and \(1.31\) and \(1.08\) at \(z=0\).} The \(M_{200c}\) cross-check with spherical-overdensity masses gives the same qualitative conclusion (Fig.~\ref{fig:m200c_bocquet16_hmf}).
    \item \revisionreplace{Enhanced small-scale primordial power shifts halo assembly earlier.}{Enhanced small-scale input power shifts halo assembly earlier.} In the raw same-\texttt{TrackId} half-mass diagnostic (Section~\ref{subsub:HMR}; Fig.~\ref{fig:half_mass_redshift}), the mean \(z_{1/2}\) near \(M_{z=0}\simeq1.25\times10^9\,M_\odot\) changes from \(2.28\) in PL to \(4.24\) in BT\_soft and \(2.61\) in BT\_deep. \revisionblue{The mass-accretion-rate diagnostic (Fig.~\ref{fig:mass_accretion_rate_trackid}) shows the same qualitative picture: BT models, especially BT\_soft, populate low-mass progenitor bins earlier, and their late-time growth is not simply a uniform accretion-rate boost.} This is the catalog-level assembly counterpart of the high-redshift abundance enhancement.
    \item The structural response follows the same physical ordering. The \revisionreplace{direct radial density profiles}{NFW-equivalent compactness diagnostic} (Section~\ref{subsub:DensityProfile}; Fig.~\ref{fig:halo_density_analysis}) and the concentration--mass relation (Section~\ref{subsub:concentration}; Fig.~\ref{fig:concentration_comparison}) both show stronger low-mass \revisionreplace{central density and concentration}{compactness} in the blue-tilted runs. At \(z=0\), the median \(c_{200c}\) in the \(10^9\)--\(10^{9.5}\,M_\odot\) bin is higher than PL by factors of \(3.20\) for BT\_soft and \(1.35\) for BT\_deep.
    \item The nonlinear matter power spectrum gives a coherent but more modest \revisionreplace{response in this summary statistic}{field-level signal}. In the matched \(25\,h^{-1}\mathrm{Mpc}\) fiducial comparison (Section~\ref{subsub:PS}; Fig.~\ref{fig:power_spectra_ratio_z0}), BT\_soft reaches \(P_{\rm BT}/P_{\rm PL}=1.17\) at \(k=20\,h\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}\) and \(1.34\) at \(k=40\,h\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}\). \revisionblue{BT\_deep is consistent with PL in the same comparison, with ratios of \(1.00\) at both wavenumbers.} \revisionreplace{The direction of the response is consistent with the halo diagnostics, but its amplitude is sensitive to box size and high-\(k\) estimator choices; we therefore do not provide a calibrated nonlinear-power fitting formula.}{The direction of the response is consistent with the halo diagnostics, but its amplitude is more sensitive to box size and high-\(k\) estimator choices.}
\end{enumerate}

\revisionreplace{For reproducibility, the reference calculations used in this benchmark are the PL/BT input-power prescription in Eq.~\ref{eq:bt_input_power}, the Reed et al. (2007) FOF halo mass-function prescription \citep{Reed_2007}, the Bocquet et al. (2016) \(M_{200c}\) prescription \citep{Bocquet2016}, and the Diemer \& Joyce (2019) and Ishiyama et al. (2021) concentration--mass mappings \citep{Diemer2019,Ishiyama2021}. These are controlled reference curves evaluated with the model-matched \(P_{\rm lin}(k)\), not new calibrations to the present simulation points. The projected maps are qualitative visualizations and should not be used to infer subhalo counts.}{The analytic ingredients that we recommend carrying forward from this benchmark are the tested, model-matched prescriptions rather than new fits to the present simulation points. These are the transfer-function-modified BT broken-power-law input spectrum in Eq.~\ref{eq:bt_input_power}; the Reed07 FOF halo mass-function prescription evaluated with the corresponding PL or BT \(P_{\rm lin}(k)\); the B16 \(M_{200c}\) mass-function prescription as an SO-mass cross-check; and the Diemer--Joyce and Ishiyama concentration--mass mappings implemented in \textsc{colossus}. We do not recommend using the projected maps as subhalo-count evidence or adopting a standalone nonlinear-power fitting formula from this work; those applications require additional calibration beyond the tests performed here.}

These trends are strongest in the low-mass, high-redshift, and high-wavenumber regimes, and they weaken toward massive halos and late times. \revisionreplace{These measurements are single-realization simulation benchmarks over the resolved mass and wavenumber ranges; they are not direct observational constraints, because observational applications require baryonic physics, reionization, galaxy selection, and survey response modeling.}{The present results should be read as resolved-range, single-realization simulation benchmarks rather than direct observational constraints.} \revisionreplace{Within those caveats, the central result is clear: enhanced small-scale primordial power leaves a measurable dark-matter signature in low-mass halo abundance, assembly time, concentration, and high-\(k\) clustering. This provides concrete resolved-scale benchmark targets for future hydrodynamic simulations, semi-analytic and reionization models, and multi-probe tests with high-redshift galaxies, dwarf-galaxy structure, strong lensing, 21 cm measurements, and small-scale clustering.}{They are relevant to JWST-era high-redshift galaxy studies, dwarf-galaxy structure, strong-lensing probes of low-mass structure, 21 cm forecasts, and small-scale clustering measurements, but each application requires additional modeling of baryons, reionization, galaxy selection, and survey response.}

\section{Temporary FOF Group Reassignment in Half-Mass Histories}
\label{app:fof_gap_stitching}

The half-mass assembly diagnostic in Fig.~\ref{fig:half_mass_redshift} uses FOF masses along \textsc{HBT-HERONS} tracks. This is useful for comparing PL, BT\_soft, and BT\_deep with a common history construction, but it also exposes a catalog-level ambiguity. Some objects keep the same \textsc{HBT-HERONS} \texttt{TrackId} and remain self-bound, yet for a small number of snapshots they are no longer listed as an independent FOF host. In the \textsc{SOAP}/\textsc{HBT-HERONS} catalog this appears as \texttt{InputHalos/FOF/Masses}=0 and \texttt{InputHalos/FOF/Sizes}=0 in the object's own row, while \texttt{InputHalos/IsCentral}=0 and \texttt{InputHalos/HBTplus/HostFOFId} points to a larger host group. The object has therefore not disappeared; its mass has been assigned to the host FOF group for that snapshot, while the subhalo still has non-zero \texttt{BoundSubhalo/TotalMass}.

The resulting gap is therefore a gap in the FOF-host mass history, not evidence that the tracked object has zero physical mass. During such an episode, the receiving FOF host can also acquire a transient mass excess and later lose it when the FOF groups split again, so individual FOF masses are not guaranteed to be monotonic. The \texttt{BoundSubhalo/TotalMass} history is more continuous in this situation because it follows the self-bound object identity rather than the instantaneous FOF group assignment. This behavior is closer to the intuitive picture in which two self-bound haloes have not fully merged but are temporarily linked by the FOF algorithm or exchange outer material. However, \texttt{BoundSubhalo/TotalMass} is not the same mass definition as the FOF or spherical-overdensity host-halo masses used in the BK09 and COCO/Hellwing16 reference relations, so we use it only as a diagnostic of object survival and catalog assignment, not as a direct replacement for the reference halo mass.

This behavior is important for FOF-based mass-accretion histories. If such a snapshot is treated as permanent termination of the progenitor branch, the history is truncated even though the same tracked object may appear again as an independent FOF halo in adjacent snapshots. Conversely, reconnecting across arbitrarily long gaps may be too permissive. To quantify the size of this effect, we performed a PL-only FOF gap-stitching test. The procedure is:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Select the same \(z=0\) FOF halo sample used in the PL half-mass analysis, giving \(344271\) halos.
    \item Follow the same \textsc{HBT-HERONS} \texttt{TrackId} over snapshots 0021--0056 and record Warren-corrected \texttt{InputHalos/FOF/Masses}.
    \item Mark a snapshot as a valid independent FOF-host point only if the same \texttt{TrackId} has positive FOF mass, positive FOF size, and is central. Snapshots where the same \texttt{TrackId} is present but has \texttt{FOF/Masses}=\texttt{FOF/Sizes}=0 are treated as temporary non-FOF assignments.
    \item Recompute \(z_{1/2}\) while allowing at most \(N_{\rm gap}\) consecutive non-FOF snapshots to be crossed before reconnecting to earlier valid FOF-host points. We test \(N_{\rm gap}=0,1,2,3,5\), and the limiting case in which all gaps are allowed.
\end{enumerate}

The strict case \(N_{\rm gap}=0\) is close to a FOF-host-only main-branch definition: it gives \(262310/344271\) measurable halos and a PL--BK09 by-bin mean absolute offset of \(0.2363\) in \(z_{1/2}\). Allowing short gaps monotonically restores measurable histories and moves the PL relation toward the BK09 reference: the measurable counts and BK09 offsets are \(292027\) and \(0.1850\) for \(N_{\rm gap}=1\), \(314254\) and \(0.1206\) for \(N_{\rm gap}=2\), \(323441\) and \(0.0871\) for \(N_{\rm gap}=3\), and \(327635\) and \(0.0664\) for \(N_{\rm gap}=5\). Allowing all gaps gives \(329621\) measurable halos and an offset of \(0.0548\).

As a separate check on the failure mode, among the PL halos that are measurable by a same-\texttt{TrackId} FOF history but not by the strict corrected descendant-rule branch, \(67077/67328\) (\(99.63\%\)) stop at a snapshot where a same-\texttt{TrackId} candidate exists but has \texttt{FOF/Sizes}=0. This confirms that the dominant issue is temporary FOF group reassignment rather than loss of the \textsc{HBT-HERONS} track itself. We therefore interpret the raw same-\texttt{TrackId} assembly curve in Fig.~\ref{fig:half_mass_redshift} as a direct no-envelope catalog-history diagnostic, and the gap-stitching curves in Fig.~\ref{fig:fof_gap_stitching} as a bracket on the associated systematic, rather than as a claim that the \textsc{HBT-HERONS} bound-subhalo mass is the same quantity as the FOF main-progenitor mass used in the BK09 and COCO/Hellwing16 reference relations \citep{BoylanKolchin2009MSII,Hellwing16cocoproject}.

\section{Power-Spectrum Box-Size Reliability}
\label{app:power_spectrum_reliability}

\revisionblue{Figure~\ref{fig:power_spectrum_validation_theory} provides the simulation/theory validation view used to judge the wavenumber ranges of the different power-spectrum boxes. The measured spectra are derived from the cached \texttt{.npz} products in \texttt{powerspectrum/sim\_power\_data/}, each of which stores the arrays \texttt{k}, \texttt{P}, and \texttt{z}. These caches were generated from the SWIFT snapshots with \texttt{compute\_sim\_power\_spectrum.py}: dark-matter particles are deposited on a \(1024^3\) mesh, the isotropically averaged power spectrum is computed with the \textsc{swiftsimio} power-spectrum utilities, and the output is converted to \(k\) in \(h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\) and \(P(k)\) in \(({\rm Mpc}/h)^3\). The reduced public-data package contains the corresponding tabulated power-spectrum measurements in \texttt{public\_data/figure\_data/nonlinear\_power\_spectrum/}, together with provenance copies of \texttt{compute\_sim\_power\_spectrum.py} and \texttt{plot\_power\_spectrum\_halofit.py}.}

\revisionblue{The validation plot is intentionally different from the main BT/PL response plot in Fig.~\ref{fig:power_spectra_ratio_z0}. The upper panels compare absolute measured spectra with reference nonlinear curves: the solid black curve is the PL reference, while the dashed blue curve is the BT\_soft reference obtained by applying the \(k_p=1\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\), \(m_s=1.5\) broken-power-law modification to the linear spectrum and evolving it with the same HMcode2020 setup described in Section~\ref{subsub:PS}. The lower panels show simulation/theory ratios. PL boxes are divided by the PL theory curve, and BT\_soft boxes are divided by the BT\_soft theory curve. We use these ratios to identify where neighboring box sizes agree and where finite-volume or mesh effects become visible.}

\revisionblue{The useful range is not set by a single hard cut. Large boxes anchor the low-\(k\) and intermediate-\(k\) regime because they contain more long-wavelength modes, whereas the \(5\) and \(2.5\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\) boxes are used only as high-\(k\) extensions. At the high-\(k\) end, the vertical markers show the mesh Nyquist scales, \(k_{\rm Ny}=\pi N_{\rm mesh}/L_{\rm box}\), for \(N_{\rm mesh}=1024\). Points approaching \(k_{\rm Ny}\), and especially points beyond the conservative \(0.5k_{\rm Ny}\) caution scale, are interpreted as diagnostic rather than precision measurements. The gray high-\(k\) region in Fig.~\ref{fig:power_spectrum_validation_theory} marks this caution regime for the fiducial \(25\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\) box.}
