\begin{abstract}
The primordial power spectrum is well constrained on large scales but remains uncertain on small scales. We use dark-matter-only cosmological \(N\)-body simulations initialized with the same random phases to determine how enhanced small-scale power changes nonlinear structure formation. We compare a standard power-law (PL) model with two broken-power-law blue-tilted (BT) models whose spectra steepen above pivot wavenumbers \(k_p=1\) and \(10\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\). We measure halo mass functions, \change{halo--matter bias,} assembly histories, radial density profiles, concentration--mass relations, and nonlinear matter power spectra. Enhanced small-scale power increases the abundance of low-mass halos at high redshift, lowers the bias of fixed-mass halos at high redshift, shifts halo assembly earlier, and increases the concentrations of low-mass halos. The \(k_p=1\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\) model also produces denser resolved inner halo profiles. These effects are stronger in the \(k_p=1\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\) model than in the \(k_p=10\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\) model and weaken toward higher halo masses and lower redshifts. The nonlinear matter power enhancement grows toward higher redshift and higher wavenumber. Published mass-function, concentration, and mass-assembly prescriptions reproduce the broad trends, but their agreement with the simulations varies with redshift and halo mass. These dark-matter-only results can be used in studies that include baryonic physics and observational selection.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction\label{intro}}
The standard \(\Lambda\)CDM model assumes cold dark matter, a cosmological constant, and a nearly scale-invariant primordial power spectrum, \(P_{\rm prim}(k)\propto k^{n_s}\) with \(n_s\simeq0.965\). This model agrees with measurements of the cosmic microwave background (CMB) \citep{Planck20CMB}, galaxy clustering \citep{Blanton17SDSSsurvey}, and the Lyman-\(\alpha\) forest \citep{Chabanier19LyalphaPS,Abolfathi18DR14}. However, the primordial power spectrum is less tightly constrained on scales much smaller than a megaparsec. Probes of these scales include CMB spectral distortions \citep{Chluba2012CMBSmallscalepowerspectrum}, satellite statistics and density profiles \citep{Wu25BT,Dekker25DwarfPPS,Esteban2024lumpyDM,Nadler2025enhanced}, strong-lensing flux-ratio and substructure measurements \citep{Gilman22SubgalacticPPS}, high-redshift galaxy counts \citep{Parashari23JWSTPPS,Hira24bluetilt}, ultra-compact minihalos \citep{Bringmann2026ultracompactminihaloonprimordialpowerspectrum}, and future 21-cm observations \citep{Munoz20SmallScale21cm}.

Previous studies have examined enhanced small-scale power using high-redshift cosmological simulations \citep{Hirano15BlueTiltedPPS,Inman23EnhancedSmallScaledarkage} and zoom-in simulations of Milky Way--mass environments \citep{Wu25BT,Nadler2025enhanced}. Few studies have compared the nonlinear evolution produced by different primordial spectra in the same cosmological volume.


Small-scale enhancements of the primordial power spectrum arise in several classes of early-universe models, including warm inflation \citep{Arya20WarmInflation}, axion models with curved field-space effects \citep{Sai26BlueTilt}, and multi-field or hybrid inflation models \citep{Linde94Hybridinflation,Wang24BTinflationtheory,Wang25HybridInflation}. A population of primordial black holes (PBHs) can also add small-scale matter power through an isocurvature component seeded by Poisson fluctuations \citep{Inman2019PBHBT}.

We use broken-power-law blue-tilted spectra to study how enhanced small-scale primordial power changes nonlinear structure formation. Here, ``blue-tilted'' (BT) refers specifically to a broken-power-law enhancement whose small-scale slope is steeper than the PL slope, following previous small-scale-power studies \citep[e.g.][]{Hirano15BlueTiltedPPS,Parashari23JWSTPPS,Hira24bluetilt,Tkac24BumpyPPS,deKruijf25DarkAges,Dekker25DwarfPPS,Wu25BT}.


\change{We follow three dark-matter-only cosmological \(N\)-body models with matched initial phases to \(z=0\), comparing a standard power-law spectrum with two BT spectra. Sections~\ref{sec:theory} and \ref{sec:numerical} describe the spectra and simulations. In Sec.~\ref{sec:results}, we measure the halo mass function (HMF), halo--matter bias, assembly histories, radial density profiles, concentrations, and the nonlinear matter power spectrum. For the mass functions, concentrations, and assembly histories, we evaluate published prescriptions using each model's linear spectrum and the published parameters. Section~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses the physical trends, observational implications, and numerical limits.}

\section{Power-Spectrum Models and Linear Matter Power}
\label{sec:theory}
\label{subsec:power_models}

\change{In the power-law (PL) model, the primordial power spectrum scales as \(P_{\rm prim}(k)\propto k^{n_s}\), where \(k\) is the comoving wavenumber and \(n_s\simeq0.965\) is the scalar spectral index \citep{Planck20CMB}.}

We model small-scale enhancements with blue-tilted (BT) primordial power-spectrum models \citep[e.g.][]{Hira24bluetilt,Dekker25DwarfPPS}. In these models, the spectrum follows the PL form up to \(k_p\) and becomes steeper above \(k_p\):
\begin{equation}
P_{\rm prim}^{\rm BT}(k)
\propto
\begin{cases}
k^{n_s}, & k \le k_p,\\[4pt]
k^{n_s}\left(k/k_p\right)^{m_s-n_s}, & k > k_p,
\end{cases}
\label{eq:bt_input_power}
\end{equation}
where \(k_p\) is the pivot wavenumber and \(m_s\) controls the high-\(k\) slope of the BT primordial power-spectrum model. This form reduces to the PL shape when \(m_s=n_s\), while \(m_s>n_s\) gives a blue-tilted enhancement above \(k_p\). The transfer function and normalization to the common \(\sigma_8\) target are applied when constructing the input linear matter spectrum at \(z_i=200\).

The corresponding linear matter power spectrum can be written schematically as
\begin{equation}
P_{\rm lin}(k,z) \propto P_{\rm prim}(k)\,T^2(k)\,D^2(z),
\label{eq:P_MD}
\end{equation}
where \(T(k)\) is the linear transfer function and \(D(z)\) is the linear growth factor.


We compare a standard power-law primordial power spectrum, denoted PL, with two broken-power-law models, \BTKPone{} and \BTKPten{}. Both BT models use \(m_s=1.5\). \BTKPone{} has \(k_p=1\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\), while \BTKPten{} has \(k_p=10\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\). The lower pivot in \BTKPone{} enhances power over a broader range of resolved wavenumbers than the higher pivot in \BTKPten{}.

To estimate the halo mass scale associated with each pivot, we follow \citet{Wu25BT} and define a comoving Lagrangian radius \(r_l=\pi/k_p\). The corresponding characteristic mass is
\begin{equation}
M_p=\frac{4\pi}{3}r_l^3\rho_{{\rm crit},0}\Omega_{{\rm m},0}.
\label{eq:pivot_mass}
\end{equation}
Here, \(\rho_{{\rm crit},0}\) is the present-day critical density and \(\Omega_{{\rm m},0}\) is the present-day matter density parameter. For our cosmology, \(k_p=1\) and \(10\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\) give \(M_p=1.69\times10^{13}\) and \(1.69\times10^{10}\,M_\odot\), respectively. The \BTKPone{} choice enhances linear power by \(\sim80\%\)--\(137\%\) at \(k=3\)--\(5\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\), exceeding current Lyman-\(\alpha\) forest constraints, and therefore serves as a strong-perturbation benchmark to isolate physical trends and stress-test analytic prescriptions. By contrast, \BTKPten{} restricts the enhancement to subgalactic scales closer to current observational frontiers, with an implicit high-\(k\) ultraviolet cutoff assumed beyond the simulated scale range.

\section{Numerical Methods}
\label{sec:numerical}

\subsection{Cosmological Parameters}
\label{subsec:cosmo_params}

All simulations use the following Planck 2018 background cosmology \citep{Planck20CMB}:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Present-day density parameters: total matter \(\Omega_{\rm m}=0.3153\), baryons \(\Omega_{\rm b}=0.0493\), and the cosmological constant \(\Omega_\Lambda=0.6847\).
    \item Hubble constant: \(H_0=67.36\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}\), corresponding to \(h\equiv H_0/(100\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}})=0.6736\).
    \item Scalar spectral index \(n_s = 0.9649\).
    \item The PL and BT initial conditions use the same target normalization, \(\sigma_8 = 0.8111\), where \(\sigma_8\) is the root-mean-square linear density contrast at \(z=0\) in spheres of radius \(8\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\).
\end{enumerate}
Table~\ref{tab:simulation_parameters} lists the cosmological and numerical parameters.


\subsection{Initial Conditions}\label{sec:IC}
\change{We generate the initial conditions at \(z_i=200\) with \textsc{Music2-MonofonIC} \citep{Hahn2020MUSIC2monofonIC,Michaux2021MUSIC2monofonIC} using third-order Lagrangian perturbation theory (3LPT). PL uses the Eisenstein \& Hu transfer function \citep{Eisenstein1998}. For each BT model, we modify the transfer function above \(k_p\). MonofonIC then applies a small model-dependent global normalization to reach the shared target \(\sigma_8=0.8111\). Below \(k_p\), the BT models retain the PL/Eisenstein shape. Over \(0.01\le k/(h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1})\le0.1\), the input linear power is about \(0.033\%\) lower than PL for \BTKPone{} and about \(9\times10^{-6}\%\) lower for \BTKPten{}.}

For each set of corresponding PL and BT runs, we keep the background cosmology, numerical settings, and MonofonIC random seed fixed. The main PL, \BTKPone{}, and \BTKPten{} \(25\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\), \(1024^3\) simulations therefore share the same initial random phases. Using the same phases reduces sample variance in the PL--BT comparisons.

For the power-spectrum measurements (\S\ref{subsub:PS}), we use phase-paired simulations following \citet{Pontzen2016Inverted}. Each pair comprises the original simulation and a counterpart with the initial linear density contrast reversed, \(\delta_{\rm inv}(\boldsymbol{x},z_i)=-\delta_{\rm orig}(\boldsymbol{x},z_i)\), equivalent to shifting every initial Fourier phase by \(\pi\) while retaining its amplitude. At each redshift, we take the arithmetic mean of their measured power spectra before forming the BT/PL ratios. Averaging cancels contributions that are odd under the reversal of the initial density field, reducing phase-dependent fluctuations in the power-spectrum comparison within the finite simulation volume. The halo measurements use the original simulations.


As an initial-condition consistency test, we compare the BT pivot scales with the particle-grid Nyquist wavenumber, \(k_{\rm Ny}=\pi N_{\rm side}/L_{\rm box}\), where \(N_{\rm side}\) is the number of particles along one dimension and \(L_{\rm box}\) is the comoving box length listed in Table~\ref{tab:simulation_parameters}. For the main \(25\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\), \(1024^3\) runs, \(k_{\rm Ny}\simeq128.7\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\). The two pivots, \(k_p=1\) and \(10\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\), are therefore well below the grid Nyquist scale. (For the auxiliary \(256\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\), \(1024^3\) runs used for large-scale halo bias, \(k_{\rm Ny}\simeq12.6\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\), which lies close to \(k_p=10\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\) so only large-scale modes below the pivot are resolved.) Plotting the input spectra in the standard cosmological Fourier convention matching Fig.~\ref{fig:power_spectra_ratio_z0} and defining the dimensionless linear power as \(\Delta^2(k,z)=k^3P_{\rm lin}(k,z)/(2\pi^2)\), the largest dimensionless linear power at \(k\le k_{\rm Ny}\) is \(\Delta^2(k,z_i)\simeq1.4\times10^{-2}\) (root-mean-square contrast \(\simeq0.12\)) for \BTKPone{}, so the enhanced modes remain linear under 3LPT initialization at \(z_i=200\).

\subsection{Halo Catalogs and Merger Trees}
\label{subsec:halo_catalogs}

\change{\textsc{SWIFT} identifies friends-of-friends (FOF) groups \citep{Davis1985FOF} using a linking length of \(0.2\) times the mean interparticle separation. The total dark matter mass of each group is \(M_{\rm FOF}\). We identify bound objects associated with these groups with \textsc{HBT-HERONS} \citep{Victor25HBTHERONS}, retaining candidates with at least 20 particles.}

\change{\textsc{SOAP} \citep{McGibbon2025SOAP} measures spherical-overdensity (SO) properties around the most-bound \textsc{HBT-HERONS} particle. The mass within \(R_{200}\), the radius enclosing a mean density of \(200\rho_{\rm ref}(z)\), is}
\begin{equation}
M_{200}=\frac{4\pi}{3}\,200\rho_{\rm ref}(z)R_{200}^3.
\end{equation}
\change{The critical density \(\rho_{\rm crit}(z)\) defines \(M_{200c}\) and \(R_{200c}\), while the cosmic mean matter density \(\rho_{\rm m}(z)\) defines \(M_{200m}\) and \(R_{200m}\). The corresponding \textsc{SOAP} catalog fields are \texttt{SO/200\_crit} and \texttt{SO/200\_mean}.}
%\Tk{You also use M200 below. Need to mention here.}

We describe halo structure with the Navarro--Frenk--White (NFW) density profile \citep{Navarro1996NFW,Navarro1997NFW},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:nfw_profile}
\rho_{\rm NFW}(r)=
\frac{\rho_s}{(r/r_s)(1+r/r_s)^2},
\end{equation}
where \(r_s\) is the scale radius and \(\rho_s\) is the characteristic density. The \(200c\) concentration is \(c_{200c}=R_{200c}/r_s\).

\change{For the radial profiles, we use persistent halo identifiers to match the catalog entries to the positions of their most-bound particles and bin the dark matter in spherical shells around those positions. The \textsc{SOAP} values of \(M_{200m}\) and \(R_{200m}\) set the mass selection and radial scale.}

The concentration analysis uses the \textsc{SOAP} \(c_{200c}\) value for the SO aperture centered on the most-bound-particle position supplied by \textsc{HBT-HERONS}. \textsc{SOAP} derives this concentration from the first radial moment of the mass distribution inside \(R_{200c}\), assuming an NFW profile. Particle radii smaller than the force-softening length are set to that length in this calculation.


\textsc{HBT-HERONS} constructs the merger trees by tracking bound particles between snapshots. Each object has a persistent \texttt{TrackId} and a link to its descendant. For the half-mass redshift and median FOF assembly histories, we follow the same \texttt{TrackId} backward through the snapshots. This sequence defines the main progenitor branch used in those measurements.

\subsection{Halo Mass Function\label{subsub:HMF}}

Using the FOF groups defined in Sec.~\ref{subsec:halo_catalogs}, we measure the FOF halo mass function at representative snapshots from \(z=19.41\) to \(z=0\) (Fig.~\ref{fig:mass_function}). Before binning, we correct each FOF mass for low-particle sampling bias using
\(M_{\rm corr}=M_{\rm FOF}[1-(M_{\rm FOF}/m_{\rm DM})^{-0.6}]\) \citep{Warren_2006,Lukic2007HMF}. We use logarithmic FOF mass bins of width \(\Delta\log_{10}M=1/6\) dex. The binned number density is \(dn/d\log_{10}M=N_{\rm h}/(V\,\Delta\log_{10}M)\), where \(V\) is the comoving simulation volume. For each non-empty bin, the plotted mass coordinate is the mean corrected halo mass,
\(\bar M=\sum_i M_{{\rm corr},i}/N_{\rm h}\), where \(N_{\rm h}\) is the number of halos in the bin. We use \(\bar M\), rather than the bin midpoint, as the plotted mass coordinate \citep{Lukic2007HMF}. The HMF error bars show Poisson counting uncertainties. We compute the BT/PL ratios only in bins populated in both simulations and propagate the Poisson uncertainties from both measurements.

We adopt the plotted catalog lower limit \(M_{\rm FOF}\ge10^8\,M_\odot\) listed in Table~\ref{tab:trusted_ranges}. Appendix~\ref{app:fof_hmf_resolution} tests this limit with lower-resolution PL runs and a larger PL box, showing sub-2\% agreement at \(z=0\) above the 50-particle scale of each comparison run and a \(7\)--\(19\%\) resolution suppression near 50 particles at \(z=8.52\); quoted high-redshift BT/PL enhancement ratios above \(\sim10^9\,M_\odot\) (\(\gtrsim500\) particles) are well above this threshold.


\change{We compare the FOF measurements with the Reed et al. (2007, hereafter Reed07) mass function \citep{Reed_2007} in Fig.~\ref{fig:mass_function}. For the SOAP \(M_{200c}\) measurements, we use the dark-matter-only (\texttt{hydro=False}) fit of Bocquet et al. (2016, hereafter B16) \citep{Bocquet2016} in Fig.~\ref{fig:m200c_bocquet16_hmf}. For both prescriptions, we compute the linear variance with a spherical top-hat filter applied to each model's linear matter power spectrum and use the published fit parameters.}

Within the resolved mass range, both blue-tilted models increase the low-mass FOF abundance relative to PL, with the largest enhancement in \BTKPone{}. The enhancement is largest at high redshift. For \BTKPone{}, the abundance ratio relative to PL is \(4.81\)--\(6.55\) over \(M_{\rm FOF}\simeq1.2\times10^9\)--\(1.2\times10^{10}\,M_\odot\) at \(z=8.52\). By \(z=0\), the ratio over the same mass range falls to \(1.42\)--\(1.48\). The BT/PL ratio decreases toward higher masses and lower redshifts.

    \label{fig:halo_bias}

Halo bias is a measure of the excess clustering of halos over that of dark matter. To obtain halo bias, we compute the halo--matter cross-spectrum \(P_{\rm hm}\) and the matter auto-spectrum \(P_{\rm mm}\) on a \(256^3\) mesh. We use nearest-grid-point assignment with its window correction and estimate the bias in each mass bin as
\begin{equation}
    b_{\rm hm} = \frac{\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}} P_{\rm hm}(\boldsymbol{k})}
                       {\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}} P_{\rm mm}(\boldsymbol{k})},
    \label{eq:halo_matter_bias}
\end{equation}
where the sums run over independent Fourier modes in the selected \(k\) interval. We use \(0.03\leq k/(h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1})\leq0.15\) for the \(256\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\) box and \(0.25\leq k/(h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1})\leq0.60\) for the \(25\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\) box. Each halo contains at least 100 dark matter particles, and each \(0.30\)-dex mass bin contains at least 200 halos. We plot the median mass in each bin. \change{Figure~\ref{fig:halo_bias} shows \textsc{HBT-HERONS} central halos \citep{Han2018HBTPlus,Victor25HBTHERONS}, using their \textsc{SOAP} mass \(M_{200m}\) enclosed at 200 times the mean matter density. Appendix~\ref{app:fof_bias} gives the corresponding FOF measurements.}

\change{We evaluate the \citet{Tinker2010HaloBias} relation at \(\nu=\delta_{\rm c}/\sigma(M,z)\), with \(\delta_{\rm c}=1.686\), using the \(200\bar\rho_{\rm m}\) calibration. We compute \(\sigma(M,z)\) from each model's linear matter power spectrum with a spherical top-hat window whose comoving radius encloses mass \(M\) at the present mean matter density. Under peak-background split, enhanced small-scale power increases \(\sigma(M,z)\) and lowers the peak height \(\nu\) at fixed halo mass, reducing halo rarity and lowering the bias most strongly in \BTKPone{} at high redshift. At \(z=8.52\), the median \BTKPone{}/PL bias ratio is \(0.54\) across the five common mass bins in the smaller box. In the \(256\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\) box over \(z=0\)--\(3.44\), \BTKPten{}/PL remains between \(0.988\) and \(1.009\) across all 23 paired bins, providing a large-scale consistency check because \(k_{\rm Ny}\simeq12.6\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\) lies near \(k_p=10\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\) and the resolved halo masses lie well above \(M_p(\BTKPten{})\). At \(z=0\), the \(256\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\) \textsc{SOAP} central measurements lie \(10\%\)--\(18\%\) below Tinker10 over \(5\times10^{11}\lesssim M_{200m}/M_\odot\lesssim4\times10^{13}\) (before turning slightly positive near \(2.5\times10^{14}\,M_\odot\)). This offset is not a particle-resolution artifact (high-mass \(25\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\) centrals with \(\sim10^5\) particles show the same negative offset), but reflects two physical and estimator effects: (i) \textsc{HBT-HERONS} classifies backsplash and outer-FOF halos near massive groups as satellites, excluding a strongly biased population (\(b\sim1.6\)) relative to distinct-SO halo finders on which Tinker10 was calibrated, and (ii) mode-count weighting in Eq.~\eqref{eq:halo_matter_bias} concentrates 73\% of the \(256\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\) modes in \(0.10\le k/(h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1})\le0.15\), where quasi-nonlinear scale dependence suppresses \(b_{\rm hm}(k)\) for low-\(\nu\) halos; similarly, in the \(25\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\) box, \(0.25\le k\le0.60\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\) spans only \(1.0\)--\(2.4\,k_{\rm f}\) (28 modes) and enters the nonlinear regime at \(z\le1.07\). Leave-one-mode-out jackknife uncertainties quantify within-box mode sampling and halo stochasticity under an independent-mode assumption; they omit cosmic realization variance, super-sample covariance, and residual scale dependence, and the uncertainty of the measured BT/PL ratios further depends on the covariance between the phase-matched runs. Comparisons at \(z=3.44\) and \(8.52\) extend beyond the Tinker calibration range of \(z\le2.5\).}

\subsection{Half-Mass Redshift\label{subsub:HMR}}
\change{The half-mass assembly redshift, \(z_{1/2}\), is the redshift at which a \(z=0\) halo first reaches half of its final FOF mass \(M_0\). We use the main progenitor branch defined in Sec.~\ref{subsec:halo_catalogs}. For each \(z=0\) FOF halo, we follow its persistent \textsc{HBT-HERONS} \texttt{TrackId} over 36 snapshots (0021--0056, extending above the \(z=19.41\) snapshot 0024 down to \(z=0\)) and record its Warren-corrected FOF mass. Pooling the final-mass bins with \(M_0\ge 10^9\,M_\odot\) separately for each model, we measure \(z_{1/2}\) for approximately \(98\%\) of the halos in each simulation. We determine \(z_{1/2}\) by linear interpolation between the first pair of consecutive snapshots in which the recorded FOF mass increases through \(M_0/2\). The same procedure is applied to PL, \BTKPone{}, and \BTKPten{}.}

We do not fill missing snapshots or force the recorded mass histories to increase monotonically. Temporary FOF bridging or splitting events can therefore remove a halo from the measurable sample or produce a temporary mass decrease.

Figure~\ref{fig:half_mass_redshift} compares our measurements with the relations of \citet{BoylanKolchin2009MSII} (BK09) and \citet{Hellwing16cocoproject} (COCO/Hellwing16). BK09 measured the mean half-mass formation redshift in the Millennium-II simulation using virial masses. COCO/Hellwing16 used the same fitting form with FOF masses. We evaluate both published relations at the mean final mass in each of our FOF mass bins. The external simulations use different background cosmologies, and BK09 also uses a different halo-mass definition.

For the 15 resolved PL bins spanning \(1.25\times10^9\le M_{\rm FOF}/M_\odot\le8.22\times10^{11}\), the mean absolute offset \(\langle|z_{1/2}^{\rm PL}-z_{1/2}^{\rm ref}|\rangle\) is \(0.053\) from BK09 and \(0.097\) from COCO/Hellwing16. The maximum absolute offsets are \(0.128\) and \(0.207\), respectively. Thus, the PL measurements follow both external relations to within about \(0.1\) in \(z_{1/2}\) on average.

\change{At low final masses, halos in both blue-tilted models form earlier than PL halos, with the largest shift in \BTKPone{}. Around \(M_0\simeq1.25\times10^9\,M_\odot\), the mean \(z_{1/2}\) is \(2.28\) for PL, \(4.24\) for \BTKPone{}, and \(2.61\) for \BTKPten{}. The corresponding shifts relative to PL are \(\Delta z_{1/2}=1.96\) for \BTKPone{} and \(0.33\) for \BTKPten{}.}

\change{Around \(M_0\simeq7.9\times10^9\,M_\odot\), the mean \(z_{1/2}\) values are \(1.95\), \(3.12\), and \(2.05\) for PL, \BTKPone{}, and \BTKPten{}, giving BT--PL shifts of \(1.17\) and \(0.10\). At \(M_0\simeq2.0\times10^{11}\,M_\odot\), the values are \(1.43\), \(1.78\), and \(1.39\), respectively. The model with the largest high-redshift HMF enhancement therefore also has the largest assembly-time shift, especially below \(\sim10^{10}\,M_\odot\).}

\subsection{Median Mass-Assembly Histories\label{subsub:MAH}}

Figure~\ref{fig:mass_assembly_history_correa} shows the median mass-assembly histories. We use \(M_0\) for the final FOF mass at the center of a selection window and \(M(z)\) for the corresponding main-progenitor FOF mass at redshift \(z\). We select halos at \(z=0\) in six Warren-corrected FOF mass windows with \(|\log_{10}[M_{\rm FOF}(z=0)/M_0]|\le0.1\). The windows are centered on \(M_0=10^9\), \(3\times10^9\), \(10^{10}\), \(3\times10^{10}\), \(10^{11}\), and \(3\times10^{11}\,M_\odot\). We follow the main progenitor of each selected halo through its persistent \texttt{TrackId} from \textsc{HBT-HERONS}. For visual clarity across the six mass windows, this comparison shows PL and \BTKPone{} (the intermediate \BTKPten{} model closely tracks PL at these masses).

%\Tk{The paragragh is unclear: what is cumulative-maximum filter? ``Missing detections'' sounds odd.}

At each snapshot, only halos with an identified main progenitor contribute to the median \(M_{\rm FOF}(z)\). We do not interpolate across snapshots in which a progenitor is not identified. The number of contributing halos therefore decreases with redshift. We restrict the quantitative comparison to \(M_0\ge10^9\,M_\odot\) and \(z\le6\); for PL at \(M_0=10^9\,M_\odot\), the detected fraction is \(90.3\%\) at \(z=5.50\) and drops to \(83.6\%\) at \(z=6.15\) (and \(60.5\%\) at \(z=7.65\)), whereas for \BTKPone{} it remains \(>99\%\) at \(z\ge5.5\). Consequently, conditional medians for PL near \(M_0=10^9\,M_\odot\) and \(z\sim6\) are biased upward by left-truncation survivorship near the 20-particle detection floor.

%\Tk{cite  (Bond et al. 1991; Lacey \& Cole 1993) for EPS}

\change{We compare these histories with the \citet{Correa2015MAHI} mass-assembly model. This model is based on the extended Press--Schechter (EPS) framework \citep{Bond1991ExcursionSet,LaceyCole1993MergerRates}.}

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:correa2015_mah}
M(z)=M_0(1+z)^{a f(M_0)}\exp[-f(M_0)z],
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:correa2015_fm0}
f(M_0)=\left[S(M_0/q)-S(M_0)\right]^{-1/2},
\end{equation}
where \(S(M)=\sigma^2(M)\) is the variance of the linear density contrast at \(z=0\), smoothed with a spherical top-hat filter. Thus \(\sigma(M)\) is the root-mean-square density contrast on mass scale \(M\). The filter radius encloses mass \(M\) at the present mean matter density. We compute this variance from the corresponding PL or \BTKPone{} linear matter power spectrum.

The published mass-dependent relations for \(\widetilde z_{\rm f}\) and \(q\) are
%\Tk{Need to say what are q; i.e. the numerical form of q.}
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\widetilde z_{\rm f}&=-0.0064\left(\log_{10}\frac{M_0}{M_\odot}\right)^2
+0.0237\log_{10}\frac{M_0}{M_\odot}+1.8837,\\
q&=4.137\,\widetilde z_{\rm f}^{-0.9476}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:correa_q_closure}
\end{equation}
\change{The Correa formation redshift satisfies \(M(\widetilde z_{\rm f})=M_0/q\), while the half-mass redshift satisfies \(M(z_{1/2})=M_0/2\).}

For the Correa-EPS curves, we use the Planck 2018 cosmology in Sec.~\ref{subsec:cosmo_params} for linear growth, the mean matter density that sets the smoothing scale, and the normalization of the PL and \BTKPone{} linear matter power spectra, and adopt the WMAP5 relations for \(\widetilde z_{\rm f}(M_0)\) and \(q(\widetilde z_{\rm f})\) from \citet{Correa2015MAHI}. For the model curves, we evaluate \(a=1+1.686\sqrt{2/\pi}\,[\mathrm{d}D/\mathrm{d}z]_{z=0}=0.292\) using the Planck 2018 growth factor, normalized to \(D(0)=1\), and compute \(S(M)\) from each simulation model's linear spectrum. The parameter \(q\) sets the progenitor mass scale \(M_0/q\) in the EPS calculation. Across the six mass bins in Fig.~\ref{fig:mass_assembly_history_correa}, \(q\) ranges from 2.68 to 3.20. At fixed \(M_0\), we use the same \(q\), \(\widetilde z_{\rm f}\), and \(a\) for PL and \BTKPone{}. The two predictions differ only because their linear matter power spectra give different values of \(S(M_0/q)-S(M_0)\). None of these parameters is fitted to our simulations. Note that fixing the WMAP5 \((\widetilde z_{\rm f},q)\) closure across models is an approximate fixed-parameter baseline in which \(M(\widetilde z_{\rm f})=M_0/q\) holds strictly for the WMAP5 calibration rather than for \BTKPone{}, and the comparison also pairs FOF simulation histories with an \(M_{200c}\)-calibrated prescription.


At fixed final FOF mass, both the simulations and the Correa-EPS calculation predict larger progenitor masses for \BTKPone{} than for PL. The difference increases with redshift, indicating earlier assembly in \BTKPone{}. With this fixed-parameter baseline, the Correa-EPS calculation lies closer to the simulated \BTKPone{} conditional medians than to PL, particularly near \(M_0=10^9\,M_\odot\) at \(z=6\) where PL suffers progenitor truncation. For \(10^9\le M_0/M_\odot\le10^{11}\), the maximum absolute difference \(\lvert M_{\rm sim}/M_{\rm Correa}-1\rvert\) at \(z=1,2,4,\) and \(6\) is \(46\%\) for PL and \(9\%\) for \BTKPone{}. At \(M_0=3\times10^{11}\,M_\odot\), the corresponding differences are \(23\%\) and \(16\%\), respectively.
%\Tk{Mention that We also expect the results from the EPS formula: In BT, we have higher sigma, so lower f(M0), so slower exponential delay.}
The same trend follows from Eqs.~(\ref{eq:correa2015_mah}) and (\ref{eq:correa2015_fm0}). The enhanced small-scale power in \BTKPone{} increases \(S(M_0/q)-S(M_0)\) and therefore reduces \(f(M_0)\). Since \(\ln[M(z)/M_0]=f(M_0)[a\ln(1+z)-z]\), a smaller \(f(M_0)\) causes \(M(z)/M_0\) to decrease more slowly with redshift.

\subsection{Halo Internal Structure}

We next test whether earlier halo growth also changes internal structure. We use direct radial density profiles and the concentration--mass relation.

\subsubsection{Radial Halo Density Profiles\label{subsub:DensityProfile}}

We stack the \(z=0\) profiles centered on the most-bound particle in four \(M_{200m}\) bins (Sec.~\ref{subsec:halo_catalogs}). Figure~\ref{fig:halo_density_analysis} compares median profiles measured directly from simulation particles with NFW profiles (Eq.~\ref{eq:nfw_profile}) computed using the Diemer \& Joyce (2019, hereafter D19) concentration--mass relation for the corresponding PL and BT models \citep{Diemer2019}. \change{For each reference curve, we evaluate D19 at the bin's target mass \(M_{200m}\) to obtain \(c_{200m}\), then use that concentration in the NFW profile.} The error bars show bootstrap 16th--84th percentile intervals for the median profile in each bin.

To identify the radial convergence limit, we follow \citet{Power2003} and calculate
\begin{equation}
\kappa(r)=\frac{N(<r)}{8\ln N(<r)}
\left[\frac{200\rho_{{\rm m},0}}{\overline{\rho}(<r)}\right]^{1/2},
\label{eq:power_kappa}
\end{equation}
where \(N(<r)\) is the enclosed dark matter particle count, \(\overline{\rho}(<r)\) is the mean enclosed density, and \(\rho_{{\rm m},0}\) is the present-day cosmic mean matter density. We define \(r_{\rm conv}\) as the smallest radius at which \(\kappa\ge0.6\) \citep{Power2003}. (Using \(200\rho_{{\rm m},0}\) rather than \(200\rho_{{\rm crit},0}\) in Eq.~\eqref{eq:power_kappa} makes \(\kappa\ge0.6\) more conservative than the original critical-density threshold by a factor of \(\Omega_{{\rm m},0}^{-1/2}\simeq1.78\).) At smaller radii, two-body relaxation can transfer energy to central particles and artificially flatten the measured density profile.

At the innermost radius outside the shaded region in each mass bin, the \BTKPone{}/PL density ratio ranges from \(1.32\) to \(1.52\). The ratio decreases toward \(R_{200m}\), falling below unity (\(\sim0.7\)--\(0.8\)) in the outer halo because fixed \(M_{200m}\) requires mass conservation within \(R_{200m}\). The \BTKPten{}/PL ratio ranges from \(0.93\) to \(1.08\) across the resolved radii of the four mass bins.

\subsubsection{Concentration-Mass Relation\label{subsub:concentration}}

We quantify halo structure using \(c_{200c}\), defined in Sec.~\ref{subsec:halo_catalogs}. We retain halos with at least 100 dark matter particles, corresponding to \(M_{200c}\simeq1.9\times10^8\,M_\odot\) in the main runs, and require finite positive concentrations with \(c_{200c}<500\). \change{The concentration sample includes both relaxed and unrelaxed halos.}

The stronger concentration enhancement in \BTKPone{} follows the same ordering as the HMF and assembly-history changes. Its lower pivot enhances a broader range of resolved modes than in \BTKPten{}. Halo concentration is linked to formation history and responds to changes in the linear matter spectrum \citep{Ludlow2016WDMConcentration,Brown2020HaloPPS}.

\subsection{Power Spectrum\label{subsub:PS}}

\change{We measure the nonlinear matter power spectrum from the full dark matter density field. For each snapshot, we assign particle masses to the nearest cells of a \(1024^3\) Cartesian mesh and compute the isotropically averaged spectrum with the \textsc{swiftsimio} power-spectrum utilities. We subtract the Poisson term \(P_{\rm shot}=L_{\rm box}^3/N_{\rm p}\), where \(N_{\rm p}\) is the total number of particles. The absolute spectra include smoothing by the nearest-grid-point window (amounting to \(\sim3\%\)--\(5\%\) near \(k\simeq20\)--\(32\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\)). A common window factor cancels in the ratio of individual BT and PL modes. The ratios of shell-averaged spectra can still contain residual aliasing. We use the phase-pair-averaged spectra \citep{Pontzen2016Inverted} (see \S\ref{sec:IC} for details) for the \(25\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\) runs and form the BT/PL ratios from these spectra.}

\change{Figure~\ref{fig:power_spectra_ratio_z0} compares PL, \BTKPone{}, and \BTKPten{} at four redshifts. The displayed interval is \(10k_{\rm f}\le k\le0.25k_{\rm Ny}\), or \(2.51\lesssim k/(h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1})\lesssim32.2\), where \(k_{\rm f}=2\pi/L_{\rm box}\) and \(k_{\rm Ny}=\pi N_{\rm mesh}/L_{\rm box}\), with \(N_{\rm mesh}=1024\). The BT and PL spectra in each ratio are measured within the same box. The finite box excludes long-wavelength modes and massive \(M\gtrsim10^{13}\,M_\odot\) 1-halo contributors at \(z=0\), which can shift the absolute power and relative low-redshift enhancement \citep{Takahashi2008FiniteModes}; Appendix~\ref{app:trusted_ranges} [Eq.~\eqref{eq:finite_mode_power_error}] summarizes the Gaussian finite-mode sampling diagnostic.}

\BTKPone{} has more small-scale power than PL, with a larger enhancement at higher redshift and higher \(k\). At \(z=0\), the power exceeds PL by approximately \(5\%\), \(11\%\), and \(16\%\) near \(k=5\), \(10\), and \(20\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\), respectively. At \(z=8.52\) and \(k\simeq20\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\), the enhancement is approximately \(321\%\) (lower panels of Fig.~\ref{fig:power_spectra_ratio_z0}). At this point, \(P_{\rm PL}\simeq3.10\times10^{-3}\) and \(P_{\rm BT}\simeq1.30\times10^{-2}\) in units of \(h^{-3}\,{\rm Mpc}^3\), with \(k^3P_{\rm PL}/(2\pi^2)\simeq1.28\). The subtracted Poisson term is about \(0.5\%\) of \(P_{\rm PL}\). In contrast, \BTKPten{} remains within \(\lesssim1\%\) of PL at \(z=0\)--\(3.44\) (where the \(-0.3\%\) to \(-0.9\%\) excursion at \(z=0\) reflects residual finite-box realization variance rather than the \(9\times10^{-6}\%\) linear normalization offset) and shows a modest high-\(k\) enhancement above \(k_p=10\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\) at \(z=8.52\).

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Physical Interpretation of the Main Trends}

Enhancing the primordial power spectrum above \(k_p\) increases the amplitude of the initial density fluctuations on those scales. Regions with larger overdensities collapse earlier. \change{At high redshift, the HMF enhancement is strongest below the characteristic mass \(M_p\) in Eq.~\eqref{eq:pivot_mass}.} At later times, the HMF difference decreases, while the assembly histories and central densities remain different from PL.

\BTKPone{} produces the larger change because its lower pivot enhances a broader range of resolved modes. The higher pivot of \BTKPten{} restricts the added power to smaller scales, so the differences from PL decrease more rapidly with halo mass. Under peak-background split, increasing \(\sigma(M,z)\) reduces the peak height \(\nu=\delta_{\rm c}/\sigma(M,z)\) at fixed mass, lowering halo rarity and reducing the halo--matter bias most strongly in \BTKPone{} at high redshift. Nonlinear growth and mergers further reduce the abundance contrast toward lower redshift.

%The matter power spectrum measures fluctuations in the full matter distribution, whereas the HMF and concentration describe halo abundance and internal structure.

\subsection{Relation to Previous Work}

Analytic and semi-analytic studies have examined halo abundances and structure for enhanced small-scale power. \citet{Parashari23JWSTPPS} used the Sheth--Tormen halo mass function to estimate high-redshift stellar mass densities. \citet{Esteban2024lumpyDM} modeled how enhanced small-scale power changes dwarf-halo concentrations and satellite kinematics. \citet{Dekker25DwarfPPS} combined a concentration model with existing \(\Lambda\)CDM merger trees and a galaxy-formation model. Our \(N\)-body simulations provide direct measurements of halo abundance and structure for comparison with published analytic models.

Other studies ran cosmological simulations with modified power spectra. \citet{Hira24bluetilt} studied halo abundance and high-redshift galaxies for blue-tilted spectra. \citet{Tkac24BumpyPPS} measured halo mass functions for spectra with a Gaussian bump. These studies focused mainly on halo abundance at high redshift. We follow halos to \(z=0\) to determine whether the early abundance changes persist in their assembly histories and internal structure.

\citet{Wu25BT} and \citet{Nadler2025enhanced} used zoom-in simulations of Milky Way--mass halos to study their subhalos. Our periodic-box simulations measure halo statistics as functions of mass across the simulation volume.


\subsection{Connection to Observations}

We found that enhanced small-scale primordial power increases the abundance of low-mass halos at high redshift and raises their concentrations. These changes could affect the abundance of high-redshift galaxies observed with the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST), although a quantitative comparison requires a galaxy-formation model.

Dwarf-galaxy dynamics and strong-lensing perturbers provide complementary tests \citep{Dekker25DwarfPPS,Gilman22SubgalacticPPS}. Strong-lensing analyses have identified unusually dense low-mass perturbers in SDSS J0946+1006 and JVAS B1938+666 \citep{Minor2021HighConcentration,Tajalli2025DensePerturbers}. A separate study (Wu, Chan, Lange, \& Huang, submitted) examines whether BT models can account for these systems. Finally, the halo abundances and assembly histories measured here can be incorporated into source-population and reionization models to predict 21-cm signals \citep{Munoz20SmallScale21cm,deKruijf25DarkAges,Zhu26SKAPPS}.


\subsection{Numerical and Modeling Limitations}

The main limitations are the dark-matter-only setup, limited sampling of initial random phases, finite mass resolution, finite force resolution, and finite box size.

First, the simulations are collisionless dark-matter-only runs. This setup isolates the gravitational effect of the modified primordial power-spectrum model, but it omits gas cooling, star formation, stellar and active galactic nucleus (AGN) feedback, and reionization. These processes can change halo density profiles, subhalo survival, and the connection between halos and galaxies.


Second, the main halo comparisons use one initial realization per model. Using the same initial random phases reduces the uncertainty in PL--BT ratios, but it does not measure the variation among independent realizations. The HMF error bars include only Poisson counting uncertainties, while the concentration error bars show halo-to-halo scatter. Neither includes cosmic variance. The power spectra use phase-pair averages, which reduce phase-dependent fluctuations but do not establish convergence with box size.

Third, finite resolution and box size set the ranges summarized in Table~\ref{tab:trusted_ranges}. The main FOF mass-function results are restricted to \(M_{\rm FOF}\ge10^8\,M_\odot\), and the assembly-history comparisons use halos with \(M_0\ge10^9\,M_\odot\) and \(z\le6\). The density profiles are not reliable for \(r<r_{\rm conv}\). The concentration-model comparisons are restricted to masses above the 1000-particle scale, \(M_{200c}=1.89\times10^9\,M_\odot\). The power-spectrum interval used for quantitative interpretation is \(2.51\le k/(h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1})\le32.2\) for the \(25\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\) box. Appendix~\ref{app:fof_hmf_resolution} separately tests the effects of mass resolution and box volume on the FOF mass function.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusion}


We used dark-matter-only cosmological \(N\)-body simulations to quantify how enhanced small-scale primordial power affects nonlinear structure formation. We compared a standard power-law (PL) model with two broken-power-law blue-tilted (BT) models defined by Eq.~\eqref{eq:bt_input_power}. Both BT models have an enhanced power spectrum index \(m_s=1.5\) above a pivot wavenumber \(k_p\): \BTKPone{} has \(k_p=1\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\), and \BTKPten{} has \(k_p=10\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\).


The main conclusions are as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \change{Enhanced small-scale primordial power increases halo abundance most strongly at low mass and high redshift (Sec.~\ref{subsub:HMF}). At a friends-of-friends halo mass \(M_{\rm FOF}\simeq1.21\times10^9\,M_\odot\), the \BTKPone{}/PL ratio decreases from \(4.81\) at \(z=8.52\) to \(1.42\) at \(z=0\), while \BTKPten{} produces a smaller change. The \(M_{200c}\) spherical-overdensity mass function shows the same trend (Fig.~\ref{fig:m200c_bocquet16_hmf}).}

    The median differences between the simulations and analytic models of Reed et al. (2007) \citep{Reed_2007} and Bocquet et al. (2016) \citep{Bocquet2016} are below \(8\%\) at \(z=0\) and \(3.44\) and reach \(32\%\) at \(z=8.52\), while diverging substantially in the extrapolated \(z=10.52\)--\(19.41\) regime (up to \(+107\%\) above Reed07 and down to \(-67\%\) below B16 in PL).

    \item \change{At \(z=8.52\) in the \(25\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\) box, the median \BTKPone{}/PL halo--matter bias ratio across the five common mass bins is \(0.54\) for SOAP \(M_{200m}\) centrals, whereas in the \(256\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\) box at \(z=0\)--\(3.44\), \BTKPten{}/PL remains \(0.988\)--\(1.009\) across all 23 paired bins (Sec.~\ref{subsec:halo_bias}).}

    \item \change{Enhanced small-scale primordial power shifts halo assembly earlier (Sec.~\ref{subsub:HMR}). For halos with a present-day halo mass \(M_0\simeq1.25\times10^9\,M_\odot\), the half-mass assembly redshift \(z_{1/2}\) increases from \(2.28\) in PL to \(4.24\) in \BTKPone{} and \(2.61\) in \BTKPten{}. The full mass-assembly histories also show earlier assembly in \BTKPone{} than in PL (Sec.~\ref{subsub:MAH}).}

    \change{Across \(10^9\le M_0/M_\odot\le10^{11}\), the fixed-parameter Correa et al. (2015) curves \citep{Correa2015MAHI} agree with the conditional median histories at \(z=1,2,4,\) and \(6\) to within \(46\%\) for PL (affected at \(M_0=10^9\,M_\odot, z\sim6\) by progenitor detection truncation) and \(9\%\) for \BTKPone{}, and to within \(23\%\) and \(16\%\), respectively, at \(M_0=3\times10^{11}\,M_\odot\).}

    \item The \BTKPone{} run has denser resolved inner profiles, while the \BTKPten{}/PL density ratio stays between \(0.93\) and \(1.08\) outside the shaded convergence limits (Sec.~\ref{subsub:DensityProfile}). Both blue-tilted runs have higher concentrations at low halo mass (Sec.~\ref{subsub:concentration}). At \(z=0\), the median \(c_{200c}\) in the first wholly resolved \(10^{9.5}\)--\(10^{10}\,M_\odot\) interval (\(>1000\) particles and \(r_s>\epsilon_{\rm Pl}\)) exceeds PL by factors of \(2.71\) for \BTKPone{} and \(1.19\) for \BTKPten{} (and by \(3.20\) and \(1.35\) over the lower \(10^9\)--\(10^{9.5}\,M_\odot\) interval where \(r_s\lesssim\epsilon_{\rm Pl}\) in \BTKPone{}).


    For the \(0.25\)-dex mass bins lying wholly above \(M_{200c}=1.89\times10^9\,M_\odot\) and containing at least 20 halos, the Diemer \& Joyce (2019) concentration-mass relation agrees with the simulation medians within \(20\%\) for PL, \BTKPone{}, and \BTKPten{} at all four redshifts (Sec.~\ref{subsub:concentration}) \citep{Diemer2019}.


    \item \change{\BTKPone{} increases the nonlinear matter power at high \(k\), with a larger enhancement at higher redshift. At \(k\simeq20\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\), \BTKPone{} has approximately 16\% and 321\% more power than PL at \(z=0\) and \(z=8.52\), respectively. The \BTKPten{} power spectrum remains close to PL (Sec.~\ref{subsub:PS}; Fig.~\ref{fig:power_spectra_ratio_z0}).}
\end{enumerate}


Future work should test other enhanced primordial spectra, including localized Gaussian bumps \citep{Tkac24BumpyPPS} and smooth blue tilts \citep{2026arXiv260522161K}, against the same analytic models. Simulations with independent random phases and different box sizes are needed to quantify variation among realizations and finite-volume effects. Connecting these models to observations will also require semi-analytic or hydrodynamic galaxy-formation models. Dwarf galaxies, strong-lensing systems (Wu, Chan, Lange, \& Huang, submitted), high-redshift galaxy counts, and 21-cm observations (Yang, Chan, Huang, Theuns, Richings, in prep.) can probe the primordial power spectrum on subgalactic scales.

We estimate the Gaussian finite-mode uncertainty using \citeauthor{Takahashi2008FiniteModes}'s mode-count approximation \citep{Takahashi2008FiniteModes},
\begin{equation}
N_{\rm mode}(k)=\frac{L_{\rm box}^{3}k^2\Delta k}{2\pi^2},
\qquad
\frac{\sigma_P}{P}=\left(\frac{2}{N_{\rm mode}N_{\rm real}}\right)^{1/2}.
\label{eq:finite_mode_power_error}
\end{equation}
Here, \(\Delta k\) is the bin width, \(N_{\rm mode}\) is the estimated number of Fourier modes, and \(N_{\rm real}\) is the number of independent realizations. For an individual realization, \(N_{\rm real}=1\). The quantity \(\sigma_P/P\) is the fractional \(1\sigma\) uncertainty. This continuum approximation is least accurate near the fundamental mode \(k_{\rm f}=2\pi/L_{\rm box}\). It is a sampling diagnostic for an absolute spectrum; the correlated PL and BT measurements require a covariance estimate to assign uncertainties to BT/PL.

\section{FOF Mass-Function Resolution and Volume Check}
\label{app:fof_hmf_resolution}

This appendix tests the FOF halo-mass-function range against changes in mass resolution and box volume. Figure~\ref{fig:fof_hmf_resolution} compares four PL runs at \(z=0\) and \(z=8.52\). The fiducial run has \(L_{\rm box}=25\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\) and \(1024^3\) particles. The same-volume resolution tests use \(512^3\) and \(256^3\) particles, while the volume test uses a \(50\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\), \(512^3\) run. The \(25\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\), \(256^3\) and \(50\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\), \(512^3\) runs have the same particle mass, allowing a volume comparison at fixed mass resolution.

We use the same logarithmic bins, Poisson uncertainties, and Warren correction as in the main FOF mass-function analysis. \change{The Reed07 reference in Sec.~\ref{subsub:HMF} uses the linear variance computed with a spherical top-hat filter.} Vertical dotted lines mark the 50-particle mass for each resolution, and the lower panels show ratios to PL-25-\(1024^3\). The reduced data and plotting script are included in the accompanying GitHub and Zenodo public-data release.

At \(z=0\), the same-volume tests agree well above each comparison run's 50-particle mass. Over the common bins with at least 20 objects in both catalogs, PL-25-\(512^3\) has a median ratio of \(0.986\) to PL-25-\(1024^3\) over \(10^{8.92}\)--\(10^{12.25}\,M_\odot\). PL-25-\(256^3\) gives \(0.987\) over \(10^{9.92}\)--\(10^{12.25}\,M_\odot\). The corresponding 16th--84th percentile ranges are \(0.963\)--\(1.029\) and \(0.952\)--\(1.070\).

The \(50\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\), \(512^3\) volume test gives a median ratio of \(0.945\) over \(10^{9.92}\)--\(10^{12.25}\,M_\odot\), with a 16th--84th percentile range of \(0.917\)--\(1.051\). The wider interval reflects finite-volume effects and differences in the random phases.

At \(z=8.52\), the same-volume \(512^3\) run remains close to the fiducial measurement above its 50-particle mass. Its median ratio is \(0.935\) over \(10^{8.92}\)--\(10^{10.58}\,M_\odot\), with a 16th--84th percentile range of \(0.900\)--\(0.979\).

The \(256^3\) same-volume run and the \(50\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\), \(512^3\) volume test cover only five common bins above their \(6.0\times10^9\,M_\odot\) 50-particle scale. Their median ratios are \(0.813\) and \(0.871\), respectively. The \(10^8\,M_\odot\) catalog threshold corresponds to about 50 particles in the fiducial \(1024^3\) run. The cross-resolution comparisons cover the higher mass intervals quoted above. Lower-resolution boxes lose high-redshift low-mass halos when their 50-particle limits enter the plotted mass range.

\section{Alternative Concentration Prescriptions}
\label{app:concentration_alternative_models}

\change{Figures~\ref{fig:concentration_comparison_i21_fit} and \ref{fig:concentration_comparison_l16} compare our relaxed halo samples with the I21 relaxed NFW-fit and L16 predictions, respectively. For the L16 selection, we require a normalized offset below \(0.1\) between the center of mass inside \(R_{200c}\) and the most-bound particle. We also require that the lookback time to half-mass assembly satisfy \(t_{50}\ge1.25\,t_{\rm cross}\), where \(t_{\rm cross}=2R_{200c}/V_{200c}\) and \(V_{200c}=(GM_{200c}/R_{200c})^{1/2}\) \citep{Ludlow2016WDMConcentration}. We measure \(t_{50}\) from the first half-mass crossing along the most massive progenitor branch in \(M_{200c}\).}

\change{For the I21 selection we apply \(X_{\rm off}<0.07\), Peebles spin \(\lambda<0.07\), and \(2K/|W|<1.5\) \citep{Ishiyama2021}. We reconstruct virial apertures from the simulation particles around the SOAP halo centers and use a spherical-shell estimate for the potential energy. We estimate the membership of the self-bound halo by removing particles whose kinetic energy exceeds their binding energy in the spherical potential.}

\change{Of the 96 bins above the 1000-particle scale with at least 20 L16-selected halos, 95 also contain at least 20 I21-selected halos. The remaining \BTKPone{} bin at \(z=0\) and \(\log_{10}(M_{200c}/M_\odot)=12.375\) contains 18 I21-selected halos. On the 95 common bins, the median absolute fractional differences for D19, I21 relaxed, and L16 are \(5.16\%\), \(5.74\%\), and \(4.86\%\), respectively, for the L16 selection. They are \(5.31\%\), \(4.90\%\), and \(6.84\%\) for the I21 selection. The I21 relaxed fit also gives the smallest median difference when all particles in the virial aperture enter the relaxation calculation.}

\change{For the full halo sample at \(z=0\), the median of the binned ratios to the I21 all-halo NFW fit over \(10^9\le M_{200c}/M_\odot<10^{9.5}\) is \(0.96\) for PL, \(1.23\) for \BTKPone{}, and \(1.12\) for \BTKPten{}.}
