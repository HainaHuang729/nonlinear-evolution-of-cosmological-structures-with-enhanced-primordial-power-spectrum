\begin{abstract}
{\color{blue}Motivated by the weak observational constraints on the small-scale initial matter spectrum, we use high-resolution dark-matter-only cosmological $N$-body simulations to quantify how enhanced small-scale power in the initial-condition linear matter spectrum is mapped into nonlinear structure formation. We compare a standard power-law baseline with two blue-tilted benchmark models whose initial matter power spectra at \(z_i=200\) are enhanced above a pivot wavenumber. In practice, this enhancement is implemented by modifying the transfer function used to generate the initial conditions while keeping the large-scale normalization and background cosmology fixed. For each model, we measure the halo mass function, half-mass assembly redshift, concentration-based structural diagnostics, and the nonlinear matter power spectrum. In the BT\_soft case, whose enhancement begins at $k_p=1\,h\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}$, the FOF halo abundance exceeds PL by factors of $4.81$ and $6.55$ at $z=8.52$ for $M\simeq1.21\times10^9$ and $1.21\times10^{10}\,M_\odot$, respectively, while the corresponding $z=0$ ratios are $1.42$ and $1.47$ over the same mass range. Halo assembly is also shifted earlier: near $M_{z=0}\simeq1.25\times10^9\,M_\odot$, the mean $z_{1/2}$ changes from $2.28$ in PL to $4.24$ in BT\_soft. At $z=0$, the median concentration in the $10^9$--$10^{9.5}\,M_\odot$ bin is higher than PL by factors of $3.20$ and $1.35$ for the two blue-tilted models. The nonlinear matter power spectrum shows a more modest but coherent high-$k$ response, with BT\_soft reaching $P_{\rm BT}/P_{\rm PL}=1.17$ at $k=20\,h\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}$ in the fiducial box. These results establish a controlled simulation-level benchmark for future forward modeling of observational probes of small-scale matter power, including JWST high-redshift galaxy counts, local dwarf-galaxy structure, strong-lensing constraints on sub-galactic structure, and future 21 cm probes of the small-scale matter power spectrum.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction\label{intro}}
{\color{blue}The standard cosmological model, combining a nearly scale-invariant primordial spectrum with the $\Lambda$ Cold Dark Matter ($\Lambda$CDM) paradigm, provides an accurate description of the Universe on large scales. Measurements of the cosmic microwave background, galaxy clustering, and the Lyman-$\alpha$ forest are consistent with a red-tilted spectrum over the range of scales that is directly constrained by current observations \citep{Planck20CMB,Blanton17SDSSsurvey,Chabanier19LyalphaPS}. The situation is less settled on smaller scales, where constraints on the initial matter power spectrum are much weaker. The abundance and internal structure of low-mass halos remain central to long-standing small-scale questions such as the missing-satellites, core-cusp, and too-big-to-fail problems \citep{Bullock17smallscalechallenges,Sales22challenges}. At the same time, early JWST measurements have revealed unexpectedly abundant and potentially massive galaxy candidates at $z\gtrsim 8$--$10$ \citep{Labbe23MassiveGalaxies,Donnan23UVLF}. These observations have motivated renewed interest in enhanced small-scale initial matter power, although the mapping from halo abundance to observed galaxies remains degenerate with star-formation efficiency, feedback, dust, and selection effects \citep{BoylanKolchin23JWSTStress,Lovell23JWSTExtreme}.}

{\color{blue}One economical way to test this possibility is to modify the input matter power only on scales that are not already tightly constrained by CMB and large-scale structure data. We use a blue-tilted (BT) input-matter spectrum as a controlled benchmark inspired by the broader small-scale-power literature, including blue-tilted and localized-feature examples \citep[e.g.][]{Hirano15BlueTiltedPPS,Parashari23JWSTPPS,Hira24bluetilt,Tkac24BumpyPPS,deKruijf25DarkAges,Dekker25DwarfPPS,Wu25BT}. In this paper, the BT parameters should be read as a phenomenological description of the linear matter spectrum used to generate the initial conditions, not as a complete inflationary or curvature-spectrum model. The large-scale shape follows the usual red-tilted form with $n_s\simeq0.965$, but the initial-condition matter power spectrum at \(z_i=200\) is enhanced above a pivot wavenumber $k_p$ through a transfer-function modification. The pivot therefore separates the well-measured large scales from the more weakly constrained small scales, preserving the successful large-scale predictions of $\Lambda$CDM while directly changing the abundance and collapse history of small halos.}
{\color{blue}Several recent studies have addressed different observational aspects of this question. Enhanced or modified small-scale spectra have been invoked to interpret high-redshift galaxy abundances in light of JWST \citep{Parashari23JWSTPPS,Hira24bluetilt}. Related BT initial conditions have also been explored with zoom-in simulations of Milky Way--mass environments \citep{Wu25BT}, while dwarf-galaxy densities, satellite statistics, sub-galactic strong-lensing probes, and 21 cm observations provide complementary tests of small-scale matter power \citep{Kim18nomissingsatellites,Dekker25DwarfPPS,Gilman22SubgalacticPPS,Munoz20SmallScale21cm,deKruijf25DarkAges,Zhu26SKAPPS}. These works show that the same physical idea---changing the small-scale initial matter power---can be tested through several distinct observables, but they also highlight a key difficulty: galaxy formation, reionization, baryonic physics, and observational selection effects can be degenerate with the initial-power signal.}

{\color{blue}The purpose of this paper is to isolate the gravitational, nonlinear response to enhanced small-scale power in the initial-condition matter spectrum before adding those observation-specific complications. We therefore perform controlled dark-matter-only cosmological $N$-body simulations in periodic boxes, comparing a standard power-law input matter spectrum with two transfer-function-modified BT benchmark models. This approach is complementary to zoom-in studies of selected host environments \citep{Wu25BT}: here we measure population-level halo and field diagnostics across a periodic volume. Our diagnostics include the halo mass function, half-mass assembly redshift, concentration-based structural diagnostics, and the nonlinear matter power spectrum. These statistics probe different stages of the same physical process: enhanced small-scale fluctuations collapse earlier, populate the low-mass halo regime more efficiently, and are redistributed by nonlinear evolution into halo internal structure and high-$k$ clustering power.}


\section{Theoretical Overview}
\label{sec:theory}

{\color{blue}This study investigates the imprints of a transfer-function-modified initial-condition matter power spectrum on the late-time distribution and internal structure of dark matter halos. The theoretical overview defines the input matter spectra used to generate the simulations and summarizes how their small-scale differences propagate into nonlinear structure formation.}

\subsection{From Input Matter Power to Cosmic Structure}
{\color{blue}The simulation inputs are specified by the linear matter power spectrum at the initial redshift. In a complete early-universe model, this spectrum would be obtained by combining a primordial curvature spectrum with a transfer function and growth factor. Here, however, we do not introduce an independently normalized curvature-spectrum model. Instead, we describe the relevant small-scale modification directly through the shape of the initial-condition linear matter power spectrum used by the initial-condition generator. Up to a common normalization fixed by the shared background cosmology, the input linear spectrum can be written schematically as \(P_{\mathrm{lin}}(k,a)\propto D^2(a)T^2(k)k^{n_s}\), where \(D(a)\) is the growth factor and \(T(k)\) is the transfer function used by the initial-condition calculation. In the PL case, \(T(k)\) is the Eisenstein--Hu transfer function. In the BT cases, the code modifies the transfer function above a pivot scale \(k_p\), producing the target small-scale enhancement in the resulting input matter power spectrum. The subsequent gravitational collapse and nonlinear evolution of overdensities lead to the formation of a hierarchical network of dark matter halos, whose internal structure and abundance serve as fundamental probes of the underlying cosmology.}

\subsection{\textcolor{blue}{The Blue-Tilted Input-Power Model}}
{\color{blue}We consider a phenomenological blue-tilted (BT) input-power model, specified by the target enhancement of the linear matter power spectrum relative to PL:}
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:bt_input_power}
    \frac{P_{\rm lin}^{\rm BT}(k)}{P_{\rm lin}^{\rm PL}(k)}
    =
    \begin{cases}
        1, & \left(\text{for } k < k_p\right),\\
        \left(\frac{k}{k_p}\right)^{m_s-n_s}, & \left(\text{for } k > k_p\right),
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}
{\color{blue}where $k_p$ is a pivot wavenumber and $m_s$ controls the target high-\(k\) slope of the resulting input matter spectrum. Operationally, this target is implemented by modifying the transfer function used in the power-spectrum calculation, so that \(P_{\rm lin}\propto T^2(k)k^{n_s}\) acquires the enhancement above \(k_p\). Equivalently, the transfer-function ratio corresponding to the target matter-power ratio is \(T_{\rm BT}(k)/T_{\rm PL}(k)=1\) for \(k<k_p\) and \(T_{\rm BT}(k)/T_{\rm PL}(k)=(k/k_p)^{(m_s-n_s)/2}\) for \(k>k_p\). In this work the large-scale normalization is kept fixed; only the effective small-scale shape above $k_p$ is varied, so the model description can be specified by $k_p$ and $m_s$ for the present comparison. The BT spectra are therefore controlled transfer-function modifications of the initial-condition matter spectrum. The parameters $k_p$ and $m_s$ should not be interpreted as a complete inflationary parameterization or as independently constrained curvature-spectrum parameters in this paper. By construction, the model leaves the large-scale input spectrum unchanged while enhancing power on smaller scales ($k > k_p$) when $m_s > n_s$ (blue tilt).}

\subsection{Initial Conditions}
{\color{blue}We generate the initial conditions with \textsc{Music2-MonofonIC}, adopting the Eisenstein \& Hu transfer function \citep{Eisenstein1998} as the PL baseline. For the BT models, the implementation modifies the transfer function above \(k_p\), rather than introducing a separate explicitly normalized curvature-spectrum amplitude, so that the resulting input linear matter power spectrum at \(z_i=200\) follows the broken-power-law enhancement described in Section~\ref{sec:theory}. The large-scale modes remain unchanged while the power at $k>k_p$ is enhanced. For each fixed box size and particle number, the background cosmology and numerical settings are kept identical across the compared models. The main PL, BT\_soft, and BT\_deep \(25\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\), \(1024^3\) runs use the same random seed in the initial-condition generation, so the compared simulations share the same initial phases. The intended controlled variable is therefore the input linear matter power-spectrum shape at \(z_i=200\).}

{\color{blue}As a basic consistency check on the initial conditions, we also evaluate the dimensionless input power \(\Delta^2(k,z_i)=k^3P_{\rm lin}(k,z_i)/(2\pi^2)\). For the main \(25\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\), \(1024^3\) runs, the particle Nyquist scale is \(k_{\rm Ny}\simeq128.7\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\). Over \(k\le k_{\rm Ny}\), the largest value among the BT input spectra is \(\Delta^2\simeq5.8\times10^{-5}\) for BT\_soft, so the enhanced modes are still deeply in the linear regime at \(z_i=200\).}

\subsection{Simulation Code}
{\color{blue}We evolve the initial conditions with the cosmological $N$-body code \textsc{SWIFT} \citep{Schaller2024SWIFT}. In the dark-matter-only, collisionless mode used here, \textsc{SWIFT}'s gravity solver uses a fast-multipole method, optionally coupled to a Fourier-space Particle-Mesh solver for periodic volumes. The PL and BT simulations use the same numerical setup listed in Table~\ref{tab:simulation_parameters}; the intended controlled variable is therefore the input linear matter power spectrum at \(z_i=200\), not the dynamical evolution algorithm.}
\subsubsection{Cosmological parameters}
\label{subsub:cosmo_params}

For all the cosmological simulations in this paper,

\begin{enumerate}

\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Halo Finder and Halo Property Post-processor}

{\color{blue}Halos and merger trees are identified with \textsc{HBT+}, a particle-tracking halo finder developed by Forouhar Moreno et al. (in preparation). We require a minimum of 20 particles for an object to be retained as a halo candidate, and define the halo center as the position of the most bound particle. For the main $25\,h^{-1}\mathrm{Mpc}$, $1024^3$ runs, the snapshot mass table gives \(m_{\rm DM}=1.89\times10^6\,M_\odot=1.27\times10^6\,h^{-1}M_\odot\). The nominal 20-particle halo threshold is therefore \(M\simeq3.8\times10^7\,M_\odot\), while a 100-particle structural cut corresponds to \(M\simeq1.9\times10^8\,M_\odot\). Based on the \textsc{HBT+} catalogs, we then use \textsc{SOAP} (Spherical Overdensity and Aperture Processor) to compute spherical-overdensity properties such as $M_{200c}$, $R_{200c}$, and the structural quantities used below. This separation between halo identification and property measurement retains the tracking and merger-tree information used for the half-mass assembly analysis while adopting consistent spherical-overdensity quantities for the profile and concentration analyses.}

{\color{blue}Different statistics use different halo mass definitions. For the halo mass function, we use Friends-of-Friends (FOF) halo masses and compare them to analytic predictions evaluated with the same FOF mass definition. The half-mass assembly diagnostic uses FOF-based same-TrackId mass histories from the HBT+ catalogs, so its $z=0$ mass bins should be interpreted as $M_{\rm FOF}$ bins. For the internal-structure diagnostics, we use spherical-overdensity quantities measured by \textsc{SOAP}: the NFW-equivalent density-profile diagnostic uses \texttt{SO/200\_mean} masses, radii, and concentrations, while the concentration--mass relation uses $M_{200c}$, $R_{200c}$, and $c_{200c}$ from \texttt{SO/200\_crit}. The nonlinear matter power spectrum is a particle-field statistic and does not require a halo mass definition. We therefore avoid directly mixing FOF, $M_{200m}$, and $M_{200c}$ masses when interpreting a single statistic, and the relevant captions specify the mass definition used.}


\subsection{Simulation Suite and Terminology\label{subsec:Terminology}}

{\color{blue}The simulation suite is summarized in Table~\ref{tab:simulation_parameters}. Unless stated otherwise, the main results use three matched $25\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}$, $1024^3$ runs: PL, BT\_soft, and BT\_deep. PL denotes the standard power-law input matter spectrum; BT\_soft denotes the blue-tilted model with $k_p=1\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}$ and $m_s=1.5$; and BT\_deep denotes the blue-tilted model with $k_p=10\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}$ and $m_s=1.5$. Additional small-box BT runs and PL convergence/volume runs are numerical checks and are used only when explicitly stated.}

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Simulation Maps}

{\color{blue}The projected dark matter maps provide a first visual check of the simulations. The largest structures and the most massive collapsed regions are broadly similar across PL, BT\_soft, and BT\_deep, while the blue-tilted maps show visibly more small-scale contrast. We use these maps only as qualitative context. The present analysis does not measure a subhalo mass function, radial subhalo distribution, or subhalo survival statistic, so the maps are not used as quantitative evidence for subhalo abundance or concentration.}

    \caption{{\color{blue}Projected dark matter density fields for the PL, BT\_soft, and BT\_deep simulations at $z=0$. \subsection{Halo Mass Function\label{subsub:HMF}}

{\color{blue}Figure~\ref{fig:mass_function} shows the FOF halo mass functions at representative snapshots from $z=8.52$ to $z=0$. The plotted simulation points are computed from the FOF catalogs in logarithmic mass bins, with Poisson counting uncertainties and the Warren et al. correction, $N_{\rm corr}=N(1-N^{-0.6})$, applied at the low-particle end \citep{Warren_2006}. For the fiducial $25\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}$, $1024^3$ runs, the particle mass is \(1.89\times10^6\,M_\odot\), so the nominal 20-particle halo threshold is \(3.8\times10^7\,M_\odot\). The FOF mass-function plot applies a more conservative lower cut \(M_{\rm FOF}\ge10^8\,M_\odot\), corresponding to roughly 50 particles before the Warren correction, and the quantitative mass bins discussed below are above this limit.}

{\color{blue}The analytic curves use the Reed07 FOF fitting function evaluated with the same FOF mass definition and with the linear power spectrum corresponding to each input-spectrum model \citep{Reed_2007}. The comparison is therefore model-matched at the level of the input linear matter spectrum, while the simulation points capture the nonlinear abundance response. Because halo mass-function fits are calibrated for specific halo definitions, we keep the FOF and spherical-overdensity comparisons separate: Fig.~\ref{fig:mass_function} uses FOF masses with Reed07, while the $M_{200c}$ cross-check in Appendix~\ref{app:m200c_hmf} uses SOAP spherical-overdensity masses with the Bocquet et al. fitting function \citep[hereafter B16]{Bocquet2016}.}

{\color{blue}The lower ratio panels show the direct model response, \(f_{\rm BT}(M)/f_{\rm PL}(M)\), computed in the same FOF mass bins and plotted where both the BT and PL bins are populated. Here \(f(M)=dn/d\log_{10}M\) is the halo mass function in the plotted logarithmic-bin convention. The ratio error bars propagate the Poisson counting uncertainties from both simulation measurements. We do not show the simulation-to-Reed07 residuals in the main figure, so that the ratio panels focus on the BT response relative to PL.}

{\color{blue}Within the plotted resolved range, both blue-tilted models increase the low-mass FOF abundance relative to PL, with the largest response in BT\_soft. The effect is most visible at high redshift and weakens by \(z=0\): for BT\_soft, the abundance ratio relative to PL is \(4.81\)--\(6.55\) over \(M_{\rm FOF}\simeq1.2\times10^9\)--\(1.2\times10^{10}\,M_\odot\) at \(z=8.52\), but \(1.42\)--\(1.47\) over the same mass range at \(z=0\). The high-mass end approaches PL because the transfer-function modification affects the smaller-scale input matter power most strongly, and because late nonlinear growth and mergers partially erase the relative abundance contrast. This interpretation is restricted to the bins above the plotted catalog cut, \(M_{\rm FOF}\ge10^8\,M_\odot\).}

{\color{blue}Following the multiplicity-function convention used for the Reed07 fitting formula \citep{Reed_2007}, the analytic halo mass function can be written as}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:hmf_multiplicity}
\color{blue}
\frac{dn}{d\ln M}
=
\frac{\bar{\rho}_{\rm m}}{M}
f(\sigma)
\left|\frac{d\ln \sigma^{-1}}{d\ln M}\right| ,
\end{equation}
{\color{blue}where \(\bar{\rho}_{\rm m}\) is the mean matter density, \(\sigma(M,z)\) is the linear-theory rms fluctuation of the density field smoothed on the mass scale \(M\), and \(f(\sigma)\) is the Reed07 FOF multiplicity function. In our comparison, \(\sigma(M,z)\) is computed from the model-matched linear power spectrum for PL, BT\_soft, or BT\_deep.}

\subsubsection{\textcolor{blue}{NFW-Equivalent Compactness Diagnostic}\label{subsub:DensityProfile}}

The comparison is therefore separate from the FOF mass definition used for the HMF and should be read as a visualization of concentration-based compactness, not as an independent particle-stacked density-profile measurement.}

{\color{blue}In this reconstruction, the structural response is concentrated toward the halo centers. In the inner regions (\(r<0.1R_{200}\)), BT\_soft has the largest NFW-equivalent density enhancement, BT\_deep is intermediate, and PL has the lowest central densities. This ordering is consistent with the direct \(V_{\max}\)--\(R_{\max}\) summary at \(z=0\): in the \(10^9\)--\(10^{9.5}\,M_\odot\) bin, BT\_soft has a median \(V_{\max}\) that is \(1.27\) times larger than PL and a median \(R_{\max}\) that is \(0.59\) times the PL value, while BT\_deep gives ratios of \(1.06\) and \(0.82\).}

In the highest-mass bins shown, the reconstructed profiles converge within the visual precision of the plotted curves. We therefore interpret the NFW-equivalent diagnostic as a compactness signal associated with earlier collapse, not as evidence for a different subhalo population. A subhalo interpretation would require host-controlled subhalo mass functions, radial number-density profiles, and explicit subhalo resolution cuts, which are outside the scope of this work.}

\subsubsection{Concentration-Mass Relation\label{subsub:concentration}}
{\color{blue}The concentration--mass relation provides a compact summary of the halo structural response. We use the \textsc{SOAP} \texttt{SO/200\_crit} catalogs associated with the \textsc{hbt+} halo catalogs, taking \(M_{200c}\), \(c_{200c}\), and the number of dark-matter particles for each halo. Catalog masses are converted to solar masses using the stored unit factor \(10^{10}\,M_{\odot}\). We require finite positive masses and concentrations, impose \(c_{200c}<500\), and retain only halos with at least 100 dark-matter particles, corresponding to \(M_{200c}\simeq1.9\times10^8\,M_\odot\) for the main-run particle mass.

The simulation points in Fig.~\ref{fig:concentration_comparison} show the median \(\log_{10}c_{200c}\) in 35 logarithmic mass bins over \(8.0 \le \log_{10}(M_{200c}/M_{\odot}) \le 13.5\), with error bars given by the 16th--84th percentile scatter. For comparison, the same figure shows the semi-analytic concentration models evaluated with \textsc{colossus} \citep{Diemer2018} using the \(200c\) mass definition and the corresponding PL or BT input power spectra. The plotted model curves use the same background cosmological parameters as the simulations and include the Diemer \& Joyce (2019) and Ishiyama et al. (2021) prescriptions \citep{Diemer2019,Ishiyama2021}.}

{\color{blue}Here \(c_{200c}=R_{200c}/r_s\), where \(R_{200c}\) is the spherical-overdensity radius with respect to the critical density and \(r_s\) is the scale radius of the NFW profile \citep{Navarro1996NFW,Navarro1997NFW}. Figure~\ref{fig:concentration_comparison} compares the concentration--mass relation at four representative redshifts for PL, BT\_soft, and BT\_deep. This notation replaces the older ``strong'' and ``weak'' BT language: BT\_soft is the \(k_p=1\,h\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}\) model, while BT\_deep is the \(k_p=10\,h\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}\) model.}

{\color{blue}The measured medians show a clear mass- and redshift-dependent concentration response. At \(z=0\), the median concentration in the \(10^9\)--\(10^{9.5}\,M_\odot\) bin is higher than PL by factors of \(3.20\) for BT\_soft and \(1.35\) for BT\_deep. The larger BT\_soft response is consistent with the HMF and assembly-history results because its transfer-function-modified input spectrum enhances a broader range of resolved modes. More generally, halo concentrations are known to encode formation history and to respond to changes in the linear power spectrum in non-standard small-scale-power simulations \citep{Ludlow2016WDMConcentration,Brown2020HaloPPS}. We therefore interpret the concentration enhancement as the structural counterpart of earlier assembly, not as an independent fitted input-power parameter.}
{\color{blue}The semi-analytic curves should be interpreted as published concentration--mass mappings rather than independent fits to our binned simulation points. In these comparisons,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:colossus_concentration}
c_{200c}^{\rm th}(M,z)=\mathcal{C}_{\rm model}\!\left[M_{200c},z;P_{\rm lin}(k),\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\rm cosmo}\right],
\end{equation}
where \(\mathcal{C}_{\rm model}\) denotes the Diemer--Joyce or Ishiyama prescription implemented in \textsc{colossus}, \(P_{\rm lin}(k)\) is the PL or BT linear matter power spectrum, and \(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\rm cosmo}\) denotes the shared background cosmological parameters. The mass and concentration are defined with the same spherical-overdensity convention used for the simulation points,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:m200c_c200c_definitions}
\begin{gathered}
M_{200c}=\frac{4\pi}{3}\,200\rho_{\rm crit}(z)R_{200c}^{3},
\qquad
c_{200c}=\frac{R_{200c}}{r_s}.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The underlying assumption is that halo concentration is primarily tied to the collapse history encoded in the linear density field. The blue-tilted models therefore change the predicted concentration through the input power spectrum, not through a refit of the analytic curves to the simulation measurements.}

\section{\textcolor{blue}{Discussion and Limitations}}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{\textcolor{blue}{Physical Interpretation of the Main Trends}}
{\color{blue}
The results in Section~\ref{sec:results} point to a single physical picture. Increasing the small-scale input matter power at \(z_i=200\) raises the initial amplitude of low-mass density fluctuations, so these perturbations cross the collapse threshold earlier than in the PL model. This earlier collapse first appears as a high-redshift abundance excess and then leaves a lower-redshift structural imprint. For BT\_soft, the FOF abundance at $z=8.52$ is larger than PL by factors of $4.81$--$6.55$ over $M\simeq1.2\times10^9$--$1.2\times10^{10}\,M_\odot$, while the raw same-\texttt{TrackId} half-mass diagnostic near \(M_{z=0}\simeq1.25\times10^9\,M_\odot\) shifts from \(z_{1/2}=2.28\) in PL to \(4.24\) in BT\_soft. Across the \(10^9\)--\(10^{10}\,M_\odot\) range, the same diagnostic shows BT\_soft reaching half of its final FOF mass earlier than PL by roughly \(1.1\)--\(2.0\) in redshift. At $z=0$, the same earlier formation history is reflected in the internal structure of halos: in the $10^9$--$10^{9.5}\,M_\odot$ bin, the median concentration is higher than PL by a factor of $3.20$ for BT\_soft.

The signal is not simply a uniform rescaling of all nonlinear statistics. It depends on the pivot scale, redshift, halo mass, and diagnostic. BT\_soft produces the larger response because its enhancement begins at $k_p=1\,h\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}$ and therefore affects more of the resolved mode range in the fiducial box. BT\_deep, with $k_p=10\,h\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}$, gives a weaker response in the halo mass function and concentration--mass relation, and becomes close to PL at sufficiently high halo mass. The abundance contrast also decreases toward low redshift: for example, the BT\_soft/PL ratio at $M\simeq1.21\times10^9\,M_\odot$ falls from $4.81$ at $z=8.52$ to $1.42$ at $z=0$. This evolution is expected because nonlinear growth and hierarchical merging partially erase the relative abundance contrast by the present day, even though halo assembly histories and concentrations retain a memory of the earlier collapse.

The nonlinear matter power spectrum provides a complementary field-level view. In the matched PL and BT\_soft fiducial-box comparison, the ratio reaches $P_{\rm BT}/P_{\rm PL}=1.17$ at $k=20\,h\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}$ and $1.34$ at $k=40\,h\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}$. This response is more modest than the low-mass halo and concentration signals, but it has the same sign and appears on the expected small scales. The agreement among halo abundance, assembly history, concentration-based structural diagnostics, and high-$k$ clustering supports the interpretation that the measured trends are driven by the transfer-function-induced change in the input matter power spectrum rather than by one particular analysis statistic.}

\subsection{\textcolor{blue}{Relation to Previous Work}}
{\color{blue}
This study is complementary to work that connects modified small-scale initial matter power to individual observational probes. JWST-motivated studies ask whether enhanced small-scale power can help produce more early luminous galaxies \citep{Parashari23JWSTPPS,Hira24bluetilt}. Because the simulations analyzed here are collisionless dark-matter-only runs, they do not include a galaxy-formation or survey-selection model. We therefore do not predict galaxy luminosities or observed galaxy number counts directly. Instead, we test the underlying dark-matter response required by such scenarios: the BT models form more low-mass halos at high redshift and shift halo assembly earlier, both of which would be prerequisites for increasing the abundance of early galaxies.

The present periodic-box approach also differs from zoom-in studies of individual environments. Zoom-in simulations can follow the satellite and subhalo population of a Milky Way--mass host in detail \citep{Wu25BT}, whereas our boxes are designed to measure population-level halo statistics and matter-field statistics such as the nonlinear matter power spectrum. We therefore avoid drawing subhalo-specific conclusions from the projected maps or stacked host profiles. Instead, our concentration-based structural measurements provide the corresponding dark-matter-only response for isolated halo structure, which is closely related to the quantities entering dwarf-galaxy density constraints and strong-lensing analyses of low-mass structure \citep{Dekker25DwarfPPS,Gilman22SubgalacticPPS}.

The connection to 21 cm studies is more indirect but still useful. Cosmic-dawn and dark-ages measurements are sensitive to the abundance and timing of early structure formation, and have been proposed as probes of small-scale matter fluctuations beyond the scales directly tested by the CMB and galaxy surveys \citep{Munoz20SmallScale21cm,deKruijf25DarkAges,Zhu26SKAPPS}. The simulations here identify the mass, redshift, and wavenumber ranges where enhanced initial matter power produces the largest dark-matter response. They are therefore best viewed as an intermediate simulation-level calibration, not as a replacement for observation-specific forward modeling.}

\subsection{\textcolor{blue}{Numerical and Modeling Limitations}}
{\color{blue}
Several limitations should be kept in mind. First, the simulations are collisionless dark-matter-only runs. This design cleanly isolates the gravitational response to the modified initial matter power spectrum, but it omits gas cooling, star formation, stellar and AGN feedback, and reionization. These processes can change halo density profiles, subhalo survival, and the mapping between halos and galaxies. The results should therefore be interpreted as predictions for the underlying dark matter distribution, not as direct predictions for observed galaxies or lensing samples without further modeling.

Second, the main PL, BT\_soft, and BT\_deep comparisons use one matched-seed realization per model. Using the same initial phases reduces realization noise in the model-to-model ratios and helps isolate the response to the modified input power spectrum. However, the plotted HMF error bars include Poisson counting uncertainties only, while the concentration error bars show halo-to-halo scatter; neither includes cosmic variance or realization-to-realization scatter. This limitation is most important for rare high-mass halos, large-scale modes in the matter power spectrum, and any statistic measured from the smaller auxiliary boxes. The broad direction of the BT response is consistent across diagnostics, but the quoted ratios should be interpreted as single-realization simulation benchmarks rather than covariance-level constraints.

Third, the usable range of each statistic is set by different numerical effects. The low-mass halo mass function is limited by particle discreteness and halo-finder thresholds; the concentration-based structural diagnostics are limited by particle number, force softening, centering, and the catalog concentration measurement; and the nonlinear matter power spectrum depends on box size, mesh assignment, force resolution, and high-$k$ estimator choices. In addition, the field-level power-spectrum ratio is quantified only for matched PL and BT products with the same box size.

Finally, the analytic curves used for the halo mass function and concentration--mass relation are controlled reference models, not exact predictions in the deeply nonlinear regime. They are useful because they are evaluated with the same transfer-function-modified input power spectra, but residual differences between the simulations and fitting formulae may reflect both physical nonlinear evolution and the limited calibration range of the analytic prescriptions.}

\subsection{\textcolor{blue}{Connection to Observations}}
{\color{blue}
The most direct observational applications are probes of low-mass structure and early collapse. High-redshift galaxy counts are sensitive to the abundance and assembly time of halos near the cooling and star-formation thresholds. Dwarf-galaxy dynamics and strong-lensing perturbations probe the density and abundance of low-mass structures at low redshift \citep{Dekker25DwarfPPS,Gilman22SubgalacticPPS}. Future 21 cm measurements can test related information in the timing of early structure formation and the small-scale matter field \citep{Munoz20SmallScale21cm,deKruijf25DarkAges,Zhu26SKAPPS}. The diagnostics measured here map naturally onto these observables, but only after the relevant baryonic, selection, and survey effects are added.

The main value of the present work is therefore to identify where an initial-power signal is likely to be largest before those additional complications are introduced. The simulations show that the strongest responses occur in high-redshift low-mass halo abundance, early assembly histories, low-mass halo concentrations, and high-$k$ matter clustering. These quantities can serve as a compact target set for future hydrodynamic simulations, semi-analytic galaxy modeling, reionization calculations, and likelihood analyses that combine several small-scale probes.}

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusion}

{\color{blue}We have used dark-matter-only cosmological $N$-body simulations to quantify how enhanced small-scale power in the initial-condition matter spectrum is transformed by nonlinear structure formation. By comparing a standard power-law input matter spectrum with two blue-tilted benchmark models generated through modified transfer functions, we isolated the gravitational response to the modified initial conditions before introducing baryonic physics, galaxy formation, or observational selection.

The main conclusions, with the corresponding result sections and figures, are as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The halo-abundance response is strongest at low mass and high redshift. In the FOF halo mass function (Section~\ref{subsub:HMF}; Fig.~\ref{fig:mass_function}), BT\_soft exceeds PL by factors of \(4.81\) and \(6.55\) at \(z=8.52\) for \(M_{\rm FOF}\simeq1.21\times10^9\) and \(1.21\times10^{10}\,M_\odot\), respectively, while the corresponding \(z=0\) ratios are \(1.42\) and \(1.47\). The \(M_{200c}\) cross-check with spherical-overdensity masses gives the same qualitative conclusion (Appendix~\ref{app:m200c_hmf}; Fig.~\ref{fig:m200c_bocquet16_hmf}).
    \item Enhanced small-scale input power shifts halo assembly earlier. In the raw same-\texttt{TrackId} half-mass diagnostic (Section~\ref{subsub:HMR}; Fig.~\ref{fig:half_mass_redshift}), the mean \(z_{1/2}\) near \(M_{z=0}\simeq1.25\times10^9\,M_\odot\) changes from \(2.28\) in PL to \(4.24\) in BT\_soft and \(2.61\) in BT\_deep. This is the catalog-level assembly counterpart of the high-redshift abundance enhancement.
    \item The structural response follows the same physical ordering. The NFW-equivalent compactness diagnostic (Section~\ref{subsub:DensityProfile}; Fig.~\ref{fig:halo_density_analysis}) and the concentration--mass relation (Section~\ref{subsub:concentration}; Fig.~\ref{fig:concentration_comparison}) both show stronger low-mass compactness in the blue-tilted runs. At \(z=0\), the median \(c_{200c}\) in the \(10^9\)--\(10^{9.5}\,M_\odot\) bin is higher than PL by factors of \(3.20\) for BT\_soft and \(1.35\) for BT\_deep.
    \item The nonlinear matter power spectrum gives a coherent but more modest field-level signal. In the matched \(25\,h^{-1}\mathrm{Mpc}\) fiducial comparison (Section~\ref{subsub:PS}; Fig.~\ref{fig:power_spectra_ratio_z0}), BT\_soft reaches \(P_{\rm BT}/P_{\rm PL}=1.17\) at \(k=20\,h\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}\) and \(1.34\) at \(k=40\,h\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}\). The direction of the response is consistent with the halo diagnostics, but its amplitude is more sensitive to box size and high-\(k\) estimator choices.
\end{enumerate}

The analytic ingredients that we recommend carrying forward from this benchmark are the tested, model-matched prescriptions rather than new fits to the present simulation points. These are: the transfer-function-modified BT input-power ratio in Eq.~(\ref{eq:bt_input_power}); the Reed07 FOF halo mass-function prescription evaluated with the corresponding PL or BT \(P_{\rm lin}(k)\), using the multiplicity-function form in Eq.~(\ref{eq:hmf_multiplicity}) and the same FOF mass definition as Fig.~\ref{fig:mass_function}; the B16 \(M_{200c}\) mass-function prescription as an SO-mass cross-check (Appendix~\ref{app:m200c_hmf}; Fig.~\ref{fig:m200c_bocquet16_hmf}); and the Diemer--Joyce and Ishiyama concentration--mass mappings implemented in \textsc{colossus}, evaluated as in Eq.~(\ref{eq:colossus_concentration}) with the definitions in Eq.~(\ref{eq:m200c_c200c_definitions}). We do not recommend using the projected maps as subhalo-count evidence or adopting a standalone nonlinear-power fitting formula from this work; those applications require additional calibration beyond the tests performed here.

These trends are strongest in the low-mass, high-redshift, and high-wavenumber regimes, and they weaken toward massive halos and late times. The present results are simulation-level benchmarks rather than direct observational constraints. They are relevant to JWST-era high-redshift galaxy studies, dwarf-galaxy structure, strong-lensing probes of low-mass structure, 21 cm forecasts, and small-scale clustering measurements, but each application requires additional modeling of baryons, reionization, galaxy selection, and survey response.}

\section{Temporary FOF Group Reassignment in Half-Mass Histories}
\label{app:fof_gap_stitching}

{\color{blue}The half-mass assembly diagnostic in Fig.~\ref{fig:half_mass_redshift} uses FOF masses along HBT+ tracks. This is useful for comparing PL, BT\_soft, and BT\_deep with a common history construction, but it also exposes a catalog-level ambiguity. Some objects keep the same HBT+ \texttt{TrackId} and remain self-bound, yet for a small number of snapshots they are no longer listed as an independent FOF host. In the SOAP/HBT+ catalog this appears as \texttt{InputHalos/FOF/Masses}=0 and \texttt{InputHalos/FOF/Sizes}=0 in the object's own row, while \texttt{InputHalos/IsCentral}=0 and \texttt{InputHalos/HBTplus/HostFOFId} points to a larger host group. The object has therefore not disappeared; its mass has been assigned to the host FOF group for that snapshot, while the subhalo still has non-zero \texttt{BoundSubhalo/TotalMass}.}

{\color{blue}The resulting gap is therefore a gap in the FOF-host mass history, not evidence that the tracked object has zero physical mass. During such an episode, the receiving FOF host can also acquire a transient mass excess and later lose it when the FOF groups split again, so individual FOF masses are not guaranteed to be monotonic. The \texttt{BoundSubhalo/TotalMass} history is more continuous in this situation because it follows the self-bound object identity rather than the instantaneous FOF group assignment. This behavior is closer to the intuitive picture in which two self-bound haloes have not fully merged but are temporarily linked by the FOF algorithm or exchange outer material. However, \texttt{BoundSubhalo/TotalMass} is not the same mass definition as the FOF or spherical-overdensity host-halo masses used in the BK09 and COCO/Hellwing16 reference relations, so we use it only as a diagnostic of object survival and catalog assignment, not as a direct replacement for the reference halo mass.}

{\color{blue}This behavior is important for FOF-based mass-accretion histories. If such a snapshot is treated as permanent termination of the progenitor branch, the history is truncated even though the same tracked object may appear again as an independent FOF halo in adjacent snapshots. Conversely, reconnecting across arbitrarily long gaps may be too permissive. To quantify the size of this effect, we performed a PL-only FOF gap-stitching test. The procedure is:}

\begin{enumerate}
\color{blue}
    \item Select the same \(z=0\) FOF halo sample used in the PL half-mass analysis, giving \(344271\) halos.
    \item Follow the same HBT+ \texttt{TrackId} over snapshots 0021--0056 and record Warren-corrected \texttt{InputHalos/FOF/Masses}.
    \item Mark a snapshot as a valid independent FOF-host point only if the same \texttt{TrackId} has positive FOF mass, positive FOF size, and is central. Snapshots where the same \texttt{TrackId} is present but has \texttt{FOF/Masses}=\texttt{FOF/Sizes}=0 are treated as temporary non-FOF assignments.
    \item Recompute \(z_{1/2}\) while allowing at most \(N_{\rm gap}\) consecutive non-FOF snapshots to be crossed before reconnecting to earlier valid FOF-host points. We test \(N_{\rm gap}=0,1,2,3,5\), and the limiting case in which all gaps are allowed.
\end{enumerate}

{\color{blue}The strict case \(N_{\rm gap}=0\) is close to a FOF-host-only main-branch definition: it gives \(262310/344271\) measurable halos and a PL--BK09 by-bin mean absolute offset of \(0.2363\) in \(z_{1/2}\). Allowing short gaps monotonically restores measurable histories and moves the PL relation toward the BK09 reference: the measurable counts and BK09 offsets are \(292027\) and \(0.1850\) for \(N_{\rm gap}=1\), \(314254\) and \(0.1206\) for \(N_{\rm gap}=2\), \(323441\) and \(0.0871\) for \(N_{\rm gap}=3\), and \(327635\) and \(0.0664\) for \(N_{\rm gap}=5\). Allowing all gaps gives \(329621\) measurable halos and an offset of \(0.0548\).}

{\color{blue}As a separate check on the failure mode, among the PL halos that are measurable by a same-\texttt{TrackId} FOF history but not by the strict corrected descendant-rule branch, \(67077/67328\) (\(99.63\%\)) stop at a snapshot where a same-\texttt{TrackId} candidate exists but has \texttt{FOF/Sizes}=0. This confirms that the dominant issue is temporary FOF group reassignment rather than loss of the HBT+ track itself. We therefore interpret the raw same-\texttt{TrackId} assembly curve in Fig.~\ref{fig:half_mass_redshift} as a direct no-envelope catalog-history diagnostic, and the gap-stitching curves in Fig.~\ref{fig:fof_gap_stitching} as a bracket on the associated systematic, rather than as a claim that the HBT+ bound-subhalo mass is the same quantity as the FOF main-progenitor mass used in the BK09 and COCO/Hellwing16 reference relations \citep{BoylanKolchin2009MSII,Hellwing16cocoproject}.}

    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.86\textwidth,height=0.70\textheight,keepaspectratio]{halfmass-redshift-fof-gap-stitching.png}
    \caption{{\color{blue}PL half-mass assembly redshift under different treatments of temporary FOF group reassignment. All curves use the same \(z=0\) FOF halo sample and the same HBT+ \texttt{TrackId} FOF mass histories; only the maximum number of consecutive non-FOF snapshots allowed for reconnection is varied. The strict \(N_{\rm gap}=0\) case terminates a history once the object is temporarily classified as a satellite or hostless entry with zero FOF mass/size in its own catalog row.
