\begin{abstract}
The small-scale primordial power spectrum remains weakly constrained. An enhancement of primordial power on these scales can cause low-mass density fluctuations to collapse earlier, modifying both the abundance and internal structure of dark matter halos. We quantify this response with matched-phase, dark-matter-only cosmological \(N\)-body simulations. We compare a standard power-law (PL) model with two blue-tilted (BT) benchmark models in which the power is enhanced above a pivot wavenumber \(k_p\). We measure Friends-of-Friends (FOF) halo mass functions, assembly histories, radial density profiles, concentration--mass relations, and the nonlinear matter power spectrum.

Among the BT models considered here, the \(k_p=1\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\), \(m_s=1.5\) case, hereafter BT\_soft, produces the largest response. Its FOF halo abundance exceeds that of PL by factors of \(4.8\)--\(6.6\) at \(z=8.52\) over \(M_{\rm FOF}\simeq1.2\times10^9\)--\(1.2\times10^{10}\,M_\odot\), and by \(1.4\)--\(1.5\) at \(z=0\) over the same mass range. Halo assembly also shifts earlier: near \(M_{z=0}\simeq1.25\times10^9\,M_\odot\), the half-mass redshift increases from \(z_{1/2}=2.28\) in PL to \(4.24\) in BT\_soft. At \(z=0\), the median concentration in the \(10^9\)--\(10^{9.5}\,M_\odot\) bin is higher by a factor of \(3.20\). The nonlinear matter power spectrum shows a more modest response, with \(P_{\rm BT}/P_{\rm PL}=1.17\) at \(k=20\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\) and \(1.34\) at \(k=40\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\).

These results indicate that enhanced small-scale primordial power primarily affects low-mass halos and high-\(k\) nonlinear clustering, with a response that depends on pivot scale and redshift. The simulations provide dark-matter-only benchmark predictions for future comparisons with high-redshift galaxies, dwarf-galaxy dynamics, strong lensing, and 21-cm probes.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction\label{intro}}
On large scales, the standard \(\Lambda\)CDM cosmology with a nearly power-law primordial power spectrum is consistent with measurements of the cosmic microwave background, galaxy clustering, and the Lyman-\(\alpha\) forest \citep{Planck20CMB,Blanton17SDSSsurvey,Chabanier19LyalphaPS}. On smaller scales, the primordial power spectrum is less directly constrained. Long-standing apparent small-scale tensions, including the missing-satellites, core-cusp, and too-big-to-fail problems, have motivated tests of departures from the simplest collisionless cold-dark-matter picture \citep{Bullock17smallscalechallenges,Sales22challenges}. These tensions do not uniquely constrain the primordial spectrum, because baryonic physics can affect the same observables \citep{Benson2002reionization,Chan2015corecusp}.

Recent JWST reports of abundant, potentially massive galaxy candidates at \(z\gtrsim8\)--\(10\) have renewed interest in the small-scale primordial power spectrum \citep{Labbe23MassiveGalaxies,Donnan23UVLF}. One class of explanations invokes enhanced small-scale primordial power, which increases the abundance and formation redshifts of low-mass halos \citep{Parashari23JWSTPPS,Hira24bluetilt}. However, the inferred galaxy counts also depend on galaxy formation, dust attenuation, feedback, star-formation efficiency, UV emission efficiency, and selection effects \citep{Lovell23JWSTExtreme}.



%One economical way to test this possibility is to modify the input matter power only on scales that are not already tightly constrained by CMB and large-scale structure data. 


Such enhancements arise in several classes of early-universe models. Warm inflation can amplify curvature perturbations \citep{Arya20WarmInflation}. Axion models with curved field-space effects can produce blue spectra \citep{Sai26BlueTilt}, and multi-field or hybrid inflation models can produce broken-power-law enhancements \citep{Linde94Hybridinflation,Wang24BTinflationtheory,Wang25HybridInflation}. A population of primordial black holes (PBHs) can also add small-scale matter power through Poisson fluctuations, which act as an isocurvature component \citep{Inman2019PBHBT}. We do not model these mechanisms separately. Instead, we use blue-tilted spectra as phenomenological benchmarks for quantifying the dark-matter response to enhanced small-scale primordial power.




Following previous literature \citep[e.g.][]{Hirano15BlueTiltedPPS,Parashari23JWSTPPS,Hira24bluetilt,Tkac24BumpyPPS,deKruijf25DarkAges,Dekker25DwarfPPS,Wu25BT}, we call these models ``blue-tilted'' (BT) because their small-scale slope is steeper than the usual power-law slope. Below a pivot wavenumber \(k_p\), the primordial power-spectrum model follows the standard PL shape. Above \(k_p\), the slope changes to \(m_s\), with \(m_s>n_s\), so the model has more power at larger \(k\), or smaller physical scales. In this paper the BT spectra are phenomenological benchmarks, not direct fits to a specific inflation model.





%In this paper, the BT parameters should be read as a phenomenological description of the linear matter spectrum used to generate the initial conditions, not as a complete inflationary or curvature-spectrum model. 


Recent work has examined enhanced small-scale primordial power with several complementary probes. Recent JWST-motivated studies test whether enhanced small-scale primordial power can increase the abundance of early galaxies \citep{Parashari23JWSTPPS,Hira24bluetilt}. The BT models have also been studied with zoom-in simulations of Milky Way--mass environments \citep{Wu25BT}. Dwarf-galaxy densities, satellite statistics, sub-galactic strong lensing, and 21-cm observations provide complementary tests \citep{Kim18nomissingsatellites,Dekker25DwarfPPS,Gilman22SubgalacticPPS,Munoz20SmallScale21cm,deKruijf25DarkAges,Zhu26SKAPPS}. Interpreting these probes requires astrophysical and observational modeling. They therefore do not, by themselves, isolate the dark-matter halo response to changes in the small-scale primordial power spectrum.

Here we isolate the dark-matter halo response before modeling galaxy formation or survey selection. We run dark-matter-only cosmological \(N\)-body simulations in periodic boxes, with matched initial phases for PL and BT runs. We compare a standard power-law primordial power-spectrum model with two BT models. We measure the halo mass function, half-mass assembly redshift, radial density profiles, the concentration--mass relation, and the nonlinear matter power spectrum. Together, these statistics quantify how enhanced small-scale primordial power changes halo abundance, assembly, internal structure, and nonlinear matter clustering.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. Sec.~\ref{sec:theory} defines the PL and BT primordial power-spectrum models and their corresponding input linear matter power spectra at \(z_i=200\). Sec.~\ref{sec:numerical} describes the initial conditions, simulations, halo catalogs, and analysis choices. Sec.~\ref{sec:results} presents the halo mass function, assembly-redshift, concentration, and nonlinear matter-power results. Sec.~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses the physical interpretation, numerical limitations, and links to observations. Sec.~\ref{sec:conclusion} summarizes the main quantitative findings.


\section{Theoretical Overview}
\label{sec:theory}

We study how changes in the primordial power spectrum affect the abundance, spatial distribution, and internal structure of dark matter halos. We first define the PL and BT primordial power-spectrum models and the corresponding linear matter power spectra used to generate the initial conditions. We then summarize how small-scale differences in these spectra enter nonlinear halo formation. Throughout the paper, we use two terminology levels to distinguish primordial models from their matter-power-spectrum realizations.

\subsection{Power-Spectrum Models and Linear Matter Power}

In the power-law (PL) model, the primordial power spectrum scales as \(P_{\rm prim}(k)\propto k^{n_s}\), with \(n_s\simeq0.96\) \citep{Planck20CMB}.

We model small-scale enhancements with blue-tilted (BT) primordial power-spectrum models \citep[e.g.][]{Hira24bluetilt,Dekker25DwarfPPS}. In these models, the spectrum follows the PL form up to \(k_p\) and becomes steeper above \(k_p\):
\begin{equation}
P_{\rm prim}^{\rm BT}(k)
\propto
\begin{cases}
k^{n_s}, & k \le k_p,\\[4pt]
k^{n_s}\left(k/k_p\right)^{m_s-n_s}, & k > k_p,
\end{cases}
\label{eq:bt_input_power}
\end{equation}
where \(k_p\) is the pivot wavenumber and \(m_s\) controls the high-\(k\) slope of the BT primordial power-spectrum model. This form reduces to the PL shape when \(m_s=n_s\), while \(m_s>n_s\) gives a blue-tilted enhancement above \(k_p\). The initial redshift \(z_i=200\) enters later, when this model is converted into the input linear matter power spectrum used by the initial-condition generator.

In the initial-condition calculation, we implement the broken-power-law enhancement in Eq.~\eqref{eq:bt_input_power} by modifying the transfer function. The input linear matter spectrum at \(z_i=200\), \(P_{\rm lin}\propto T^2(k)k^{n_s}\), is therefore enhanced above \(k_p\).

The corresponding linear matter power spectrum can be written schematically as
\begin{equation}
P_{\rm lin}(k,z) \propto P_{\rm prim}(k)\,T^2(k)\,D^2(z),
\label{eq:P_MD}
\end{equation}
where \(T(k)\) is the linear transfer function and \(D(z)\) is the linear growth factor.



%Equivalently, the transfer-function ratio needed to realize Eq.~\eqref{eq:bt_input_power} is \(T_{\rm BT}(k)/T_{\rm PL}(k)=1\) for \(k\le k_p\) and \(T_{\rm BT}(k)/T_{\rm PL}(k)=(k/k_p)^{(m_s-n_s)/2}\) for \(k>k_p\). In this work the large-scale normalization is kept fixed; only the effective small-scale shape above $k_p$ is varied, so the model description can be specified by $k_p$ and $m_s$ for the present comparison. The BT spectra are therefore controlled transfer-function modifications of the initial-condition matter spectrum. The parameters $k_p$ and $m_s$ should not be interpreted as a complete inflationary parameterization or as independently constrained curvature-spectrum parameters in this paper.

We compare a standard power-law primordial spectrum, denoted PL, with two blue-tilted benchmark spectra, denoted BT\_soft and BT\_deep. The two BT runs use the same target small-scale spectral index but different pivot scales, as summarized in the combined model and run table, Table~\ref{tab:simulation_parameters}. BT\_soft affects a broader range of mildly nonlinear scales, whereas BT\_deep confines the enhancement to smaller scales. This pair of models allows us to test how the nonlinear response depends on the range of modes whose primordial power is enhanced.


\subsubsection{Cosmological parameters}
\label{subsub:cosmo_params}

All simulations use the following background cosmology:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Density parameters: \(\Omega_{\rm m}=0.3153\), \(\Omega_{\rm b}=0.0493\), and \(\Omega_\Lambda=0.6847\).
    \item Hubble constant: \(H_0=67.36\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}\), corresponding to \(h\equiv H_0/(100\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}})=0.6736\).
    \item Scalar spectral index \(n_s = 0.9649\).
    \item The PL and BT initial conditions use the same target normalization, \(\sigma_8 = 0.8111\).
    
    The BT spectra are not assigned separately fitted \(\sigma_8\) values. They use the same background cosmology and target \(\sigma_8\) normalization as PL. MonofonIC then applies the required global normalization to each generated spectrum. The BT prescription changes the spectral shape for \(k>k_p\); below \(k_p\), the transfer-function shape remains the PL/Eisenstein shape, apart from the small global renormalization needed to enforce the same target \(\sigma_8\).
\end{enumerate}



Table~\ref{tab:simulation_parameters} lists these cosmological parameters together with the numerical simulation parameters.

\section{Numerical Methods}
\label{sec:numerical}

This section describes the numerical setup, including the generation of matched PL and BT initial conditions, halo identification, halo-property measurements, and the numerical ranges used for quantitative comparisons.



\subsection{Initial Conditions}
We generate the initial conditions with \textsc{Music2-MonofonIC} \citep{Hahn2020MUSIC2monofonIC,Michaux2021MUSIC2monofonIC}, using the Eisenstein \& Hu transfer function \citep{Eisenstein1998} for PL. For the BT runs, we modify the MonofonIC transfer-function plugin only for \(k>k_p\). MonofonIC normalizes PL and BT to the same target value, \(\sigma_8=0.8111\). Thus the BT runs change the small-scale shape of the input spectrum, not the large-scale amplitude. For each matched set of runs, we keep the background cosmology, numerical settings, and MonofonIC random seed fixed. The main PL, BT\_soft, and BT\_deep \(25\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\), \(1024^3\) simulations therefore share the same initial phases. The matched phases ensure that PL--BT differences primarily trace the enhanced small-scale primordial power-spectrum model rather than realization-to-realization variance.

As an initial-condition consistency check, we compare the BT pivot scales with the particle-grid scale. For the main \(25\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\), \(1024^3\) runs, \(k_{\rm Ny}\simeq128.7\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\). The two pivots, \(k_p=1\) and \(10\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\), are therefore well below the grid Nyquist scale. The largest BT input value over this range is also small, \(\Delta^2(k,z_i)\simeq5.8\times10^{-5}\) for BT\_soft, so the enhanced modes are still linear at \(z_i=200\). This check addresses the location and initial linearity of the imposed break, but it does not constitute a nonlinear convergence test.

\subsection{Simulation Code}
We evolve the initial conditions with the cosmological $N$-body code \small{SWIFT} \citep{Schaller2024SWIFT}\footnote{Publicly available at \url{https://swiftsim.com/}.}. In the dark-matter-only, collisionless mode used here, {\small SWIFT}'s gravity solver uses a fast-multipole method \citep{Dehn00FMM}, coupled to a Particle-Mesh solver at large scales \citep{Hock88PM}.

\subsection{Halo Finder and Halo Property Post-processor}

The halo statistics require two catalog layers: one for tracking object histories and one for measuring spherical-overdensity properties.

Halos and merger trees are identified with \textsc{HBT-HERONS}, a particle-tracking halo finder developed by \cite{Victor25HBTHERONS}\footnote{\url{https://github.com/SWIFTSIM/HBT-HERONS}}. We require a minimum of 20 particles for an object to be retained as a halo candidate, and define the halo center as the position of the most bound particle. For the main $25\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}$, $1024^3$ runs, the snapshot mass table gives \(m_{\rm DM}=1.89\times10^6\,M_\odot=1.27\times10^6\,h^{-1}M_\odot\). The nominal 20-particle halo threshold is therefore \(M\simeq3.8\times10^7\,M_\odot\), while a 100-particle structural cut corresponds to \(M\simeq1.9\times10^8\,M_\odot\). Based on the \textsc{HBT-HERONS} catalogs, we then use \textsc{SOAP} (Spherical Overdensity and Aperture Processor) \cite{McGibbon2025SOAP}\footnote{\url{https://github.com/SWIFTSIM/SOAP}} to compute spherical-overdensity properties such as $M_{200c}$, $R_{200c}$, and the structural quantities used below. This separation between halo identification and property measurement retains the tracking and merger-tree information used for the half-mass assembly analysis while adopting consistent spherical-overdensity quantities for the radial-profile and concentration analyses.

Different statistics use different halo mass definitions. For the halo mass function, we use the Friends-of-Friends (FOF) algorithm \citep{Davis1985FOF} to define halo masses and compare them to analytic predictions evaluated with the same FOF mass definition. The half-mass assembly diagnostic uses FOF-based same-TrackId mass histories from the \textsc{HBT-HERONS} catalogs, so its $z=0$ mass bins should be interpreted as $M_{\rm FOF}$ bins. For the internal-structure diagnostics, we use spherical-overdensity quantities measured by \textsc{SOAP}. The direct radial-profile stacks use \texttt{SO/200\_mean} masses and radii for the mass bins and for \(r/R_{200m}\). The dashed NFW curves are reference profiles, not fits. The concentration--mass relation uses $M_{200c}$, $R_{200c}$, and $c_{200c}$ from \texttt{SO/200\_crit}. The nonlinear matter power spectrum is measured from the particle density field and does not require a halo mass definition. We therefore avoid directly mixing FOF, $M_{200m}$, and $M_{200c}$ masses when interpreting a single statistic, and the relevant captions specify the mass definition used.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

This section presents the simulation results. We first show projected density maps for qualitative context, and then quantify the response in halo abundance, assembly time, internal structure, and the nonlinear matter power spectrum.

\subsection{Simulation Maps}

The projected dark matter maps provide qualitative context for the simulations. The largest structures and the most massive collapsed regions are similar across PL, BT\_soft, and BT\_deep, whereas the blue-tilted runs show enhanced small-scale contrast. We use these maps only as qualitative context. The present analysis does not measure a subhalo mass function, radial subhalo distribution, or subhalo survival statistic, so the maps are not used as quantitative evidence for subhalo abundance or concentration.

\subsection{Halo Mass Function\label{subsub:HMF}}

We quantify halo abundance with the FOF halo mass function. Fig.~\ref{fig:mass_function} shows the FOF halo mass functions at representative snapshots from $z=8.52$ to $z=0$. The plotted simulation points are computed from the FOF catalogs in logarithmic mass bins, with Poisson counting uncertainties and the Warren et al. correction, $N_{\rm corr}=N(1-N^{-0.6})$, applied at the low-particle end \citep{Warren_2006}. Before binning, each FOF mass is corrected for the low-particle sampling bias using \(M_{\rm corr}=M_{\rm FOF}[1-(M_{\rm FOF}/m_{\rm DM})^{-0.6}]\), following the Warren correction as used in the high-redshift HMF analysis of \citet{Lukic2007HMF}. For each non-empty logarithmic bin, the plotted mass coordinate is the mean corrected halo mass in that bin, \(\bar M=\sum_i M_{{\rm corr},i}/N_{\rm h}\), rather than the logarithmic bin midpoint, following the bin-position prescription of \citet{Lukic2007HMF}. The FOF mass-function plot uses the conservative lower cut summarized in Table~\ref{tab:trusted_ranges}, \(M_{\rm FOF}\ge10^8\,M_\odot\), and the quantitative mass bins discussed below are above this limit. Appendix~\ref{app:fof_hmf_resolution} repeats the PL FOF mass-function measurement at lower particle resolution and in a larger PL box to show how this adopted mass range behaves under numerical resolution and volume changes.

The analytic curves are reference predictions rather than fits to the simulation measurements. For the FOF comparison, we use the Reed et al. (2007) fitting function \citep{Reed_2007} with the corresponding PL, BT\_soft, or BT\_deep linear matter spectrum. The simulation points and the BT/PL ratios are the measured nonlinear response. Because mass-function fits depend on halo definition, we keep the FOF and spherical-overdensity comparisons separate: Fig.~\ref{fig:mass_function} uses FOF masses with Reed et al. (2007), while Fig.~\ref{fig:m200c_bocquet16_hmf} uses SOAP \(M_{200c}\) masses with Bocquet et al. (2016) \citep{Bocquet2016}.

The lower ratio panels show the BT/PL abundance ratio, \(f_{\rm BT}(M)/f_{\rm PL}(M)\), computed in the same FOF mass bins and plotted where both the BT and PL bins are populated. Here \(f(M)=dn/d\log_{10}M\) is the halo mass function in the plotted logarithmic-bin convention. The ratio error bars propagate the Poisson counting uncertainties from both simulation measurements. We do not show the simulation-to-Reed et al. (2007) residuals in the main figure, so that the ratio panels focus on the BT response relative to PL.

Within the resolved mass range, both blue-tilted models increase the low-mass FOF abundance relative to PL, with the largest enhancement in BT\_soft. The enhancement is largest at high redshift. For BT\_soft, the abundance ratio relative to PL is \(4.81\)--\(6.55\) over \(M_{\rm FOF}\simeq1.2\times10^9\)--\(1.2\times10^{10}\,M_\odot\) at \(z=8.52\). By \(z=0\), the ratio over the same mass range falls to \(1.42\)--\(1.47\). The BT/PL ratio decreases toward higher masses and lower redshifts. This interpretation is restricted to the bins above the plotted catalog cut, \(M_{\rm FOF}\ge10^8\,M_\odot\).

The analytic FOF mass function follows the standard form used in the Reed et al. (2007) fitting formula \citep{Reed_2007},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:hmf_multiplicity}
\frac{dn}{d\ln M}
=
\frac{\bar{\rho}_{\rm m}}{M}
f(\sigma)
\left|\frac{d\ln \sigma^{-1}}{d\ln M}\right| ,
\end{equation}
where \(\bar{\rho}_{\rm m}\) is the mean matter density, \(\sigma(M,z)\) is the linear-theory rms fluctuation of the density field smoothed on the mass scale \(M\), and \(f(\sigma)\) is the Reed et al. (2007) FOF multiplicity function. In our comparison, \(\sigma(M,z)\) is computed from the model-matched linear matter power spectrum for PL, BT\_soft, or BT\_deep.

\subsection{Half-Mass Redshift\label{subsub:HMR}}
The half-mass assembly redshift, \(z_{1/2}\), is defined as the redshift at which a \(z=0\) halo first reaches half of its final FOF mass. Fig.~\ref{fig:half_mass_redshift} uses a raw same-\texttt{TrackId} FOF diagnostic. For each \(z=0\) FOF halo, we follow the same \textsc{HBT-HERONS} \texttt{TrackId} over snapshots 0021--0056 and record Warren-corrected FOF masses. We assign \(z_{1/2}\) only when two adjacent snapshots bracket \(M_0/2\); no gap filling or cumulative-envelope smoothing is applied. The same rule is used for PL, BT\_soft, and BT\_deep. Appendix~\ref{app:fof_gap_stitching} gives the detailed gap-stitching test and shows how temporary FOF bridge/split episodes affect the measurable sample.

The BK09 and COCO/Hellwing16 curves in Fig.~\ref{fig:half_mass_redshift} are external \(\Lambda\)CDM reference relations based on a standard power-law primordial spectrum, used only to check that the PL catalog-level diagnostic lies in a plausible range. The main comparison in this paper is the matched BT--PL difference measured with the same diagnostic. The assembly shift is largest in BT\_soft. Around \(M_{z=0}\simeq1.25\times10^9\,M_\odot\), the mean \(z_{1/2}\) is \(2.28\) for PL, \(4.24\) for BT\_soft, and \(2.61\) for BT\_deep. Thus BT\_soft forms earlier than PL by \(\Delta z_{1/2}=1.96\), while BT\_deep forms earlier by \(0.33\), in this mass bin. Around \(M_{z=0}\simeq7.9\times10^9\,M_\odot\), the corresponding means are \(1.95\), \(3.12\), and \(2.05\), giving BT--PL shifts of \(1.17\) and \(0.10\). At \(M_{z=0}\simeq2.0\times10^{11}\,M_\odot\), the difference is smaller: \(1.43\) for PL, \(1.78\) for BT\_soft, and \(1.39\) for BT\_deep. Thus, the model with the largest high-redshift HMF enhancement also produces the largest assembly-time shift, especially below \(\sim10^{10}\,M_\odot\).

\subsection{Halo Internal Structure}

We next test whether earlier halo growth also changes internal structure. We use direct radial density profiles and the concentration--mass relation.

\subsubsection{Radial Halo Density Profiles\label{subsub:DensityProfile}}
The radial-profile analysis uses direct particle density profiles to test whether earlier assembly is reflected in halo internal structure. Fig.~\ref{fig:halo_density_analysis} compares median particle-count radial profiles in \textsc{SOAP} \(M_{200m}\) bins, rather than profiles reconstructed only from catalog concentrations. The dashed curves show Diemer et al. NFW reference profiles evaluated for the corresponding PL and BT models \citep{Diemer2019}. These dashed curves are reference profiles, not fits to the particle stacks. The error bars show bootstrap 16th--84th percentile intervals for the median stacks. The gray shaded region marks radii inside the plotted convergence criterion.

The structural response is concentrated toward the halo centers. In the resolved inner regions, BT\_soft has the largest central-density enhancement relative to PL, BT\_deep shows a weaker enhancement, and the three models approach one another toward larger \(r/R_{200m}\). The lower ratio panels show that the BT\_soft enhancement is strongest in the low- and intermediate-mass bins, while BT\_deep is closer to PL. The direct \(V_{\max}\)--\(R_{\max}\) summary at \(z=0\) shows the same ordering. In the \(10^9\)--\(10^{9.5}\,M_\odot\) bin, BT\_soft has a median \(V_{\max}\) that is \(1.27\) times larger than PL and a median \(R_{\max}\) that is \(0.59\) times the PL value. For BT\_deep, the corresponding ratios are \(1.06\) and \(0.82\).

At larger radii (\(r\gtrsim0.3R_{200m}\)), the differences are weaker and more mass dependent. In the highest-mass bins shown, the measured profiles agree within the plotted scatter. We therefore interpret the radial profiles as a direct dark-matter structural signal associated with earlier collapse, not as evidence for a different subhalo population. A subhalo interpretation would require host-controlled subhalo mass functions, radial number-density profiles, and explicit subhalo resolution cuts, which are outside the scope of this work.

\subsubsection{Concentration-Mass Relation\label{subsub:concentration}}
The concentration--mass relation summarizes the structural response through \(c_{200c}\), a scalar measure of halo compactness. We use the \textsc{SOAP} \texttt{SO/200\_crit} catalogs associated with the \textsc{HBT-HERONS} halo catalogs, taking \(M_{200c}\), \(c_{200c}\), and the number of dark-matter particles for each halo. Catalog masses are converted to solar masses using the stored unit factor \(10^{10}\,M_{\odot}\). We require finite positive masses and concentrations, impose \(c_{200c}<500\), and retain only halos with at least 100 dark-matter particles, corresponding to \(M_{200c}\simeq1.9\times10^8\,M_\odot\) for the main-run particle mass.

The simulation points in Fig.~\ref{fig:concentration_comparison} show the median \(\log_{10}c_{200c}\) in 35 logarithmic mass bins over \(8.0 \le \log_{10}(M_{200c}/M_{\odot}) \le 13.5\), with error bars given by the 16th--84th percentile scatter. For comparison, the same figure shows the semi-analytic concentration models evaluated with \textsc{colossus} \citep{Diemer2018} using the \(200c\) mass definition and the corresponding PL or BT linear matter power spectra generated from the primordial power-spectrum models. The plotted model curves use the same background cosmological parameters as the simulations and include the Diemer \& Joyce (2019) and Ishiyama et al. (2021) prescriptions \citep{Diemer2019,Ishiyama2021}.

Here \(c_{200c}=R_{200c}/r_s\), where \(R_{200c}\) is the spherical-overdensity radius with respect to the critical density and \(r_s\) is the scale radius of the NFW profile \citep{Navarro1996NFW,Navarro1997NFW}. Fig.~\ref{fig:concentration_comparison} compares the concentration--mass relation at four representative redshifts for PL, BT\_soft, and BT\_deep.

The measured medians show a mass- and redshift-dependent concentration response. At \(z=0\), the median concentration in the \(10^9\)--\(10^{9.5}\,M_\odot\) bin is higher than PL by factors of \(3.20\) for BT\_soft and \(1.35\) for BT\_deep. The 16th--84th percentile ranges in Fig.~\ref{fig:concentration_comparison} show substantial halo-to-halo scatter, especially toward the low-particle end, so we use the median trends and quoted bin ratios rather than fitting individual-halo concentrations. The larger BT\_soft response is consistent with the HMF and assembly-history results, reflecting the broader range of resolved modes enhanced by its primordial power-spectrum model after transfer-function conversion. More generally, halo concentrations are known to encode formation history and to respond to changes in the linear power spectrum in non-standard small-scale-power simulations \citep{Ludlow2016WDMConcentration,Brown2020HaloPPS}. We therefore interpret the concentration enhancement as the structural counterpart of earlier assembly, not as an independent fitted input-power parameter.
The semi-analytic curves should be interpreted as published concentration--mass mappings rather than independent fits to our binned simulation points. In these comparisons,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:colossus_concentration}
c_{200c}^{\rm th}(M,z)=\mathcal{C}_{\rm model}\!\left[M_{200c},z;P_{\rm lin}(k),\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\rm cosmo}\right],
\end{equation}
where \(\mathcal{C}_{\rm model}\) denotes the Diemer \& Joyce (2019) or Ishiyama et al. (2021) prescription implemented in \textsc{colossus}, \(P_{\rm lin}(k)\) is the PL or BT linear matter power spectrum, and \(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\rm cosmo}\) denotes the shared background cosmological parameters. The mass and concentration are defined with the same spherical-overdensity convention used for the simulation points,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:m200c_c200c_definitions}
\begin{gathered}
M_{200c}=\frac{4\pi}{3}\,200\rho_{\rm crit}(z)R_{200c}^{3},
\qquad
c_{200c}=\frac{R_{200c}}{r_s}.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
The underlying assumption is that halo concentration is primarily tied to the collapse history encoded in the linear density field. The blue-tilted models therefore change the predicted concentration through the linear matter power spectrum generated from the primordial power-spectrum model, not through a refit of the analytic curves to the simulation measurements.

\subsection{Power Spectrum\label{subsub:PS}}

We finally measure the nonlinear matter power spectrum from the particle density field. This statistic tests whether the halo-level response is also present in the full dark-matter density field. We measure the spectra from the dark-matter particle distribution using the \textsc{swiftsimio} power-spectrum utilities. For each snapshot, particle masses are deposited onto a regular \(1024^3\) Cartesian mesh with \texttt{render\_to\_deposit} using \texttt{project="masses"}, and the isotropically averaged spectrum is then computed with \texttt{deposition\_to\_power\_spectrum}. In the production script we set \texttt{folding=0}, \texttt{cross\_deposition=None}, \texttt{wavenumber\_bins=None}, and pass the number of dark-matter particles as \texttt{shot\_noise\_norm}. The resulting wavenumbers and powers are converted from \({\rm Mpc}^{-1}\) and \({\rm Mpc}^3\) to \(h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\) and \((h^{-1}{\rm Mpc})^3\), respectively. For visualization only, the measured spectra and ratios are subsequently log-binned by taking the geometric-mean wavenumber and median power in each plotting bin.

The nonlinear theory curves shown for orientation are generated separately from the simulations with the HMcode2020 dark-matter-only nonlinear mapping as implemented in \texttt{pyhmcode}. We first build a PL linear matter spectrum from the Eisenstein--Hu transfer function and shared cosmological parameters. For each BT case, the same PL linear spectrum is multiplied by the broken-power-law enhancement above the corresponding \(k_p\), and the resulting PL or BT linear spectrum is supplied to HMcode2020. Thus the lower-panel ``BT theory/PL theory'' curves are ratios of these HMcode2020 predictions, not fits to the simulated BT/PL ratios and not an independent calibration for the blue-tilted cosmology.

Fig.~\ref{fig:power_spectra_ratio_z0} shows matched BT/PL ratios for the \(k_p=1\) and \(k_p=10\), \(m_s=1.5\) variants in the \(25\) and \(256\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\) boxes. We restrict the quantitative ratio analysis to matched PL and BT products with the same box size. No additional folding correction or external window-function deconvolution is applied outside the \textsc{swiftsimio} routine, so the highest-\(k\) points are interpreted as a consistency check on the small-scale trend rather than as a calibrated nonlinear-power fitting formula. Appendix~\ref{app:power_spectrum_reliability} shows the separate simulation/theory validation plot used to visualize the box-size-dependent reliability ranges.

At \(z=0\), the fiducial \(25\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\) BT\_soft/PL ratio is close to unity on large scales and rises gradually toward high \(k\): \(1.06\), \(1.10\), \(1.17\), and \(1.34\) at \(k=5\), \(10\), \(20\), and \(40\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\), respectively. In the larger \(256\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\) comparison, the response is weaker over its conservative mesh range, reaching \(1.06\) at \(k=10\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\) and \(1.08\) near its mesh Nyquist scale, \(k_{\rm Ny}\simeq12.6\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\). We therefore interpret the nonlinear power-spectrum result as a high-\(k\) enhancement consistent in sign with the halo diagnostics, while its amplitude remains sensitive to box size, estimator choices, and the adopted high-\(k\) reliability range.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

This section interprets the main trends and summarizes the numerical and observational limitations.

\subsection{Physical Interpretation of the Main Trends}

The results in Sec.~\ref{sec:results} support the following physical interpretation. Enhancing the small-scale primordial power spectrum raises the amplitude of the corresponding small-scale density fluctuations in the initial conditions. In the BT runs, these stronger initial fluctuations tend to collapse earlier than in PL. At high redshift, the primary effect is an excess of low-mass halos; at later times, the same trend appears in earlier assembly times, shifted mass-accretion histories, and higher central densities. For BT\_soft, the FOF abundance at $z=8.52$ is larger than PL by factors of $4.81$--$6.55$ over $M\simeq1.2\times10^9$--$1.2\times10^{10}\,M_\odot$, while the raw same-\texttt{TrackId} half-mass diagnostic near \(M_{z=0}\simeq1.25\times10^9\,M_\odot\) shifts from \(z_{1/2}=2.28\) in PL to \(4.24\) in BT\_soft. Across the \(10^9\)--\(10^{10}\,M_\odot\) range, the same diagnostic shows BT\_soft reaching half of its final FOF mass earlier than PL by roughly \(1.1\)--\(2.0\) in redshift. The mass-accretion-rate diagnostic further shows that BT\_soft growth is shifted to earlier epochs rather than being a late-time boost. At $z=0$, the same earlier formation history is reflected in the internal structure of halos: in the $10^9$--$10^{9.5}\,M_\odot$ bin, the median concentration is higher than PL by a factor of $3.20$ for BT\_soft, and the direct radial profile shows a corresponding inner-density enhancement.

The BT response depends on the statistic considered. It changes with pivot scale, redshift, halo mass, and diagnostic. BT\_soft produces the larger response because its enhancement begins at \(k_p=1\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\), so it affects more resolved modes in the fiducial box. BT\_deep begins at \(k_p=10\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\); its response is therefore weaker and approaches the PL result at high halo mass. The abundance ratio also drops with time: for BT\_soft at \(M\simeq1.21\times10^9\,M_\odot\), it falls from \(4.81\) at \(z=8.52\) to \(1.42\) at \(z=0\). Late nonlinear growth and mergers reduce the abundance contrast, while assembly histories and concentrations still retain the earlier-collapse signal.

The nonlinear matter power spectrum gives a second, more compact summary of the same response. In the matched PL and BT\_soft fiducial-box comparison, \(P_{\rm BT}/P_{\rm PL}=1.17\) at \(k=20\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\) and \(1.34\) at \(k=40\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\). This is smaller than the low-mass halo and concentration response, but it has the same sign and appears on the same small scales. Taken together, these diagnostics are consistent with a common origin in the modified input power spectrum.

\subsection{Relation to Previous Work}

Our comparison is intentionally narrower than direct observational modeling. JWST-oriented studies have examined whether enhanced small-scale primordial power can increase the abundance of early galaxy hosts \citep{Parashari23JWSTPPS,Hira24bluetilt}. We isolate the corresponding dark-matter response directly: the BT models form more low-mass halos at high redshift and shift their assembly earlier. Translating that halo-side response into luminosity functions or number counts requires galaxy-formation and selection modeling, so we do not use the simulations as direct galaxy constraints.

Our periodic boxes also differ from zoom-in simulations. Zoom-ins can follow satellites and subhalos around a Milky Way--mass host in detail \citep{Wu25BT}. Our boxes instead measure population-level halo statistics and the nonlinear matter power spectrum. We therefore do not infer subhalo counts from the projected maps. Our structural measurements are a dark-matter-only test of isolated halo profiles, which are relevant for dwarf-galaxy density and strong-lensing studies of low-mass structure \citep{Dekker25DwarfPPS,Gilman22SubgalacticPPS}.

For 21-cm studies, the relevant link is the timing and abundance of early structure. Cosmic-dawn and dark-ages measurements can respond to the same low-mass collapse histories tested here \citep{Munoz20SmallScale21cm,deKruijf25DarkAges,Zhu26SKAPPS}. The simulations identify the halo masses, redshifts, and wavenumbers where enhanced primordial power produces the largest dark-matter response; a 21-cm prediction still requires a source-population, heating, and reionization model.

\subsection{Numerical and Modeling Limitations}

The main limitations are the dark-matter-only setup, the single realization, and the numerical range of each statistic. First, the simulations are collisionless dark-matter-only runs. This design cleanly isolates the gravitational response to the modified primordial power-spectrum model, but it omits gas cooling, star formation, stellar and AGN feedback, and reionization. These processes can change halo density profiles, subhalo survival, and the mapping between halos and galaxies. The results should therefore be interpreted as predictions for the underlying dark matter distribution, not as direct predictions for observed galaxies or lensing samples without further modeling.

Second, the main PL, BT\_soft, and BT\_deep comparisons use one matched-seed realization per model. Using the same initial phases reduces realization noise in the model-to-model ratios and helps isolate the response to the modified primordial power spectrum after MonofonIC conversion. However, the plotted HMF error bars include Poisson counting uncertainties only, while the concentration error bars show halo-to-halo scatter; neither includes cosmic variance or realization-to-realization scatter. This affects the covariance and precision of the quoted ratios, especially for rare high-mass halos, large-scale modes in the matter power spectrum, and statistics measured from smaller auxiliary boxes. For observational interpretation, however, the dominant additional ingredients are baryonic physics, reionization, galaxy selection, and survey response.

Third, the usable range of each statistic is set by different numerical effects. For the low-mass halo mass function, the main limits are particle discreteness and halo-finder thresholds. For the structural diagnostics, the limits are particle number, force softening, centering, and catalog concentration measurements. For the nonlinear matter power spectrum, the limits are box size, mesh assignment, force resolution, and high-$k$ estimator choices. In addition, the matter power-spectrum ratio is quantified only for matched PL and BT products with the same box size.

Finally, the analytic curves used for the halo mass function and concentration--mass relation should be interpreted as controlled reference models rather than exact predictions in the deeply nonlinear regime. They are useful because they are evaluated with the same linear matter power spectra generated from the transfer-function-modified primordial power-spectrum models, but residual differences between the simulations and fitting formulae may reflect both physical nonlinear evolution and the limited calibration range of the analytic prescriptions.

\subsection{Connection to Observations}

The connection to observations is indirect. The results most relevant for observational modeling are the enhanced high-redshift low-mass halo abundance, earlier low-mass assembly, increased low-mass concentrations and inner densities, and enhanced high-\(k\) matter clustering. High-redshift galaxy counts are sensitive to halo abundance and assembly histories. Dwarf-galaxy dynamics and strong lensing probe low-mass structure at low redshift \citep{Dekker25DwarfPPS,Gilman22SubgalacticPPS}. Future 21-cm measurements are sensitive to the timing of early collapse \citep{Munoz20SmallScale21cm,deKruijf25DarkAges,Zhu26SKAPPS}. In each case, the simulation result is an input to the observational model rather than an observational constraint by itself.

Within this scope, the simulations rank the dark-matter-only responses before baryonic physics and survey selection are added. The strongest responses occur in high-redshift low-mass halo abundance, early assembly, low-mass halo concentrations and inner densities, and high-\(k\) matter clustering. These quantities provide benchmark targets for hydrodynamic simulations, semi-analytic galaxy modeling, reionization calculations, and multi-probe likelihood analyses.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We used dark-matter-only cosmological \(N\)-body simulations to quantify how enhanced small-scale primordial power affects nonlinear structure formation. By comparing matched-phase simulations of a standard power-law model with two blue-tilted benchmark models, we isolated the gravitational response of dark matter halos before introducing baryonic physics, galaxy formation, or observational selection.

The main conclusions are as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The halo-abundance response is largest at low mass and high redshift. In the FOF halo mass function (Sec.~\ref{subsub:HMF}; Fig.~\ref{fig:mass_function}), BT\_soft exceeds PL by factors of \(4.81\) and \(6.55\) at \(z=8.52\) in the two mass bins, \(M_{\rm FOF}\simeq1.21\times10^9\) and \(1.21\times10^{10}\,M_\odot\). At \(z=0\), the corresponding ratios are \(1.42\) and \(1.47\). In the same two mass bins, BT\_deep gives ratios of \(1.39\) and \(1.05\) at \(z=8.52\), and \(1.31\) and \(1.08\) at \(z=0\). The \(M_{200c}\) cross-check with spherical-overdensity masses gives the same qualitative conclusion (Fig.~\ref{fig:m200c_bocquet16_hmf}).
    \item Enhanced small-scale primordial power shifts halo assembly earlier. In the raw same-\texttt{TrackId} half-mass diagnostic (Sec.~\ref{subsub:HMR}; Fig.~\ref{fig:half_mass_redshift}), the mean \(z_{1/2}\) near \(M_{z=0}\simeq1.25\times10^9\,M_\odot\) changes from \(2.28\) in PL to \(4.24\) in BT\_soft and \(2.61\) in BT\_deep. The mass-accretion-rate diagnostic (Fig.~\ref{fig:mass_accretion_rate_trackid}) shows the same qualitative picture: BT models, especially BT\_soft, populate low-mass progenitor bins earlier, and their late-time growth is not simply a uniform accretion-rate boost. This is the catalog-level assembly counterpart of the high-redshift abundance enhancement.
    \item The structural diagnostics show the same model ordering. The direct radial density profiles (Sec.~\ref{subsub:DensityProfile}; Fig.~\ref{fig:halo_density_analysis}) and the concentration--mass relation (Sec.~\ref{subsub:concentration}; Fig.~\ref{fig:concentration_comparison}) both show stronger low-mass central density and concentration in the blue-tilted runs. At \(z=0\), the median \(c_{200c}\) in the \(10^9\)--\(10^{9.5}\,M_\odot\) bin is higher than PL by factors of \(3.20\) for BT\_soft and \(1.35\) for BT\_deep.
    \item The nonlinear matter power spectrum shows a coherent but more modest response. In the matched \(25\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\) fiducial comparison (Sec.~\ref{subsub:PS}; Fig.~\ref{fig:power_spectra_ratio_z0}), BT\_soft reaches \(P_{\rm BT}/P_{\rm PL}=1.17\) at \(k=20\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\) and \(1.34\) at \(k=40\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\). BT\_deep is consistent with PL in the same comparison, with ratios of \(1.00\) at both wavenumbers. The direction of the response is consistent with the halo diagnostics, but its amplitude is sensitive to box size and high-\(k\) estimator choices; we therefore do not provide a calibrated nonlinear-power fitting formula.
\end{enumerate}

The analytic comparisons in this paper are controlled reference checks, not new calibrations. The Reed et al. (2007) FOF halo mass-function prescription \citep{Reed_2007}, the Bocquet et al. (2016) \(M_{200c}\) prescription \citep{Bocquet2016}, and the Diemer \& Joyce (2019) and Ishiyama et al. (2021) concentration--mass mappings \citep{Diemer2019,Ishiyama2021} are evaluated with the model-matched PL or BT linear spectra. They help show whether the simulated responses have the expected sign and scale dependence. The projected maps remain qualitative visualizations, and the nonlinear-power curves should not be used as a standalone fitting formula.

These simulations therefore provide dark-matter-only benchmark predictions for models with enhanced small-scale primordial power. The signal is largest at low halo mass, high redshift, and high wavenumber, and weakens toward massive halos and late times. A single matched-seed realization limits covariance-level precision, especially for rare halos and large-scale modes. Connecting these results to observations requires baryonic physics, reionization, galaxy selection, and survey response. Within these limitations, the measurements provide benchmark targets for hydrodynamic simulations, semi-analytic and reionization models, and multi-probe tests of small-scale primordial power.

\section{FOF Mass-Function Resolution and Volume Check}
\label{app:fof_hmf_resolution}

This appendix checks whether the FOF halo-mass-function mass range used in the main result is stable to resolution and volume changes. Figure~\ref{fig:fof_hmf_resolution} shows the numerical check used to assess that range. We recompute the PL FOF mass function at \(z=0\) and \(z=8.52\) for four PL runs. The fiducial run is the \(25\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\), \(1024^3\) simulation. The same-volume lower-resolution checks use \(25\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\) boxes with \(512^3\) and \(256^3\) particles. The volume check uses a \(50\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\), \(512^3\) run. The \(25\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\), \(256^3\) and \(50\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\), \(512^3\) runs have the same particle mass, so their comparison separates a mass-resolution check from a box-volume check at fixed particle mass. The same logarithmic bins, Poisson uncertainties, and Warren et al. FOF low-particle correction used in the main mass-function figure are applied here. Vertical dotted lines mark the 50-particle mass of each plotted resolution, and the lower panels show ratios to the fiducial PL-25-\(1024^3\) measurement. The reduced data are provided in \texttt{public\_data/figure\_data/fof\_hmf\_resolution/}, and the plotting provenance is preserved in \texttt{public\_data/scripts/plot\_fof\_hmf\_resolution.py}.

At \(z=0\), the same-volume checks agree well above each comparison run's 50-particle mass. Over the common bins with at least 20 objects in both the comparison and fiducial catalogs, PL-25-\(512^3\) has a median ratio of \(0.986\) to PL-25-\(1024^3\) over \(10^{8.92}\)--\(10^{12.25}\,M_\odot\). PL-25-\(256^3\) gives \(0.987\) over \(10^{9.92}\)--\(10^{12.25}\,M_\odot\). The corresponding 16th--84th percentile ranges are \(0.963\)--\(1.029\) and \(0.952\)--\(1.070\). The \(50\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\), \(512^3\) volume check gives a median ratio of \(0.945\) over the same \(10^{9.92}\)--\(10^{12.25}\,M_\odot\) mass interval, with a wider range \(0.917\)--\(1.051\). This is consistent with finite-volume and phase differences at the level relevant for the PL reference curve.

At \(z=8.52\), the same-volume \(512^3\) run remains close to the fiducial measurement above its 50-particle mass. Its median ratio is \(0.935\) over \(10^{8.92}\)--\(10^{10.58}\,M_\odot\), with a 16th--84th percentile range \(0.900\)--\(0.979\). The \(256^3\) same-volume run and the \(50\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\), \(512^3\) volume check cover only five common high-mass bins above their \(6.0\times10^9\,M_\odot\) 50-particle scale. Their median ratios are \(0.813\) and \(0.871\), respectively. We therefore treat this test as a numerical reliability bracket, not as an additional cosmological result. The fiducial \(1024^3\) HMF has substantial resolution margin above \(10^8\,M_\odot\). Lower-resolution comparison boxes show the expected loss of high-redshift low-mass abundance when their own 50-particle limits move into the plotted mass range.

\section{Temporary FOF Group Reassignment in Half-Mass Histories}
\label{app:fof_gap_stitching}

This appendix explains a catalog-level ambiguity in the half-mass assembly diagnostic. Figure~\ref{fig:half_mass_redshift} uses FOF masses along \textsc{HBT-HERONS} tracks. This is useful for comparing PL, BT\_soft, and BT\_deep with a common history construction, but it also exposes a catalog-level ambiguity. Some objects keep the same \textsc{HBT-HERONS} \texttt{TrackId} and remain self-bound, yet for a small number of snapshots they are no longer listed as an independent FOF host. In the \textsc{SOAP}/\textsc{HBT-HERONS} catalog this appears as \texttt{InputHalos/FOF/Masses}=0 and \texttt{InputHalos/FOF/Sizes}=0 in the object's own row, while \texttt{InputHalos/IsCentral}=0 and \texttt{InputHalos/HBTplus/HostFOFId} points to a larger host group. The object has therefore not disappeared; its mass has been assigned to the host FOF group for that snapshot, while the subhalo still has non-zero \texttt{BoundSubhalo/TotalMass}.

The resulting gap is therefore a gap in the FOF-host mass history, not evidence that the tracked object has zero physical mass. During such an episode, the receiving FOF host can also acquire a transient mass excess and later lose it when the FOF groups split again, so individual FOF masses are not guaranteed to be monotonic. The \texttt{BoundSubhalo/TotalMass} history is more continuous in this situation because it follows the self-bound object identity rather than the instantaneous FOF group assignment. This behavior is closer to the intuitive picture in which two self-bound haloes have not fully merged but are temporarily linked by the FOF algorithm or exchange outer material. However, \texttt{BoundSubhalo/TotalMass} is not the same mass definition as the FOF or spherical-overdensity host-halo masses used in the BK09 and COCO/Hellwing16 reference relations. We therefore use it only to diagnose object survival and catalog assignment. We do not use it as a direct replacement for the reference halo mass.

This behavior is important for FOF-based mass-accretion histories. If such a snapshot is treated as permanent termination of the progenitor branch, the history is truncated even though the same tracked object may appear again as an independent FOF halo in adjacent snapshots. Conversely, reconnecting across arbitrarily long gaps may be too permissive. To quantify the size of this effect, we performed a PL-only FOF gap-stitching test. The procedure is:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Select the same \(z=0\) FOF halo sample used in the PL half-mass analysis, giving \(344271\) halos.
    \item Follow the same \textsc{HBT-HERONS} \texttt{TrackId} over snapshots 0021--0056 and record Warren-corrected \texttt{InputHalos/FOF/Masses}.
    \item Mark a snapshot as a valid independent FOF-host point only if the same \texttt{TrackId} has positive FOF mass, positive FOF size, and is central. Snapshots where the same \texttt{TrackId} is present but has \texttt{FOF/Masses}=\texttt{FOF/Sizes}=0 are treated as temporary non-FOF assignments.
    \item Recompute \(z_{1/2}\) while allowing at most \(N_{\rm gap}\) consecutive non-FOF snapshots to be crossed before reconnecting to earlier valid FOF-host points. We test \(N_{\rm gap}=0,1,2,3,5\), and the limiting case in which all gaps are allowed.
\end{enumerate}

The strict case \(N_{\rm gap}=0\) is close to a FOF-host-only main-branch definition. It gives \(262310/344271\) measurable halos and a PL--BK09 by-bin mean absolute offset of \(0.2363\) in \(z_{1/2}\). Allowing short gaps monotonically restores measurable histories and moves the PL relation toward the BK09 reference. For \(N_{\rm gap}=1,2,3,5\), the measurable counts are \(292027\), \(314254\), \(323441\), and \(327635\). The corresponding BK09 offsets are \(0.1850\), \(0.1206\), \(0.0871\), and \(0.0664\). Allowing all gaps gives \(329621\) measurable halos and an offset of \(0.0548\).

As a separate check on the failure mode, among the PL halos that are measurable by a same-\texttt{TrackId} FOF history but not by the strict corrected descendant-rule branch, \(67077/67328\) (\(99.63\%\)) stop at a snapshot where a same-\texttt{TrackId} candidate exists but has \texttt{FOF/Sizes}=0. This confirms that the dominant issue is temporary FOF group reassignment rather than loss of the \textsc{HBT-HERONS} track itself. We therefore use the raw same-\texttt{TrackId} assembly curve in Fig.~\ref{fig:half_mass_redshift} as a direct no-envelope catalog-history diagnostic. The gap-stitching curves in Fig.~\ref{fig:fof_gap_stitching} bracket the associated systematic. These diagnostics should not be read as a claim that the \textsc{HBT-HERONS} bound-subhalo mass is the same quantity as the FOF main-progenitor mass used in the BK09 and COCO/Hellwing16 reference relations. \citep{BoylanKolchin2009MSII,Hellwing16cocoproject}

\section{Power-Spectrum Box-Size Reliability}
\label{app:power_spectrum_reliability}

This appendix records the box-size reliability check used to interpret the power-spectrum ratios. Figure~\ref{fig:power_spectrum_validation_theory} provides the simulation/theory validation view used to judge the wavenumber ranges of the different power-spectrum boxes. The measured spectra are derived from the cached \texttt{.npz} products in \texttt{powerspectrum/sim\_power\_data/}, each of which stores the arrays \texttt{k}, \texttt{P}, and \texttt{z}. These caches were generated from the SWIFT snapshots with \texttt{compute\_sim\_power\_spectrum.py}. The script deposits dark-matter particles on a \(1024^3\) mesh and computes the isotropically averaged power spectrum with the \textsc{swiftsimio} power-spectrum utilities. It then converts the output to \(k\) in \(h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\) and \(P(k)\) in \(({\rm Mpc}/h)^3\). The reduced public-data package contains the corresponding tabulated power-spectrum measurements in \texttt{public\_data/figure\_data/nonlinear\_power\_spectrum/}, together with provenance copies of \texttt{compute\_sim\_power\_spectrum.py} and \texttt{plot\_power\_spectrum\_halofit.py}.

The validation plot is intentionally different from the main BT/PL response plot in Fig.~\ref{fig:power_spectra_ratio_z0}. The upper panels compare absolute measured spectra with reference nonlinear curves. The solid black curve is the PL reference. The dashed blue curve is the BT\_soft reference. For the BT\_soft reference, we apply the \(k_p=1\,h\,{\rm Mpc}^{-1}\), \(m_s=1.5\) broken-power-law modification to the linear spectrum and evolve it with the same HMcode2020 setup described in Sec.~\ref{subsub:PS}. The lower panels show simulation/theory ratios. PL boxes are divided by the PL theory curve, and BT\_soft boxes are divided by the BT\_soft theory curve. We use these ratios to identify where neighboring box sizes agree and where finite-volume or mesh effects become important.

The useful range is not set by a single hard cut. Large boxes anchor the low-\(k\) and intermediate-\(k\) regime because they contain more long-wavelength modes, whereas the \(5\) and \(2.5\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\) boxes are used only as high-\(k\) extensions. At the high-\(k\) end, the vertical markers show the mesh Nyquist scales, \(k_{\rm Ny}=\pi N_{\rm mesh}/L_{\rm box}\), for \(N_{\rm mesh}=1024\). Points approaching \(k_{\rm Ny}\), and especially points beyond the conservative \(0.5k_{\rm Ny}\) caution scale, are interpreted as diagnostic rather than precision measurements. The gray high-\(k\) region in Fig.~\ref{fig:power_spectrum_validation_theory} marks this caution regime for the fiducial \(25\,h^{-1}{\rm Mpc}\) box.
